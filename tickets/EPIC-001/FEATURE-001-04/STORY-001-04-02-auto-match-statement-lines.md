## User Story

**As a** Treasury Analyst

**I want** every imported statement line of a named company's **Bank** journal scored against the open journal items it could settle — on amount, reference, partner, date proximity and transaction type — with reusable reconciliation rules carrying their own precedence and their own write-off postings, so that a line I have seen a hundred times before is claimed by a rule and a line I have never seen arrives as a ranked, scored suggestion I confirm or decline

**So that** 950 of the 1,000 lines imported for `US-01` over 2025-03-01 to 2025-03-31 are resolved without a line-by-line lookup — a matching rate of 95.0%, at the SM-002 floor of 95% or higher — each of them either claimed outright by a rule whose `trigger` is `auto_reconcile` or carrying a suggestion at 95% or higher that I release in one batch decision instead of 950 separate ones, leaving me the 50 exceptions rather than all 1,000 lookups, with each accepted match posting a balanced entry whose total debits equal its total credits at a difference of `0.00 USD`, and with the reason a line was claimed recorded against it so the External Auditor tests the control instead of re-performing the reconciliation by hand

## Acceptance Criteria

The rules are the configurable half of this one capability, not a separate capability: a rule hit is a signal that feeds the same 0-to-100% score, a rule with an automatic trigger consumes a line the scoring pass would otherwise have offered as a suggestion, and both paths end in the same balanced posting through the same **Bank** journal. All 8 criteria below are written against one deterministic artifact set, so every assertion has exactly one pass-or-fail reading. The set is fixed by the parent feature and is not restated per criterion:

**Coverage distribution.** Criteria 1, 3, 4, 5 and 8 are the valid-input path — an exact single-candidate match, a ranked multi-candidate result, an automatic rule application with its write-off, a rule preview that commits nothing, and an accepted suggestion posting its balanced clearing entry. Criterion 2 is the partial-signal path, where the amount and partner signals hit and the reference signal does not. Criterion 6 is the invalid and incomplete input case. Criteria 6 and 7 are the error-handling cases. Criterion 8 also carries the accounting edge case: the statement line no candidate reaches stays unreconciled, is presented as "No Match Found" and remains visible on the **Bank Reconciliation Statement** rather than being absorbed into a balancing figure. Criterion 6 additionally discharges the two expression tests C-022 requires of this story. The foreign-currency settlement with its rate basis and its exchange difference is not this story's: [STORY-001-04-03](./STORY-001-04-03-manual-reconciliation.md) Criterion 7 owns it, and what remains here is the conversion of a rule write-off denominated in another currency, which Edge Case 3 owns.

### Scenario 1: An exact amount and reference match returns exactly one high-band suggestion and commits nothing

- **Given** the imported statement line of company `US-01` dated 2024-02-01, bank reference `REF-0201`, narration "Invoice Payment INV/2024/0201", amount `+$1,250.00 USD` on the `US-01` **Bank** journal, carrying the provisional posting `STORY-001-04-01` created against Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099; and one open customer invoice `INV/2024/0201` of `US-01` on Accounts Receivable 1200 with a residual of `$1,250.00 USD` and a transaction date within 30 days of the statement line, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01; and the fiscal period containing 2024-02 open for `US-01` with no journal-entry lock date on or after 2024-02-01
- **When** the matching engine evaluates that statement line against the open journal items of `US-01`
- **Then** exactly 1 suggestion is returned, it identifies the open invoice `INV/2024/0201` for `$1,250.00 USD`, and it falls in the high-confidence band at a score of 95% or higher because the amount signal, the reference signal and the partner signal all hit, with each contributing signal's score readable alongside the total; and the evaluation itself commits nothing — the count of reconciliations committed is 0, the count of journal entries created is 0, the statement line's reconciled state stays false and the Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 line of its provisional posting still carries `$1,250.00 USD` — because scoring a line is not reconciling it. Accepting this suggestion is a separate trigger and Criterion 8 owns it, together with the balanced clearing entry that acceptance posts. Each amount above is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01

### Scenario 2: A partner and amount correlation without a reference hit scores in the medium band and waits for confirmation

- **Given** the imported statement line of company `US-01` dated 2024-02-15, bank reference `REF-0215`, narration "Customer Payment INV/2024/0215", counterparty name "Beta Industries Ltd", amount `+$2,100.00 USD` on the `US-01` **Bank** journal; and exactly one open customer invoice of `US-01` for the partner Beta Industries Ltd on Accounts Receivable 1200 with a residual of `$2,100.00 USD`, numbered `INV/2024/0231` and carrying no value in its `ref` field, so that neither its number nor its `ref` appears in the statement line's narration or in its bank reference `REF-0215`; and the token `INV/2024/0215` present in that narration resolves to no open journal item of `US-01`, the invoice it names having been settled and reconciled in a prior period — each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the matching engine evaluates that statement line against the open journal items of `US-01`
- **Then** exactly 1 suggestion is returned, it identifies the open invoice `INV/2024/0231` for `$2,100.00 USD`, and it falls in the medium-confidence band at a score of 75% to 94% because the amount signal and the partner signal hit while the reference signal scores 0 against both the candidate's number and its empty `ref`; and the statement line's reconciled state remains false with 0 journal entries created, so the suggestion is held for the Treasury Analyst's confirmation and the score of each contributing signal is readable alongside it, no line below the high-confidence band being reconciled without a decision recorded against it, with each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01 on every amount above

### Scenario 3: Two candidates of equal amount are ranked with a tie-break that repeats across runs

- **Given** the imported statement line of company `US-01` dated 2024-02-05, bank reference `CNS-0205`, narration "Consulting Fee Received", counterparty name "Global Consulting Inc", amount `+$3,500.00 USD` on the `US-01` **Bank** journal; and exactly two open customer invoices of `US-01` for the partner Global Consulting Inc on Accounts Receivable 1200, each with a residual of `$3,500.00 USD`, the first numbered `INV/2024/0188` and due 2024-02-10 and the second numbered `INV/2024/0194` and due 2024-02-25, neither number appearing in the statement line's narration — each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the matching engine evaluates that statement line against the open journal items of `US-01`
- **Then** exactly 2 candidates are returned, ranked by the weighted score computed over the five signals — amount, reference, partner, date proximity and transaction type — with each candidate's total score and each contributing signal's score readable on the suggestion; and where two candidates carry an equal total score the ranking is resolved first by the earlier invoice due date, placing `INV/2024/0188` due 2024-02-10 above `INV/2024/0194` due 2024-02-25, and then by the lower record identifier where the due dates are also equal, so that two evaluations of the same statement line against the same candidate set return the two candidates in the same order; and the statement line's reconciled state remains false with 0 journal entries created until one candidate is accepted

### Scenario 4: A reusable rule with an automatic trigger claims the service-fee line and posts a balanced write-off

- **Given** the reconciliation rule **Bank Service Fees** at `sequence` 10, bound by `match_journal_ids` to the `US-01` **Bank** journal alone, with `match_label` = `contains` and `match_label_param` = `SERVICE FEE`, `match_amount` = `between` with `match_amount_min` `−$100.00 USD` and `match_amount_max` `$0.00 USD`, `trigger` = `auto_reconcile`, and one `account.reconcile.model.line` whose `account_id` is Bank Charges 6800 and which absorbs the full line balance — expressed as `amount_type` = `percentage` with `amount_string` `100`, for which `amount_type` = `fixed` with `amount_string` `35.00` is the equivalent for a rule bound to one known fee amount, both producing the same `$35.00 USD` posting; and the imported statement line of `US-01` dated 2024-02-08, bank reference `FEE-0208`, narration "Monthly Bank Service Fee", amount `−$35.00 USD`, which lies inside that amount window and whose narration contains the label parameter; and the fiscal period containing 2024-02 open for `US-01`, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the matching engine processes that statement line through the active rules of the `US-01` **Bank** journal
- **Then** rule 10 claims the line and is applied without a confirmation step, no rule of a higher `sequence` value being evaluated against it; the resulting entry posts through the `US-01` **Bank** journal as debit Bank Charges 6800 `$35.00 USD` against credit Bank 1010 `$35.00 USD`, so total debits of `$35.00 USD` equal total credits of `$35.00 USD` at a difference of `0.00 USD`, with each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01; the Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 line of the provisional posting is cleared to `$0.00 USD`; the statement line's reconciled state becomes true and its residual becomes `$0.00 USD`; and the identity of rule 10 is recorded against the line together with the timestamp, so the External Auditor reads which rule claimed it rather than inferring it — and no rule bound to the `NL-01` or `GB-01` **Bank** journal is evaluated against a line of the `US-01` **Bank** journal

### Scenario 5: A rule is previewed against historical lines and the preview commits nothing

- **Given** the reconciliation rule **Invoice Reference** at `sequence` 30, bound by `match_journal_ids` to the `US-01` **Bank** journal, with `match_label` = `match_regex` and `match_label_param` = `(INV(OICE)?-\d+)` and `trigger` = `manual`, saved but not yet active; and the reconciliation rule **Customer Remittance** at `sequence` 40, bound to the same journal, with `match_label` = `contains` and `match_label_param` = `PAYMENT` and `trigger` = `manual`, whose conditions cover the same lines; and 6 unreconciled statement lines on the `US-01` **Bank** journal for 2025-03-01 to 2025-03-31 of which exactly 4 carry a narration matching that expression — "Remittance INV-10231", "PAYMENT INVOICE-10244", "Customer PAYMENT INV-10255" and "INVOICE-10261 settlement" — and 2 do not, being "PAYROLL RUN 2025-03" and "INTERNAL TRANSFER 884120"
- **When** the Treasury Analyst previews rule 30 against the existing unreconciled statement lines of the `US-01` **Bank** journal
- **Then** the preview lists exactly those 4 lines and neither of the other 2, naming rule 30 as the rule that would claim each of them and stating that rule 40 would be reached only for a line rule 30 leaves; the count of reconciliations committed by the preview is 0, the count of journal entries created is 0 and the count of rules activated by the preview is 0, so all 6 lines carry a reconciled state of false after it and rule 30 remains saved and inactive, a preview being a read of what a rule would do rather than a run of it. Activating both rules and observing rule 30 claim those 4 lines ahead of rule 40 in live evaluation is a separate trigger, as is the 1-second-per-rule evaluation budget measured on that live run. The expression `(INV(OICE)?-\d+)` requires the literal `INV` or `INVOICE` followed by a hyphen and one or more digits, so it matches none of the 8 February fixture lines, whose invoice references are written in the slash form `INV/2024/0201`; the preview corpus is therefore drawn from the worked March 2025 population rather than from the fixture

### Scenario 6: An invalid or incomplete rule definition is refused at save time and no statement line is touched

- **Given** the Treasury Analyst is defining a reconciliation rule on the `US-01` **Bank** journal, which holds 8 unreconciled statement lines and a Bank 1010 balance carried from `STORY-001-04-01`, and the count of stored `account.reconcile.model` records for that journal before the attempt is recorded
- **When** the Treasury Analyst saves that rule definition carrying one of three defects — a `match_label` of `match_regex` whose `match_label_param` is the unbalanced expression `(INV-\d+`; or a `match_amount` of `between` whose `match_amount_min` is `$500.00 USD` and whose `match_amount_max` is `$100.00 USD`, the minimum standing above the maximum; or a `match_label_param` of `(a+)+$`, an expression whose nested quantifiers make its evaluation cost grow non-linearly with the length of the label it is applied to — with each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01 on both bounds
- **Then** each of the three is refused at save time with a validation message naming the offending field and the reason it failed — the unbalanced expression named as an expression that does not compile, the inverted range named as a minimum above its maximum, and the nested-quantifier expression named as an expression exceeding the configured complexity bound; the count of stored `account.reconcile.model` records for the `US-01` **Bank** journal is unchanged from the count recorded before the attempt, so no partial rule survives; the count of statement lines whose reconciled state changed is 0 and the count of journal entries created is 0, leaving the Bank 1010 and Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 balances of `US-01` unchanged at a difference of `0.00 USD`, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01; each message names the field and the remedy and discloses no stack trace, no SQL, no file-system path and no credential; and the rule-evaluation path additionally bounds the input length an expression is applied to and the elapsed time of one evaluation, so that a stored expression reaching evaluation cannot hold a matching run open beyond the 1-second-per-rule budget and is abandoned with a named error against its own line while the rest of the run completes

### Scenario 7: A batch accept holding one stale suggestion rolls back whole and commits nothing

- **Given** 50 statement lines of the `US-01` **Bank** journal, every one of them a `US-01` line denominated in USD and drawn from the 950 lines of 2025-03-01 to 2025-03-31 that carry a high-confidence suggestion at a score of 95% or higher, of which one line's target open invoice has been settled by another posting between the moment its suggestion was scored and the moment the batch is submitted, so that invoice's residual is now `$0.00 USD`; and the fiscal period containing 2025-03 open for `US-01` with no journal-entry lock date on or after 2025-03-01 — each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the Treasury Analyst accepts that batch of 50 suggestions as one operation
- **Then** the batch is refused as one unit: the count of reconciliations committed is 0, the count of journal entries created is 0, all 50 statement lines carry a reconciled state of false, their 50 provisional postings still stand in Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 unchanged at a difference of `0.00 USD`, and the message names the conflicting statement line together with the invoice whose residual is now `$0.00 USD` and the check that failed, so that no subset of the batch is applied while one member of it is stale and no partial commit survives. Re-submitting the batch once the stale suggestion has been rescored is a separate trigger. Each amount above is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01

### Scenario 8: An accepted high-band suggestion posts a balanced clearing entry and the line no candidate reaches stays visible as No Match Found

- **Given** the single high-confidence suggestion of Criterion 1 standing against the `REF-0201` statement line of `+$1,250.00 USD` on the `US-01` **Bank** journal; and 6 of the other 7 February statement lines of that journal already reconciled — the `FEE-0208` service fee claimed by rule 10 as Criterion 4 asserts, and `PUR-0203`, `CNS-0205`, `UTL-0212`, `REF-0215` and `VND-0220` by suggestions already accepted — so the only unreconciled lines are `REF-0201` and the 2024-02-28 line `INT-0228` "Interest Earned" of `+$12.50 USD`, which no rule claims and for which the engine returns no candidate reaching the low ceiling of 75%; and the fiscal period containing 2024-02 open for `US-01` with no journal-entry lock date on or after 2024-02-01, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the Treasury Analyst accepts that suggestion for the `REF-0201` line
- **Then** the reconciliation entry posts through the `US-01` **Bank** journal as debit Bank 1010 `$1,250.00 USD` against credit Accounts Receivable 1200 `$1,250.00 USD`, so total debits of `$1,250.00 USD` equal total credits of `$1,250.00 USD` at a difference of `0.00 USD`; the Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 line of the provisional posting is cleared to `$0.00 USD`; the residual of invoice `INV/2024/0201` becomes `$0.00 USD`; the statement line's reconciled state becomes true and both records carry the same matching reference; and the accepting role together with the timestamp and the accepted score is recorded against the line, so the External Auditor reads who accepted the match rather than inferring it. `INT-0228` is then the single unreconciled line of that journal: it is presented with the status "No Match Found" and a reconciled state of false rather than being offered as a suggestion or absorbed into a balancing figure, so the **Bank Reconciliation Statement** run for the `US-01` **Bank** journal with the date-range parameter 2024-02-01 to 2024-02-29 presents an unreconciled statement lines total of `$12.50 USD` that equals the Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 balance of `$12.50 USD` behind it at a difference of `0.00 USD`, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01

## Edge Cases

| # | Edge Case | Expected Handling |
|---|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Two active rules of the `US-01` **Bank** journal have conditions that both hold for the same statement line — rule 30 **Invoice Reference** and rule 40 **Customer Remittance** both cover the narration "Customer PAYMENT INV-10255" | `sequence` decides: evaluation runs in ascending `sequence` and the first rule whose conditions are met claims the line, so rule 30 claims it and rule 40 is not applied to it; the identity of the claiming rule is recorded on the line together with the timestamp, so a reader is never left to infer which of two candidate rules acted. Where a line is claimed by no rule it falls through to the scoring pass rather than being skipped, and the count of lines claimed by more than one rule is 0 |
| 2 | A stored expression is hostile or its evaluation cost grows non-linearly with the length of the label — the unbalanced `(INV-\d+`, which does not compile, or the nested-quantifier `(a+)+$`, which compiles cleanly and therefore passes a compilation check | The two defects are refused by two different checks and neither is assumed to cover the other: the non-compiling expression is refused at save by expression compilation with a message naming the field, while the nested-quantifier expression is refused at save by an explicit complexity bound because compilation alone accepts it. Any expression that nonetheless reaches evaluation is applied under a stored length bound and an elapsed-time bound, so one evaluation cannot exceed the 1-second-per-rule budget; an abandoned evaluation raises a named error against its own statement line and the remainder of the run completes, and in every case the count of statement lines changed is 0 and the count of journal entries created is 0 |
| 3 | A rule write-off is denominated in a currency other than the journal currency — a rule on the `NL-01` **Bank** journal, whose functional currency is EUR, whose write-off line is expressed in USD | The write-off is converted at the `res.currency.rate` effective on the statement line's own transaction date, applied as units of the foreign currency per unit of the company's functional currency — 1.0850 USD per EUR on 2025-03-20 — and the applied rate together with its rate date is stored on the reconciliation so the conversion is reproducible. The residual arising between the converted write-off and the amount the ledger holds is posted to FX Gain/Loss on Settlement 7100, so the entry carries total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `0.00 EUR`; each amount, converted or original, is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at its currency's `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01, and the original amount is shown with its ISO 4217 code alongside the company-currency amount |
| 4 | A candidate invoice is settled by another posting between the moment a suggestion is scored and the moment the Treasury Analyst accepts it, so its residual is `$0.00 USD` at acceptance | The batch rolls back whole: the count of reconciliations committed is 0, the count of journal entries created is 0, and every line in the batch keeps a reconciled state of false, so no member of a batch is applied while another member is stale. The message names the conflicting statement line and the invoice whose residual changed, the stale suggestion is rescored against the current residual, and the batch is submitted again as a fresh operation. Partial application is not offered, because a half-applied batch leaves the Treasury Analyst unable to tell which lines still need a decision. Every amount named in this row is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at its currency's `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01 |
| 5 | A statement line's transaction date falls inside a period closed by a journal-entry lock date — a line dated 2024-02-15 on the `US-01` **Bank** journal with a journal-entry lock date of 2024-02-29 in force for `US-01` | Automatic reconciliation of that line is stopped by **the matching run's own pre-posting guard**, the guard the Epic's [lock-date behaviour contract](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#78-lock-date-behaviour-contract) governs as **L-11**: it reads the lock dates **before the clearing entry is constructed**, so no `account.move` exists for the attempt, no journal sequence number is consumed, and the message asserted is **the guard's own text** naming the affected company `US-01`, the violated journal-entry lock date 2024-02-29 and the remedial action — not the platform text of L-2, which applies only to a record that reached the platform. The guard exists because the platform would otherwise apply **L-1** and post the entry re-dated into the first open period, and a clearing entry re-dated out of its statement period breaks the statement tie-out; whether the write-off leg or the clearing leg triggers it makes no difference, because the guard runs ahead of both. The line keeps a reconciled state of false and stays visible on the **Bank Reconciliation Statement** for the same date range with its date, amount, label and age rather than being netted into a balancing figure; the count of journal entries created is 0, the count of journal items dated inside the locked period is 0, and the Bank 1010 and Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 balances of `US-01` are unchanged at a difference of `0.00 USD`, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01; the run itself continues, so a locked line does not stop the remaining lines of the batch from being matched, and the affected lines are reported by count. The lock date itself is administered by [`STORY-001-01-05`](../FEATURE-001-01/STORY-001-01-05-configure-period-lock-dates.md), and the sanctioned route to reconciling inside the closed period stays the recorded lock exception of **L-10** |

### Matching Signal Weights

Five signals combine into a single score expressed from 0% to 100%. The weighting is **configuration to be confirmed with the Finance SME per company and per journal**, not a constant fixed by this story: Sub-Task 1 agrees it, and the starting point is the weighting the present Community implementation declares — amount 0.35, reference 0.25, partner 0.25 and date proximity 0.15, summing to 1.0. Transaction type is the fifth signal this story adds, so the set is re-normalized to 1.0 once it is included rather than appended to a weighting that already totals 1.0.

| Signal | Relative Weight | How it is scored |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|
| Amount | High — the strongest signal | An exact equality between the statement line amount and the candidate's open residual scores the signal at 100%. Inside a tolerance window of `$0.50 USD` or 0.5% of the statement line amount, whichever is the greater, each rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01, the signal scores on a stated taper from 100% down to 0% across the window; outside the window it scores 0%. The window is quantified as a currency amount and a percentage and is never expressed as nearness. The wider 5% window the present implementation applies to one-to-many combination search is a different window for a different search, and Sub-Task 2 records which governs which |
| Reference | High | The statement line's payment reference and narration are compared against the candidate journal item's reference and its name, by substring containment and, where a rule supplies one, by expression. An exact containment of the candidate's own number scores the signal at 100%; a partial token overlap scores on a stated taper; no overlap scores 0%, which is the case Criterion 2 asserts |
| Partner | Medium-high | An exact link between the statement line's partner and the candidate's partner outranks a similarity score computed on the statement line's counterparty name, so a resolved partner is never displaced by a name that merely resembles one. An unresolved counterparty scores the signal at 0% rather than excluding the candidate, so the remaining signals still produce a score |
| Date proximity | Medium | The interval between the statement line's transaction date and the candidate's date, and where the candidate carries one its due date, scores higher the shorter it is, tapering to 0% at the edge of the configured candidate date window. This signal carries the tie-break of Criterion 3, where the earlier due date ranks first |
| Transaction type | Low-to-medium | Agreement between the statement line's transaction type — a receipt, a payment, a transfer, a fee — and the category of the candidate, so a receipt is not ranked against a vendor payment on amount alone. This is the signal absent from the present implementation's declared weighting, which is why adding it re-normalizes the set |

A rule hit is not a sixth signal with its own weight: a rule whose `trigger` is `manual` raises the score of the candidate it points at, and a rule whose `trigger` is `auto_reconcile` claims the line outright, so the rules are the configurable half of the same capability rather than a parallel one.

### Confidence Bands

| Band | Score | Treatment |
|------|-------|-----------|
| High | **95% or higher** | Eligible for batch accept and for a rule whose `trigger` is `auto_reconcile`. This is the floor behind the 950-of-1,000 rate asserted against SM-002 |
| Medium | **75% to 94%** | Surfaced to the Treasury Analyst for confirmation with each contributing signal's score readable alongside the total. Never applied without a decision recorded against it |
| Low | **below 75%** | Not surfaced as a suggestion. The line is presented with the status "No Match Found", keeps a reconciled state of false, and stays visible on the **Bank Reconciliation Statement** with its date, amount, label and age |

**One band definition governs — settled.** The table above is the one band set in force for the feature: high 95% or higher, medium 75% to 94%, low below 75%. The parent feature's artifact table now states the same three bands and links to this section, so the earlier four-tier draft of High 90% to 100%, Medium 70% to 89%, Low 50% to 69% and no suggestion below 50% is superseded and is not written anywhere in this backlog. What remains is a code alignment rather than an undecided contract: the implementation already present declares `CONFIDENCE_HIGH = 95.0`, `CONFIDENCE_MEDIUM = 70.0` and `CONFIDENCE_LOW = 50.0` in `addons/account_bank_reconciliation_ce/models/reconciliation_matching_engine.py`, which agrees on the high floor of 95% and differs below it, so the medium floor moves from 70.0 to 75.0 and the separate low floor of 50.0 is retired — a three-band model carries two boundaries and the low band is everything under the medium floor. Sub-Task 3 records the Finance SME's confirmation of those two boundaries, and every criterion in this story and every threshold in the parent is written against them.

### Reconciliation Rule Field Reference

These fields exist today on `account.reconcile.model` and `account.reconcile.model.line`, so the implementing agent extends rather than invents. The story asserts outcomes against them and prescribes no new field.

| Model | Field | Purpose |
|-------|-------|---------|
| `account.reconcile.model` | `name` | The rule's name, read back on a claimed line so the External Auditor sees which rule acted — **Bank Service Fees**, **Payroll**, **Invoice Reference**, **Customer Remittance** |
| `account.reconcile.model` | `sequence` | Evaluation order, a lower value evaluated first, defaulting to 10. Rules 10, 20, 30 and 40 are ordered by it, and the first rule whose conditions hold claims the line |
| `account.reconcile.model` | `trigger` | `manual` or `auto_reconcile`. An automatic trigger posts without a confirmation step, which is why Criterion 4 asserts the posting and Criterion 6 asserts that a defective rule never reaches this state |
| `account.reconcile.model` | `match_journal_ids` | The journals a rule is available from, restricted to journals of type bank, cash and credit. This is what binds a rule to one company's **Bank** journal and is why every rule criterion names its company |
| `account.reconcile.model` | `match_amount` with `match_amount_min` and `match_amount_max` | `lower`, `greater` or `between`. Rule 10 uses `between` with `−$100.00 USD` and `$0.00 USD`; Criterion 6 asserts that a minimum standing above its maximum is refused, a validation to be added if discovery finds none present, with each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01 on both bounds |
| `account.reconcile.model` | `match_label` with `match_label_param` | `contains`, `not_contains` or `match_regex`, tested against the statement line label, the transaction details or the note. Rules 10, 20 and 40 use `contains`; rule 30 uses `match_regex` with `(INV(OICE)?-\d+)` |
| `account.reconcile.model` | `match_partner_ids` | The partners a rule is limited to, so a rule can be narrowed to one acquirer or one payroll provider without widening its label condition |
| `account.reconcile.model` | `line_ids` | The write-off and counterpart line configurations a rule carries |
| `account.reconcile.model.line` | `account_id` | The account a write-off reaches — Bank Charges 6800 for rule 10 |
| `account.reconcile.model.line` | `amount_type` with `amount_string` | `fixed`, `percentage`, `percentage_st_line` or `regex`, defaulting to `percentage` with an amount string of `100`. A 100% write-off of the balance and a fixed write-off of `35.00` both produce the same `$35.00 USD` posting for rule 10, with each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD `res.currency` rounding increment of 0.01 |
| `account.reconcile.model.line` | `tax_ids` | The taxes a write-off line carries. Where one is present the posting separates its base amount from its tax amount, which is why the accounting-reconciliation gate below carries a tax clause rather than a formality |
| `account.reconcile.model.line` | `partner_id` and `label` | The counterparty and the narration a generated write-off line carries, so a posting created by a rule is as readable as one entered by hand |

**Expression validation already present, and what it does not cover.** A constraint on the rule object compiles `match_label_param` with Python's `re.compile()` when `match_label` is `match_regex` and refuses the record with a validation error when compilation fails, and a comparable check exists for an amount string of type `regex`. That constraint refuses the unbalanced `(INV-\d+` of Criterion 6. It does **not** refuse `(a+)+$`, which compiles cleanly and whose evaluation cost grows non-linearly with the length of the label it is applied to. Two additions are therefore required and neither substitutes for the other: a stored complexity and length bound applied before an expression is accepted, and an elapsed-time bound applied when an expression is evaluated, so a stored expression cannot hold a matching run open beyond the 1-second-per-rule budget (C-019, C-022).

**Signal fields read from the statement line and the candidate.** On `account.bank.statement.line`: the payment reference held as the line's label, the signed amount, the partner link, the counterparty name, the transaction type, the transaction detail payload, the reconciled state and the residual. On `account.move.line`: the reference, the name, the partner, the amount in currency, the date and where present the due date, the open residual and the reconciled state.

**Rationale.** The 8 points sit on four things that cannot be delivered separately and still be demonstrated: a weighted multi-signal scoring pass whose result is explainable and repeatable, a configurable rule engine with precedence and expression safety, an atomic batch accept that survives a concurrent settlement, and two quantified performance budgets that must hold at 1,000 lines and 100,000 open items. Each is a hardening of behaviour `account_bank_reconciliation_ce` 19.0.1.0.0 already carries rather than a green-field build, which is what separates 8 from 13.

**Why the scoring pass and the rules are one story at 8 points.** A rule hit feeds the same 0-to-100% score, a rule with an automatic trigger consumes a line the scoring pass would otherwise have suggested, and both paths end in the same balanced posting through the same **Bank** journal — so the rules are the configurable half of this one workflow rather than a second workflow, and dividing them would leave the rule half with no score to feed and the scoring half unable to demonstrate an automatic reconciliation. The scope that pushed the earlier draft of this story to the ceiling of the range has been moved to the story that already owns it: the settlement of a foreign-currency open item, with its rate selection, its three currency amounts and its exchange difference, is `STORY-001-04-03` Criterion 7. If refinement still finds the remaining scope above 8 points, the parent feature records that a scope change is raised against its four-story budget rather than absorbed silently or trimmed.

The superseded flat-layout backlog sized the two retired stories as "XL (Extra Large)" and "L (Large)" on a T-shirt scale. Those estimates are restated here as one Fibonacci value of **8** on the scale the Epic mandates, with Effort, Complexity and Uncertainty rated separately above.

### Governance of This Story

### Migration From the Superseded Backlog

**Migration line.** `STORY-001-04-02` supersedes and replaces **two** retired flat-layout stories, which the Epic's retirement map records as merging here: `BR-002 Algorithmic Matching` and `BR-004 Reconciliation Rules`, whose retired identifiers stay reserved for the Epic's retirement-map traceability and are stated in this one line only — `STORY-001-04-02` is this story's sole identifier everywhere else.

The merge is deliberately **not** reversible into two stories inside this backlog: the rule half is not planned as a separate future story, because a rule with no score to feed and no line to claim has nothing to demonstrate.

### Business Context

This is the story that changes the shape of the work. Matching by hand costs one lookup per line, so a 1,000-line month costs 1,000 lookups and nothing learned in one month carries into the next. Scoring every line against its candidates and letting rules claim the patterns that recur turns that into 50 decisions, and the 950 it resolves without a human are the reason period close in FEATURE-001-07 can begin on day one of the following period rather than waiting on a proved bank balance. The audit consequence matters as much as the effort: a line matched by hand leaves no record of why, so the External Auditor re-performs the reconciliation; a line matched here carries its score, its claiming rule, its deciding role and its timestamp, so the control can be tested instead.

### Why the Rules and the Scoring Belong Together

The scoring pass is probabilistic and the rules are deterministic, and finance needs both against the same statement. A customer receipt for an amount that has never appeared before is a scoring problem: no rule can be written for it in advance, and the answer is a ranked candidate with an explainable score. A monthly bank service fee is not a scoring problem at all: it recurs with the same label and the same narrow amount window every month, it has one destination account, and asking a reconciler to confirm it is asking them to make the same decision twelve times a year. Holding the two in one story is what lets a rule hit raise a score rather than bypass it, and what stops the same line being claimed twice by two mechanisms that do not know about each other.

### Scope Boundary

This story scores candidates, applies rules and posts the matches it resolves. It does not import a statement — that is [`STORY-001-04-01`](./STORY-001-04-01-import-bank-statements.md). It does not resolve an exception by hand, post a partial settlement, accept a difference into Bank Charges 6800 from inside a reconciliation, create a journal entry for a line with no counterpart, or reverse a match — those are [`STORY-001-04-03`](./STORY-001-04-03-manual-reconciliation.md), and they are outcomes the rules are not permitted to decide unaided. It does not open, count or close a cash register — that is [`STORY-001-04-04`](./STORY-001-04-04-manage-cash-registers.md). It defines no tax rate and no fiscal position: where a rule's write-off line carries a tax, the rate is the one FEATURE-001-05 defines and this story asserts only that its base and tax amounts post separately and in balance.
