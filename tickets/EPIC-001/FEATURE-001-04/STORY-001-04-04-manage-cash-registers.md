## User Story

**As a** Treasury Analyst

**I want** to open a cash register session on the **Cash** journal of a named company with an opening float carried from the prior session, record petty-cash receipts and disbursements against that session as posted journal entries, and close the session against a physical count whose variance posts to a named account rather than being adjusted on paper

**So that** the Cash 1000 balance of each legal entity is proved by a counted, posted and reviewable session instead of by a hand-written float sheet — which is what makes Epic Definition of Done item 7 ("every cash register is counted and reconciled") demonstrable, removes the last cash balance that the External Auditor has to reconstruct from paper, and hands FEATURE-001-07 a Cash 1000 figure that needs no month-end adjustment before the Balance Sheet cash line and the Cash Flow Statement are signed.

## Acceptance Criteria

Six criteria, inside the mandated band of 4 to 8. Each **When** carries one trigger, each **Then** asserts only what the Treasury Analyst, the Chief Accountant or the External Auditor can observe, and no criterion names a screen element or an implementation mechanism. Every monetary figure states its currency, its amount and its rounding rule; every journal entry described asserts total debits equal to total credits with both totals stated; and every criterion names the company whose books are affected.

| Scenario | Coverage class |
|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Valid input — happy path: session opened on the carried float |
| 2 | Valid input — happy path: petty-cash disbursement posted |
| 3 | Valid input — happy path: session closed on a count that agrees |
| 4 | Accounting edge case — counted shortage routed to an interim account, with the overage mirror |
| 5 | Invalid or incomplete input — one refused submission asserted over five examples |
| 6 | Error handling — a close attempted inside a locked period |

### Scenario 1: Session opened on the float carried from the prior session

- **Given** company `US-01` holds a journal of type **Cash** whose default account is Cash 1000, register `PETTY-US-01` is held on that journal, the fiscal period containing 2025-03-03 is open in `US-01`, and the register's prior session `CASH-US-01-S02` was closed on 2025-02-28 at a closing balance of USD 500.00, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the Treasury Analyst opens session `CASH-US-01-S03` on register `PETTY-US-01` dated 2025-03-03
- **Then** the opening float of `CASH-US-01-S03` reads USD 500.00 and equals the closing balance of `CASH-US-01-S02` at a difference of USD 0.00, both stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the session holds 0 cash transactions and an expected cash on hand of USD 500.00; the session records the opening Treasury Analyst, the opening timestamp and the company `US-01`. The refusal of a second concurrent session on the same journal is a different trigger — an open attempt rather than this one — and is asserted among the refusals of Scenario 5

### Scenario 2: Petty-cash disbursement posted against the open session

- **Given** session `CASH-US-01-S03` is open on register `PETTY-US-01` in `US-01` with an opening float of USD 500.00 and 0 cash transactions, and Expense 6100 exists in the chart of `US-01` alongside Cash 1000
- **When** the Treasury Analyst posts a petty-cash disbursement of USD 82.40 against Expense 6100 for office supplies, carrying receipt reference `PC-2025-0311`
- **Then** the resulting entry in the **Cash** journal of `US-01` debits Expense 6100 USD 82.40 and credits Cash 1000 USD 82.40, so total debits of USD 82.40 equal total credits of USD 82.40 at a difference of USD 0.00, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the expected cash on hand of `CASH-US-01-S03` becomes USD 417.60, being USD 500.00 less USD 82.40; the disbursement carries receipt reference `PC-2025-0311`, the acting Treasury Analyst and the posting timestamp; and the Cash 1000 balance of `US-01` attributable to register `PETTY-US-01` reads USD 417.60

### Scenario 3: Session closed on a physical count that agrees with the expected cash on hand

- **Given** session `CASH-US-01-S03` in `US-01` holds one posted disbursement of USD 82.40 against an opening float of USD 500.00, giving an expected cash on hand of USD 417.60, each amount stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the Treasury Analyst records a physical count of USD 417.60 in the denominations of the USD minor unit and closes session `CASH-US-01-S03`
- **Then** the counted variance reads USD 0.00, being the counted USD 417.60 less the expected USD 417.60; **no closing journal entry is created**, the count of journal entries created by the close being 0, because there is no variance to post and the session's own movements are already in the ledger from Scenario 2 — a zero-value entry is never written to record that a count agreed; the Cash 1000 balance of `US-01` attributable to register `PETTY-US-01` equals the counted USD 417.60 at a difference of USD 0.00; the session is closed with its counting Treasury Analyst, its count timestamp and its counted amount retained; and `CASH-US-01-S03` becomes the opening reference of the next session on that register, whose float reads USD 417.60 — every amount in this criterion rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01

### Scenario 4: Counted shortage posted to an interim account and raised for review, with the overage handled as its mirror

- **Given** session `CASH-US-01-S03` in `US-01` holds an expected cash on hand of USD 417.60, and Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 exists in the chart of `US-01` as the interim destination of a counted cash variance
- **When** the Treasury Analyst records a physical count of USD 412.60 and closes session `CASH-US-01-S03`
- **Then** the counted variance reads a shortage of USD 5.00, being the counted USD 412.60 less the expected USD 417.60; the closing entry in the **Cash** journal of `US-01` debits Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 USD 5.00 and credits Cash 1000 USD 5.00, so total debits of USD 5.00 equal total credits of USD 5.00 at a difference of USD 0.00; the variance is raised for **Chief Accountant** review carrying session `CASH-US-01-S03`, register `PETTY-US-01`, company `US-01`, the counting Treasury Analyst, the count timestamp, the expected USD 417.60, the counted USD 412.60 and the shortage USD 5.00; the Cash 1000 balance of `US-01` attributable to register `PETTY-US-01` equals the counted USD 412.60 at a difference of USD 0.00. The counted **overage** is the mirror of the same posting and is a different count and therefore a different trigger; it is owned by the first entry of § Edge Cases. The Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 route is recorded on the session as the **interim** treatment, because designating a dedicated cash short-and-over account belongs to the chart-of-accounts policy owned by [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md) and is carried here as a dependency rather than settled in this story. The USD 5.00 held in Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 cannot be cleared to a final account until the **Chief Accountant** approves the variance and names that account, so the count of clearing entries created before that approval is 0, the interim balance stays visible at USD 5.00 with its age, and the approval, its approver, the named account and its date are retained with the session — every amount in this criterion rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01

### Scenario 5: An invalid or incomplete submission is refused and leaves the session untouched

- **Given** session `CASH-US-01-S03` is open on register `PETTY-US-01` in `US-01` with an expected cash on hand of USD 417.60, stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the Treasury Analyst submits one of the submissions of the Examples table below against session `CASH-US-01-S03`
- **Then** that submission is refused with a validation message naming its failing condition together with register `PETTY-US-01`, session `CASH-US-01-S03` and company `US-01`; the count of closing entries created is 0, the count of journal entries created is 0 and the count of sessions created is 0; and session `CASH-US-01-S03` remains open with its expected cash on hand unchanged at USD 417.60 and the Cash 1000 balance of `US-01` attributable to register `PETTY-US-01` unchanged at USD 417.60, each stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01

**Examples — five submissions, one per run, the session re-read before each run.** Each row is one action with one outcome, and each is asserted by its own named test:

| # | Submission | Failing condition the message names |
|---|------------|-------------------------------------|
| 1 | A close with no physical count recorded | The absent count |
| 2 | A close on a physical count of USD −25.00 | The negative counted amount of USD −25.00 |
| 3 | A close on a physical count of USD 412.605 | The count of USD 412.605 against the USD rounding increment of 0.01, which permits 2 decimal places |
| 4 | A petty-cash disbursement of USD 0.00 against Expense 6100 | The zero disbursement amount of USD 0.00 |
| 5 | An attempt to open a second session on the **Cash** journal of `US-01` while `CASH-US-01-S03` is still open | The **Cash** journal, register `PETTY-US-01` and session `CASH-US-01-S03` already open on it |

### Scenario 6: A close attempted inside a locked period is refused and the session stays open

- **Given** session `CASH-US-01-S03` is open on register `PETTY-US-01` in `US-01` dated 2025-03-03 with an expected cash on hand of USD 417.60 and a counted USD 412.60 ready to record, `US-01` carries a journal-entry lock date of 2025-03-31 that closes the fiscal period containing 2025-03-03
- **When** the Treasury Analyst attempts to close session `CASH-US-01-S03` while that lock date stands
- **Then** the close is refused by the close wizard's own pre-posting guard before any entry is created — a guard this story requires rather than a platform lock behaviour, because the platform's own outcome for a first posting is the one the Epic's [lock-date behaviour contract](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#78-lock-date-behaviour-contract) records as **L-1**, which would post the closing entry re-dated to the accounting date of the first open period, and a cash close re-dated out of its session period would break the register-to-ledger tie-out — so the count of closing entries created is 0, the count of journal entries created is 0 and the count of entries re-dated into another period is 0; the message names the journal-entry lock date of 2025-03-31 and the affected company `US-01`; and session `CASH-US-01-S03` stays open with its expected cash on hand intact at USD 417.60 and its recorded count of USD 412.60 preserved, each stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01. Company isolation between `US-01` and `NL-01`, and the close that follows once the lock date is lifted with its report tie-out, are separate triggers.

## Edge Cases

- **Counted overage rather than a shortage.** A physical count of USD 422.60 against an expected cash on hand of USD 417.60 in `US-01` records an overage of USD 5.00 and posts the mirror of the shortage entry through the **Cash** journal: debit Cash 1000 USD 5.00 against credit Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 USD 5.00, so total debits of USD 5.00 equal total credits of USD 5.00 at a difference of USD 0.00, each amount rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01. An overage is raised for **Chief Accountant** review on the same terms as a shortage, because unexplained cash arriving in a register is a control finding in the same way that cash going missing is, and it is never netted against a shortage from a different session.
- **Register denominated in a currency other than the company currency.** Company `NL-01` (the Netherlands operating entity, functional currency EUR) holds a USD petty-cash float on a **Cash** journal whose currency is USD. The float of USD 500.00 is translated at the published rate of 1.1000 USD per EUR on the `res.currency.rate` date 2025-03-03 to EUR 454.55, and retranslated at the published closing rate of 1.0850 USD per EUR on the `res.currency.rate` date 2025-03-20 to EUR 460.83, each figure rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01. The revaluation difference of EUR 6.28 posts through the **Cash** journal of `NL-01` as debit Cash 1000 EUR 6.28 against credit FX Gain/Loss on Settlement 7100 EUR 6.28, so total debits of EUR 6.28 equal total credits of EUR 6.28 at a difference of EUR 0.00. The physical count stays a USD count: USD 412.60 counted is carried at EUR 380.28 at 1.0850 USD per EUR on 2025-03-20, and the rate together with its rate date is recorded on the session so the translation is reproducible after the fact. Translation of the register balance at the group reporting rate belongs to FEATURE-001-06 rather than here; the difference arising on the register itself is posted here.
- **Cash transferred between the register and the bank account.** Banking the surplus above the retained float moves cash in two balanced legs so that no movement is counted twice. The **Cash** journal leg of `US-01` posts debit Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 USD 820.00 against credit Cash 1000 USD 820.00 while the cash is in transit, total debits of USD 820.00 equal to total credits of USD 820.00 at a difference of USD 0.00; the **Bank** journal leg posts debit Bank 1010 USD 820.00 against credit Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 USD 820.00 when the bank presents the deposit as statement line `L-0331`, again with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of USD 0.00, and that second leg is the work of [STORY-001-04-02](./STORY-001-04-02-auto-match-statement-lines.md) rather than of this story. The banked USD 820.00 leaves the register at the moment the first leg posts, so it is excluded from the expected cash on hand at the close and cannot be counted both as cash in the drawer and as cash in transit; until the second leg posts, the USD 820.00 stays visible as a Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 balance with its age in days rather than disappearing from view. Every amount here is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01.
- **Session held open across a fiscal-period boundary or across a lock date.** A session opened on 2025-03-03 in `US-01` and still open when the journal-entry lock date of 2025-03-31 is applied cannot place its closing entry inside the locked period. The invariant asserted is that no movement reaches Cash 1000 or Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 of `US-01` inside 2025-03-01 to 2025-03-31, and that the session's expected cash on hand of USD 417.60 and its recorded count of USD 412.60 are both preserved, each stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01. **Which mechanism enforces it is established against the branch DEC-001 confirms rather than assumed here**, because the two candidates are not equivalent and the same open question governs the statement-import path in [STORY-001-04-01](./STORY-001-04-01-import-bank-statements.md): the close is refused with a named lock-date message, or the closing entry is re-dated into the first open period as `account.move._post` does for a manually posted entry. Under refusal the Treasury Analyst has to act before the books move; under re-dating the entry is already in April and has to be found there. Either way the count date of the session is retained so the counted figure is never restated, and the acceptance test asserts the observed mechanism rather than a disjunction. The controlled path where the close must land in March is a lock exception recorded with its author, its reason and its expiry for the External Auditor; a session is never closed by back-dating its entry past the lock date; and the lock-date administration itself belongs to [STORY-001-01-05](../FEATURE-001-01/STORY-001-01-05-configure-period-lock-dates.md).
- **Session with zero cash transactions.** A session opened at a float of USD 500.00 in `US-01` and closed the same day on a count of USD 500.00 with no receipt and no disbursement recorded closes with a variance of USD 0.00 and carries its float of USD 500.00 forward as the next session's opening reference. No entry is written for the close at all: the count of journal entries created by it is 0, which is the same outcome Scenario 3 asserts for a count that agrees, so a session that moved no cash and counted no variance leaves no zero-value entry behind. Every amount here is stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01. The session is still retained with its counting Treasury Analyst and its count timestamp, because a register counted and found unchanged is evidence of the control operating rather than an absence of activity to record.

### Cash Session Lifecycle

The lifecycle below is the outcome sequence the criteria assert. It states what must be observable at each step, not how any step is implemented.

1. **Open.** A session opens on one register held on a **Cash** journal of one named company, and its opening float is carried from the prior session's closing balance — `CASH-US-01-S03` opens at USD 500.00 because `CASH-US-01-S02` closed at USD 500.00 on 2025-02-28, the two agreeing at a difference of USD 0.00 to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01. The opening role, the opening timestamp and the company are recorded.
2. **Transact.** Cash receipts and cash disbursements are recorded against the open session, each posting through the **Cash** journal of that company as a journal entry whose total debits equal its total credits — the worked disbursement of USD 82.40 debiting Expense 6100 and crediting Cash 1000. Each movement carries its receipt reference and its acting role.
3. **Compute.** The expected cash on hand is the opening float plus session receipts less session disbursements, less any amount already banked and now in transit: USD 500.00 less USD 82.40 gives USD 417.60. Cash in transit is excluded here precisely so that it is not counted a second time at the close.
4. **Count.** A physical count is recorded at the close, expressed in the denominations of the currency's minor unit and validated against the currency's rounding increment, so a USD count of 3 decimal places is refused where the increment permits 2. The counting role, the count timestamp and the counted amount are retained against the session.
5. **Compare.** The variance is the counted amount less the expected amount — USD 412.60 less USD 417.60 gives a shortage of USD 5.00, and a count of USD 422.60 would give an overage of USD 5.00. A zero variance is a stated USD 0.00 result rather than an absence of one.
6. **Post.** Where a variance exists, a balanced closing entry is posted in the **Cash** journal of that company; where the variance is USD 0.00 no closing entry is created at all, the count of entries the close writes being 0. Where a variance exists the entry carries it against Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099, debiting that account for a shortage and crediting it for an overage, with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of USD 0.00 in either direction. A close whose entry would fall inside a period closed by the company's journal-entry lock date is refused before any entry is created.
7. **Close and carry forward.** The session closes and becomes the opening reference of the next session on that register, so the counted USD 412.60 is the next float. **Only one session may be open per Cash journal per company at any time**, which is what prevents one float from being transacted from two places or counted twice.

### Segregation of Duties

- **The Treasury Analyst is the register custodian.** The same role holds the float, records its receipts and disbursements, performs the physical count and closes the session. Custody and counting sit together deliberately, because a count performed by someone who does not hold the drawer proves nothing about the drawer.
- **The Chief Accountant reviews and clears the variance.** A counted difference routed to Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 is reviewed by the Chief Accountant, who names the account that finally receives it, and the review, the reviewer, the named account and the date are retained against the session. The role that counts is therefore never the role that decides where a difference lands.
- **A variance above an agreed threshold requires that second review before the suspense balance is cleared.** The threshold is a control to be confirmed with the Finance SME and held as governed configuration per company — it is deliberately **not** stated as a number in this story, because a figure that is right for a USD 500.00 petty-cash float in `US-01` is not right for a larger register in another entity. Until it is confirmed, every counted variance is treated as requiring review.
- **Company boundaries are part of the control.** A Treasury Analyst holding access to one company can neither open nor close a register held by another, in either direction, and the External Auditor holds read-only access to sessions, counts, variances and reviews across the companies in audit scope without needing a data request.
- **The interim variance account is not the resting place.** Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 carries a counted variance only until it is reviewed and cleared, and SM-006 requires no unexplained suspense balance to survive the period. Designating a dedicated cash short-and-over account is a chart-of-accounts decision owned by [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md).

- This session's counted shortage of USD 5.00 does not disturb that: the shortage posts to Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 at the close, the custodian reimburses USD 5.00 in cash on review, and the two net to zero inside the month. Neither leg is a receipt or a payment of the register, so neither enters the USD 1,250.00 or the USD 430.00 above.
- The feature's counted-difference variant of USD 2.50, worked at a counted USD 1,317.50 against the expected USD 1,320.00, is a variant of the `CASH-US-01-S07` close and is a different count from this session's USD 5.00 shortage.

Every amount above is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01. Neither set of figures restates the other, which is why every report assertion in this story is scoped to the **Cash** journal *and* to register `PETTY-US-01` for session `CASH-US-01-S03` rather than to a month total.

The control design that governs both levels is settled and is stated the same way in the parent feature and here: **the count is posted and the review gates the clearing.** A counted variance never blocks the close, because the ledger must carry the cash that was actually counted; the close posts the counted balance and routes the variance to Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 in a balanced entry, raising it for **Chief Accountant** review. What the review gates is the **clearing** of that interim balance: no counted variance leaves Suspense / Outstanding Payments 1099 without an approval, a named receiving account, an approver and a date, and the count of clearing entries created before that approval is 0. The review threshold above which approval is required remains governed configuration per company, recorded in [Segregation of Duties](#segregation-of-duties); until it is confirmed every counted variance is reviewed.

### Business Context

The feature's problem statement records that cash registers and petty-cash floats sit outside the ledger entirely: a float is counted on a sheet of paper, a shortfall is settled by whoever is standing there, and Cash 1000 is adjusted at month end to whatever the sheet says. There is no opening balance, no posted receipt or payment, no counted close and no review of a difference, so the cash on hand is unauditable by construction. This story is the answer to that one paragraph. It is deliberately the smallest of the four stories in scope and the only one that needs nothing from the others, which is why the feature draws it as a parallel track that can start on day one — and why a programme under schedule pressure can deliver visible, auditable cash control before the first bank statement is ever imported.
