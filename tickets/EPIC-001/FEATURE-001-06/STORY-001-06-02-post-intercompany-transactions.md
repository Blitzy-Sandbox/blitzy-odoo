This story creates the intercompany population that the rest of `FEATURE-001-06/` consumes. One shared-services charge is raised by **Global Holdings Inc.** (`US-01`) on **Global Europe SARL** (`NL-01`) and recorded twice — once in the selling company's books and once in the buying company's books — because Odoo holds one journal entry against exactly one company, so a transaction between two legal entities is two balanced entries and not one. The group fixture, the functional currencies and the dated rate table this story consumes are fixed by [STORY-001-06-01](./STORY-001-06-01-configure-company-hierarchy.md) and drawn from the Epic's canonical registers ([Appendix E.2](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#e2-canonical-group-chart-of-accounts) and [Appendix E.4](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#e4-canonical-legal-entity-register)); no entity, code or rate is redefined here. ## User Story

**As a** Consolidation Accountant

**I want** an intercompany shared-services charge raised between two group entities to be recorded as two entries that each balance in their own company's books — the selling entry in Global Holdings Inc.'s Sales journal against Intercompany Receivable 1300 and Revenue 4000, and the buying entry in Global Europe SARL's Purchase journal against Expense 6100 and Intercompany Payable 2100, converted into that company's functional currency at the rate carried for the transaction date — with the counterparty entity named on both entries at the moment of posting and with the resulting monetary balance retranslated at the closing rate for the reporting date

**So that** the group's trading with itself is identifiable from the ledger rather than reconstructed from working papers, the intercompany pair between Global Holdings Inc. and Global Europe SARL nets to `$0.00 USD` at the 2025-03-31 closing rate of 1.0850 USD/EUR — rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 — and the elimination run of [STORY-001-06-04](./STORY-001-06-04-eliminate-intercompany-balances.md) removes a matched population instead of chasing an unexplained residual (SM-012).

## Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria are authored, at the top of the 4-to-8 bound the Epic sets for a story, and they carry the mandated coverage distribution: **Scenarios 1, 2, 3 and 7** are the valid-input cases (the selling side, the buying side, the tax presentation on the buying side, then the report that proves the pair); **Scenario 4** is the invalid and incomplete-input case; **Scenarios 5 and 8** are the error-handling cases — the asymmetric period lock that drives the pair into the named in-transit state, and the refusal of one person authorizing both legs; and **Scenario 6** is the accounting edge case, where a rate movement between the transaction date and the closing date is measured rather than absorbed.

Every criterion names the company whose books are affected by registered name, and the balance assertion is made **separately in each company's books** — never once across the pair. Every amount states its currency and its rounding, and every converted amount also states the rate and the rate date it was converted at, so no figure below is a bare conversion.

### Why one transaction is two entries, and why both legs are denominated in USD

Two facts decide the shape of the criteria below, and both were read in this repository at Odoo 19.0 Community:

- **A journal entry belongs to one company.** `account.move` carries a single `company_id` (`addons/account/models/account_move.py` L174) and a single `journal_id` (L160) owned by that company, and the balance guard `_check_balanced` (L2749) refuses an unbalanced entry with `The entry is not balanced.` (L2760) per entry. An intercompany transaction is therefore **two entries, each balanced on its own** — which is exactly why the debits-equal-credits assertion is written twice below, once per company, and why a single combined assertion across the pair would prove nothing about either set of books.
- **No counterpart-generation mechanism is present.** No field of the kind Odoo uses to drive automatic generation of a counterpart document in a second company exists anywhere under `addons/*/models` in this repository. How the second entry comes into being is therefore a discovery question (§ Technical Discovery Notes), while *that* it exists, balances and names its counterparty is fixed here.

The charge is **denominated in USD on both sides**: `$40,000.00 USD`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, is the transaction currency of the document raised by Global Holdings Inc. and of the document recorded by Global Europe SARL, even though Global Europe SARL measures it in its own functional currency of EUR. That single decision is what allows the elimination in [STORY-001-06-04](./STORY-001-06-04-eliminate-intercompany-balances.md) to net Intercompany Receivable 1300 against Intercompany Payable 2100 to exactly `$0.00 USD` and to contribute `$0.00 USD` to consolidated Revenue 4000 and `$0.00 USD` to consolidated Expense 6100. It is also what makes Intercompany Payable 2100 a **foreign-currency monetary item in Global Europe SARL's books**, which is why Scenario 6 exists at all.

### ICP-001 — The Intercompany Pair Contract

A journal entry belongs to one company, so an intercompany transaction is two entries. That is a platform fact, not a licence for a pair to exist in one book only. The contract below fixes what a pair **is**, who may create each of its legs, and what happens between the first leg and the second, and every criterion in this story is read against it.

**A pair is one business episode carrying one durable, immutable identity.** At the moment the first leg is prepared, the pair is assigned a **pair identifier** — a UUID version 4 — recorded on both legs, on the pair record and in the pair's history. The identifier is held by a database unique constraint over the originating company, the counterparty company and that identifier, so a second mirroring attempt for the same pair resolves to the existing pair rather than creating another; and it is **immutable**: it is not editable on a posted leg by any role, including the **Consolidation Accountant** who created it, so the count of posted legs whose pair identifier was changed after posting is **0**. A genuinely different transaction between the same two entities is a **new pair with a new identifier**, which is what makes matching by identity rather than by amount possible — Scenario 6 asserts a second `$40,000.00 USD` charge between the same two companies resolving to its own pair.

**Either both legs post in one transaction, or the pair stands in a named in-transit state — never silently half-recorded.** Where both companies are ready, the mirroring routine writes the two entries **inside one database transaction**: both commit or neither does, so a failure between them leaves 0 journal entries in Global Holdings Inc.'s books and 0 in Global Europe SARL's books rather than one posted leg and one absent one. Where the second leg cannot be written at that moment — an asymmetric period lock, an absent counterparty designation, a counterparty entity that records its bill on receipt — the pair does not fall back to silence. It is placed in the named state **Intercompany In-Transit**, and that state is a governed object rather than a report line:

- it is recorded on the pair with its pair identifier, both company names, the transaction amount of `$40,000.00 USD`, the accounting date, the posted leg, the absent leg and the reason the absent leg could not be written;
- it carries a **named owner** and an age measured in days from the posted leg's accounting date, and it appears on the **Intercompany Reconciliation** report as of any date on which it is open, so the count of open in-transit pairs invisible on that report is **0**;
- it **blocks** the elimination run for that pair in [STORY-001-06-04](./STORY-001-06-04-eliminate-intercompany-balances.md) and the period close for the companies it spans, so a group position is never published as if the pair were complete;
- it closes only in one of two recorded ways — the absent leg is posted against the same pair identifier, or the posted leg is reversed under the reversal path of its own company — and each closure is appended to the pair's history with its actor, its timestamp and the balances before and after. An in-transit pair is never closed by writing a difference to Retained Earnings 3100, Currency Translation Adjustment 3200 or Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss 7200.

**Each leg is authorized in its own company, and one person cannot authorize both.** A leg is posted under the authority of a role holding the posting right **in the company whose book it is written in**, and the pair is released only when both legs carry an authorization recorded against a different human: the count of pairs whose two legs were authorized by one person is **0**, and the count of legs posted by a role with no posting right in that leg's own company is **0**. The refusal is a control rather than a warning — the **Consolidation Accountant** who authorized the selling leg holds no override permitting it to authorize the buying leg, and where one person is the only holder of the right in both companies the pair waits for a second holder rather than proceeding. A C-023 integration principal is scoped to a single company, so the count of principals able to authorize both legs is **0**. The per-company posting, approval, mirroring, elimination and issuing rights are enumerated in the feature's [GRP-01 authorization matrix](../FEATURE-001-06-multi-company-consolidation.md#45-grp-01-authorization-matrix), which this story implements for the posting and mirroring rows.

**The pair identity is what the run-once guard is built on.** Under C-028 the mirroring episode is keyed on the pair identifier held by that unique constraint, taken under a row lock across the read-decide-write window, revalidated inside the lock against the pair's recorded state, and reclaimed rather than blocked when a lease goes stale — so a replayed mirroring produces 1 buying leg rather than 2, and the guard is not overridable by the role it constrains.

### Scenario 1: Selling side posted in Global Holdings Inc.'s books against Intercompany Receivable 1300 and Revenue 4000

- **Given** the consolidation scope configured by [STORY-001-06-01](./STORY-001-06-01-configure-company-hierarchy.md) holds Global Holdings Inc. (`US-01`) as the consolidation parent of Global Europe SARL (`NL-01`) at functional currency EUR and Global Asia Pte Ltd (`SG-01`) at functional currency SGD, with Global Holdings Inc. measuring in USD, which is also the group presentation currency; Intercompany Receivable 1300, Intercompany Payable 2100, Revenue 4000 and Expense 6100 exist in the chart of every one of the three companies; a partner record representing Global Europe SARL as a sister company is resolvable from Global Holdings Inc.; the Sales journal of Global Holdings Inc. exists; and the fiscal period of Global Holdings Inc. covering 2025-03-15 is open
- **When** the Consolidation Accountant posts the intercompany shared-services charge of `$40,000.00 USD`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, dated 2025-03-15 from Global Holdings Inc. to Global Europe SARL through the Sales journal of Global Holdings Inc.
- **Then** the books of Global Holdings Inc. carry one posted entry with a debit to Intercompany Receivable 1300 of `$40,000.00 USD` and a credit to Revenue 4000 of `$40,000.00 USD`, so **total debits of `$40,000.00 USD` equal total credits of `$40,000.00 USD` at a difference of `0.00 USD` in Global Holdings Inc.'s books**, each amount rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the entry names Global Europe SARL as its counterparty entity and carries a document reference by which the counterpart entry is matched, so the pair is identified at posting time rather than reconstructed at period end; the charge is recorded against Intercompany Receivable 1300 rather than against Accounts Receivable 1200, so a third-party receivable balance is not overstated by group trading; and no journal item is created in the books of Global Europe SARL or of Global Asia Pte Ltd by this posting, because a journal entry belongs to one company

### Scenario 2: Buying side posted in Global Europe SARL's books in its EUR functional currency at the 2025-03-15 transaction rate

- **Given** the selling entry of Scenario 1 is posted in Global Holdings Inc.'s books; Global Europe SARL measures in its functional currency of EUR, whose rounding increment is 0.01 and whose decimal places are therefore 2; the Purchase journal of Global Europe SARL exists; a partner record representing Global Holdings Inc. as a sister company is resolvable from Global Europe SARL; the rate table carries the **USD/EUR transaction rate of 1.0800 USD/EUR for the rate date 2025-03-15**; and the fiscal period of Global Europe SARL covering 2025-03-15 is open
- **When** the Consolidation Accountant posts the mirrored USD-denominated intercompany bill of `$40,000.00 USD`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, dated 2025-03-15 through the Purchase journal of Global Europe SARL
- **Then** the books of Global Europe SARL carry one posted entry with a debit to Expense 6100 of `€37,037.04 EUR` and a credit to Intercompany Payable 2100 of `€37,037.04 EUR`, converted from `$40,000.00 USD` at the **2025-03-15 transaction rate of 1.0800 USD/EUR** — an unrounded `€37,037.0370 EUR` rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01 — so **total debits of `€37,037.04 EUR` equal total credits of `€37,037.04 EUR` at a difference of `0.00 EUR` in Global Europe SARL's books**; both journal items retain `$40,000.00 USD` as the transaction-currency amount alongside the `€37,037.04 EUR` functional-currency amount, so the payable stays identifiable as a USD monetary item; the entry names Global Holdings Inc. as its counterparty entity and carries the same document reference as the selling entry; the charge is recorded against Intercompany Payable 2100 rather than against Accounts Payable 2000, so a third-party payable balance is not overstated by group trading; and the entry posted in Global Holdings Inc.'s books is unchanged by this posting, so its total debits of `$40,000.00 USD` still equal its total credits of `$40,000.00 USD` at a difference of `0.00 USD` in Global Holdings Inc.'s books

### Scenario 3: Reverse-charge tax presented as tax code, base amount and tax amount as three separate values

- **Given** the fiscal position resolved for Global Europe SARL (`NL-01`) with Global Holdings Inc. (`US-01`) as the counterparty selects the reverse-charge tax code `VAT-21-RC` from the governed tax-code catalogue owned by [FEATURE-001-05](../FEATURE-001-05-tax-configuration-compliance.md) and configured by [STORY-001-05-01](../FEATURE-001-05/STORY-001-05-01-configure-tax-codes-fiscal-positions.md); Tax Payable 2200 and Input Tax Receivable 1290 exist in the chart of Global Europe SARL; and the mirrored intercompany bill of `$40,000.00 USD`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, dated 2025-03-15 stands ready to post through the Purchase journal of Global Europe SARL
- **When** the Consolidation Accountant posts that mirrored intercompany bill in Global Europe SARL
- **Then** the posted tax line in Global Europe SARL's books records **three separately stated values** — a **tax code** of `VAT-21-RC` ("VAT 21% (Intra-Community Acquisition, Reverse Charge)", 21.0000%, per [TAX-REG-001](../FEATURE-001-05-tax-configuration-compliance.md#111-authoritative-tax-code-register-tax-reg-001)), a **base amount** of `€37,037.04 EUR` (`$40,000.00 USD` at the 2025-03-15 transaction rate of 1.0800 USD/EUR) and a **tax amount** of `€7,777.78 EUR` (`$8,400.00 USD` at the same rate, being `€37,037.04 EUR` × 21.0000%), every amount rounded to 2 decimal places at the rounding increment of 0.01 for its currency — and no single tax-inclusive total stands in place of that split; because `VAT-21-RC` shifts the accounting for the tax to the acquirer, the `€7,777.78 EUR` it raises in Global Europe SARL's books posts as **two named legs of the same amount** — a debit to Input Tax Receivable 1290 of `€7,777.78 EUR` for the recoverable input tax and a credit to Tax Payable 2200 of `€7,777.78 EUR` for the self-assessed output tax — whose **net** movement across those two accounts is `€0.00 EUR`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01, which is a net of zero rather than a tax amount of zero; **total debits of `€44,814.82 EUR` equal total credits of `€44,814.82 EUR` at a difference of `0.00 EUR` in Global Europe SARL's books** with the tax lines present, being `€37,037.04 EUR` of base plus `€7,777.78 EUR` of tax on each side; no tax amount is added to Intercompany Payable 2100, which stands at `€37,037.04 EUR`, so the intercompany pair carries the base amount alone; and the tax code, the base amount and the tax amount are readable on the posted line for the VAT/Tax Return that [FEATURE-001-05](../FEATURE-001-05-tax-configuration-compliance.md) produces, without this story defining a tax code, a rate or a tax account of its own

### Scenario 4: Intercompany bill with no counterparty entity designation is refused and neither company's books are touched

- **Given** the selling entry of Scenario 1 is posted in Global Holdings Inc.'s books with Global Europe SARL named as its counterparty; the fiscal periods of both Global Holdings Inc. and Global Europe SARL covering 2025-03-15 are open; and a mirrored intercompany bill of `$40,000.00 USD`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, dated 2025-03-15 is prepared in Global Europe SARL against Intercompany Payable 2100 with **no counterparty entity designated** — no sister-company partner resolving to Global Holdings Inc. and no intercompany document reference
- **When** the Consolidation Accountant posts that mirrored intercompany bill in Global Europe SARL
- **Then** the posting is refused with a validation message naming Intercompany Payable 2100 and the absent counterparty entity designation as the reason; the count of journal entries created by the refused attempt is **0 in Global Europe SARL's books and 0 in Global Holdings Inc.'s books**, so the pair is never left half-recorded by this attempt; Intercompany Payable 2100 in Global Europe SARL's books stands at `€0.00 EUR`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01; the entry already posted in Global Holdings Inc.'s books is untouched, its total debits of `$40,000.00 USD` still equalling its total credits of `$40,000.00 USD` at a difference of `0.00 USD` in Global Holdings Inc.'s books, and its Intercompany Receivable 1300 balance still reading `$40,000.00 USD` rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; and that selling-side balance of `$40,000.00 USD` is therefore left unmatched in the ledger, with no counterparty leg anywhere in Global Europe SARL against it, which is the state the **Intercompany Reconciliation** report reads when it is next run — the report’s own presentation of that state belongs to Scenario 7 rather than here, because no report is run by this posting attempt

### Scenario 5: Asymmetric period lock blocks the buying side and the pair enters the named Intercompany In-Transit state

- **Given** the selling entry of Scenario 1 is posted in Global Holdings Inc.'s books, whose fiscal period covering 2025-03-15 is open; the journal-entry lock date of Global Europe SARL is set to 2025-03-31 under the closing controls owned by [STORY-001-01-05](../FEATURE-001-01/STORY-001-01-05-configure-period-lock-dates.md), so the period of Global Europe SARL covering 2025-03-15 is locked; and the mirrored intercompany bill of `$40,000.00 USD`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, dated 2025-03-15 carries Global Holdings Inc. as its counterparty entity
- **When** the Consolidation Accountant posts that mirrored intercompany bill through the Purchase journal of Global Europe SARL
- **Then** the mirrored posting is blocked by the mirroring routine's own pre-posting guard — the guard the Epic's [lock-date behaviour contract](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#78-lock-date-behaviour-contract) records as **L-11**, which inspects the lock dates **before the journal entry is constructed** so no `account.move` exists for the attempt and no journal sequence number is consumed, and which is required because the platform would otherwise post the buying-side leg re-dated into the first open period under L-1 and leave the two legs of one intercompany transaction in different periods — with the guard's own message naming **Global Europe SARL**, its violated journal-entry lock date of **2025-03-31** and the remedial action, rather than the platform text of L-2; the books of Global Europe SARL are unchanged, so the count of journal entries created is 0 and Intercompany Payable 2100 in Global Europe SARL's books stands at `€0.00 EUR`, rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01; the entry already posted in Global Holdings Inc.'s books is neither reversed nor altered, its total debits of `$40,000.00 USD` still equalling its total credits of `$40,000.00 USD` at a difference of `0.00 USD` in Global Holdings Inc.'s books; the Intercompany Receivable 1300 balance of `$40,000.00 USD` in Global Holdings Inc.’s books is left standing and unmatched, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, rather than being cleared, absorbed into equity or accepted in silence — which is the ledger state the **Intercompany Reconciliation** report reads when it is next run, its presentation belonging to Scenario 7 rather than here; the pair does not rest on that report alone — under [ICP-001](#icp-001--the-intercompany-pair-contract) it is placed in the named **Intercompany In-Transit** state carrying its immutable pair identifier, both company names, the transaction amount of `$40,000.00 USD` rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, the accounting date of 2025-03-15, the posted selling leg, the absent buying leg, the reason (the journal-entry lock date of Global Europe SARL) and a named owner, so the count of open in-transit pairs absent from the **Intercompany Reconciliation** report as of 2025-03-31 is 0; while that state is open the elimination run of [STORY-001-06-04](./STORY-001-06-04-eliminate-intercompany-balances.md) posts 0 entries for this pair and the close of Global Holdings Inc. and of Global Europe SARL is blocked, so no group position is published as though the pair were complete; and the state closes only by posting the absent buying leg against the same pair identifier or by reversing the selling leg in Global Holdings Inc.'s own books, each closure appended to the pair's history with its actor, its timestamp and the balances before and after — never by writing the `$40,000.00 USD` difference to Retained Earnings 3100, Currency Translation Adjustment 3200 or Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss 7200; and the message states the remedy — the mirrored bill is recorded on a date after the lock date of Global Europe SARL, or the period of Global Europe SARL is reopened through the recorded lock-date exception path, so no locked journal item of Global Europe SARL is rewritten either way

### Scenario 6: The USD monetary payable is retranslated at the closing rate and the exchange difference is measured

- **Given** the mirrored entry of Scenario 2 is posted in Global Europe SARL's books, so Intercompany Payable 2100 holds a USD-denominated monetary balance of `$40,000.00 USD` carried at `€37,037.04 EUR` at the 2025-03-15 transaction rate of 1.0800 USD/EUR, rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01; the rate table carries the **USD/EUR closing rate of 1.0850 USD/EUR for the rate date 2025-03-31**; Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss 7200 and Currency Translation Adjustment 3200 both exist in the chart of Global Europe SARL; and the period of Global Europe SARL covering 2025-03-31 is open

A rejected document writes no journal entry in either company, discloses no stack trace or complete payload, leaves the service available and leaves 0 temporary artifacts behind. Values written to CSV and XLSX exports of the **Intercompany Reconciliation** report are neutralized against formula injection. The exchange rates this story applies are recorded and validated by [STORY-001-06-01](./STORY-001-06-01-configure-company-hierarchy.md), which owns the rate-feed trust boundary; this story reads a stored, approved rate and never ingests one.

### Accounting Standards Compliance

- [ ] **IFRS 10 and US GAAP ASC 810 — intercompany transactions are identified for elimination.** Global Europe SARL is wholly owned at 100.00% and consolidated in full, so the whole of this charge is intragroup and none of it survives consolidation, and because ownership is 100.00% no non-controlling interest arises for any part of it — non-controlling-interest allocation stays outside this Epic's scope. Identifying the pair at posting time, on both entries, is what makes the elimination provable rather than estimated.
- [ ] **IAS 21 and US GAAP ASC 830 — monetary-item remeasurement is distinguished from net-investment translation.** Intercompany Payable 2100 is a USD monetary item in Global Europe SARL's EUR books, so it is retranslated at the 2025-03-31 closing rate of 1.0850 USD/EUR and the `€170.68 EUR` difference, rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01, is recognized in profit or loss at Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss 7200. The difference arising on translating the net investment in Global Europe SARL into the group presentation currency USD is a separate difference recognized in equity at Currency Translation Adjustment 3200, and it belongs to [STORY-001-06-03](./STORY-001-06-03-define-consolidation-rules.md) and [STORY-001-06-05](./STORY-001-06-05-generate-consolidated-financials.md). Neither difference is written at the other's code.
- [ ] **IFRS 12 — the transaction is attributable to a named entity pair.** Both entries name their counterparty entity, so the disclosure of transactions between group members is read from the ledger rather than assembled from notes.
- [ ] **Transfer pricing is recorded, not determined.** The arm's-length basis of the `$40,000.00 USD` charge, rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, is a policy input signed off by the Finance SME. This story records and pairs the resulting charge; it neither computes nor challenges the price, and the Epic keeps transfer-pricing determination outside its scope.

### Discovery versus Prescription

| **Effort** | Medium-High | Two coordinated postings across two companies rather than one posting in one: 2 trading entries carrying 4 journal items, 1 retranslation entry carrying 2 journal items, the reverse-charge tax lines, the counterparty and reference written onto both entries, 2 refusal guards, and 1 named report with an as-of-date parameter and an unmatched-item path. The fiscal position and the reverse-charge repartition interact with the posting, so the tax code, base amount and tax amount stay separable and the net movement in Global Europe SARL's books is `€0.00 EUR`. And the IAS 21 remeasurement has to land at Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss 7200 without touching Currency Translation Adjustment 3200 |
| **Uncertainty** | Medium | Two unknowns are named rather than open. How the counterpart entry is produced — generated from the selling document, authored in the receiving company, or supplied by an intercompany add-on — is a bounded design choice with an existing external precedent in OCA/multi-company, and no counterpart-generation field exists in this repository to reuse. Where the pairing key lives is equally bounded. Above the story sit DEC-001 for the platform version and DEC-002 for the edition source; neither changes the accounting outcome, because both entries are ordinary journal entries under every candidate path, but DEC-002 does change how much of the mechanism is built rather than configured |
| **Story Points** | **8** | Fibonacci scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) |

**Why 8 and not 5.** A single balanced posting in one company would sit at 3, and adding a dated cross-currency conversion would sit at 5. Three factors carry it to 8: the outcome is **two** postings whose balance has to be proved independently in two functional currencies, so the failure mode is a half-recorded pair rather than an unbalanced entry; the fiscal-position and reverse-charge interaction adds a tax presentation that has to stay separable from the base amount and out of the intercompany balance; and the asymmetric-lock failure mode of Scenario 5 is a cross-company behaviour with no single-company analogue, requiring the already-posted side to be reported as unmatched rather than reversed.

**Why 8 and not 13.** The accounting is settled and arithmetically verified — `€37,037.04 EUR`, `€36,866.36 EUR` and `€170.68 EUR`, each rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01, are fixed figures rather than open questions — and every model this story touches is present in this repository with its field surface read. No consolidation engine, no report engine and no elimination logic is built here, no data migration is involved, and the two design unknowns are bounded choices with a named external precedent rather than unanswered questions.

## Notes

### Business Context

Intercompany trading in this group is invisible until someone goes looking for it. A shared-services charge raised by Global Holdings Inc. on Global Europe SARL lands in Accounts Receivable 1200 in one company and Accounts Payable 2000 in the other, indistinguishable from third-party trade, so the group discovers its own trading at period end by comparing two trial balances in a workbook and reconciling the difference by hand. Two consequences follow every quarter: consolidated revenue and cost are overstated until someone remembers the charge, and a currency movement between the transaction date and the reporting date shows up as an unexplained residual rather than as a measured exchange difference. This story is the smallest change that makes the pair identifiable **at the moment of posting** — a dedicated receivable and payable code, a named counterparty entity on both entries, and a shared document reference — which is what turns the elimination in [STORY-001-06-04](./STORY-001-06-04-eliminate-intercompany-balances.md) from a reconciliation exercise into an assertion.

### Why Both Legs Are Denominated in USD, and Why That Decision Belongs Here

The charge is `$40,000.00 USD` in the transaction currency of both documents, even though Global Europe SARL measures it in EUR at `€37,037.04 EUR`. That single decision is what makes the pair eliminable: Intercompany Receivable 1300 in Global Holdings Inc.'s books and Intercompany Payable 2100 in Global Europe SARL's books are the **same USD obligation**, so [STORY-001-06-04](./STORY-001-06-04-eliminate-intercompany-balances.md) nets them to exactly `$0.00 USD` and the trading contributes `$0.00 USD` to consolidated Revenue 4000 and `$0.00 USD` to consolidated Expense 6100, each rounded to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01. Had each side been denominated in its own functional currency instead, the two legs would drift apart with every rate movement and the elimination would carry a permanent residual that no reconciliation could close. This note is recorded so the design intent survives refinement: a later request to raise the buying side in EUR is a change to the elimination contract, not a presentation preference.

The corollary is that Intercompany Payable 2100 is a **foreign-currency monetary item in Global Europe SARL's books**, which is why the `€170.68 EUR` remeasurement of Scenario 6, rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01, exists at all and why it is presented at Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss 7200 in profit or loss under IAS 21 rather than at Currency Translation Adjustment 3200 in equity. The two differences are distinct: the remeasurement arises inside Global Europe SARL's own books on an obligation it owes in a currency other than its functional currency, while the translation adjustment arises only when the net investment in Global Europe SARL is expressed in the group presentation currency. Writing one at the other's code would misstate both profit and equity, which is why the Epic's canonical account register assigns them separate codes and this story asserts that Currency Translation Adjustment 3200 carries no movement from the remeasurement.

### Reconciliation With the Parent Feature's Worked Figures

The parent Feature states its primary worked figure with the charge dated **2025-03-31**, in which case both legs are recognized at the closing rate of 1.0850 USD/EUR and Global Europe SARL records `€36,866.36 EUR` at initial recognition, rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01, with no remeasurement arising. This story works the same charge dated **2025-03-15** at the transaction rate of 1.0800 USD/EUR, which is the variant the Feature also carries expressly, and the two converge: under either dating, Intercompany Payable 2100 in Global Europe SARL's books **carries `€36,866.36 EUR` at 2025-03-31**, which is the balance [STORY-001-06-04](./STORY-001-06-04-eliminate-intercompany-balances.md) eliminates at `$40,000.00 USD` and [STORY-001-06-05](./STORY-001-06-05-generate-consolidated-financials.md) presents. The only difference is where the `€170.68 EUR` sits: under the 2025-03-31 dating it never arises, and under the 2025-03-15 dating it sits at Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss 7200 with Expense 6100 carrying `€37,037.04 EUR`, whose net effect on the equity of Global Europe SARL is the same `€36,866.36 EUR` — each amount rounded to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01. No consolidated total, translated balance or elimination amount in the Feature changes as a result, and the earlier dating is the more demanding of the two because it exercises the remeasurement path.
