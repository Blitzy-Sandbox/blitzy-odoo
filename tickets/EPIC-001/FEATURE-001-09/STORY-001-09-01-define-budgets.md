## User Story

**As a** FP&A Analyst

**I want** to define a budget held against one named company and one fiscal year — company `US-01`, the group's United States entity whose functional currency is USD and which carries the group reporting currency, for fiscal year 2025 running 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 — carrying budget lines against the general-ledger accounts Revenue 4000, Expense 6100 and Depreciation Expense 6500 and against the analytic accounts of the group's analytic plans, and to confirm that budget once every line carries a dimension and a period range inside its fiscal year

**So that** a measurable revenue and spending target exists inside the accounting system rather than beside it in a spreadsheet, giving [STORY-001-09-02](./STORY-001-09-02-allocate-budget-periods.md) an amount to spread across the fiscal calendar and [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) a plan figure to set against the actual amounts aggregated from posted journal entries whose total debits equal their total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, so that the variance management acts on and the general ledger behind it cannot disagree (SM-014).

### Business Context

Budget definition is the foundational step of this feature: an allocation has nothing to spread, a comparison has nothing to compare and a threshold has nothing to police until a budget record and its lines exist. Today the group's plan is held in spreadsheets and re-keyed against Odoo actuals after each period end, so the comparison lands after the spend it would have questioned was already committed, and the approval, the thresholds and the explanations that evidence budget control exist as spreadsheet history at best.

This story moves the plan itself into the system of record. It delivers the budget header — its name, its company, its fiscal year, its responsible owner and its state — and the budget lines that carry a general-ledger account, an analytic distribution, a planned amount and a period range. It delivers the confirm-time validation that refuses an incomplete plan, and it delivers the group-wide reference numbering that makes a budget citable in an audit file — one counter for every entity, so a reference names one budget in the whole group.

**A budget is a plan, not a posting.** Recording and confirming a budget creates, alters and reverses no journal entry, and it leaves every account balance and the Trial Balance of the affected company unchanged. That boundary is deliberate and is asserted as an acceptance criterion below: budget figures are management information presented beside a statutory statement, never inside one, as §7.3 of the parent feature records. The actual amounts this budget will later be measured against are aggregated from `account.move.line` records belonging to journal entries in the posted state, each of which balances with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `0.00` in the company currency.

## Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria are authored, inside the 4-to-8 bound the Epic sets in [§5.3](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#53-feature-and-story-decomposition-guidelines), and they carry the mandated coverage distribution: Scenarios 1 and 2 are the valid-input cases (an allocated plan approved by the Group Controller, then a line held against analytic dimensions), Scenario 3 is the invalid and incomplete-input case, Scenarios 4 and 5 are the error-handling cases, and Scenarios 6, 7 and 8 are the accounting edge cases — the fiscal-calendar lock, multi-company scoping and duplication of a prior-year plan. Two contracts stand ahead of the eight and are read by every one of them: [BGT-SOD-001](#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract), which fixes who may approve a plan, what must be complete before the approval, what may change afterwards and where the check lives; and [BHI-001](#bhi-001--the-budget-history-load-contract), which fixes the load of a prior-year plan that predates the system.

Every scenario states a single trigger. Every monetary figure names its currency, its amount and its rounding. Every scenario that could be mistaken for a posting states what the ledger did instead: **nothing**. The budget currency throughout is USD at 2 decimal places, rounded half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, except where company `NL-01` is named, whose functional currency is EUR at 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01.

The baseline for Scenarios 1 to 7 is company `US-01` with fiscal year 2025 defined as 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 under CAP-003 of [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md), and the three general-ledger accounts Revenue 4000 (`account_type` `income`), Expense 6100 (`account_type` `expense`) and Depreciation Expense 6500 (`account_type` `expense_depreciation`) present and live in the `US-01` chart of accounts.

### BGT-SOD-001 — The Budget Approval Authority, Allocation Completeness and Immutability Contract

The transition from `draft` to `confirmed` is the **only** approval this feature has, and everything downstream depends on it: a `confirmed` budget is the one variance reporting reads and the one threshold evaluation polices, as [FEATURE-001-09 §8.1](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md#81-budget-lifecycle) records. A transition that the plan's own author can make, over a plan that is not yet phased, into a state whose amounts can then be edited, is not an approval — it is a save with a different label. The four parts below settle who may make it, what must be true before it may be made, what may change afterwards, and where the check lives. Every story of this feature reads this contract rather than restating it.

**Part 1 — preparing a budget and approving one are two rights, held by two roles.** Two server groups are delivered, named so that an implementing agent creates two rather than one:

| Group | Held by | What it carries | What it does **not** carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| `group_budget_prepare` | FP&A Analyst | Create a budget, add and edit its lines and dimensions, allocate it across periods, duplicate a prior-year plan, submit the plan for approval | The `draft` to `confirmed` transition, the reset of a `confirmed` plan to `draft`, the close, the reopen, and the threshold and recipient configuration of [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md) |
| `group_budget_approve` | Group Controller | The `draft` to `confirmed` transition, the recorded reset of a `confirmed` plan to `draft`, the close, the reopen, and the threshold and recipient configuration | Authorship of the plan it approves — an approver who edited the plan is still refused as its approver under the self-approval rule below |

Neither group implies the other, and the access-rights matrix asserts that they are **disjoint**: the count of users holding both is **0**, the count of budgets whose `confirmed` transition was made by a holder of `group_budget_prepare` alone is **0**, and the count of budgets approved by the user who authored or last edited the plan is **0** — a self-approval is refused naming the author and the acting user as two values, and no configuration flag, context key or company setting overrides it. The Chief Accountant, the Financial Reporting Manager and the External Auditor hold neither group; the External Auditor's access is read-only over the budget, its lines, its allocation, its state history and its approval record. A C-023 integration principal is granted **at most one** of the two groups, so the separation cannot be circumvented by calling the same method from outside the user interface.

**Part 2 — a plan is approvable only once it is complete, and completeness includes its allocation.** The approval is refused, with the budget persisting in `draft` and no journal entry created, unless every one of the following holds at the moment of the transition:

- Every budget line carries at least one of a general-ledger account or an analytic distribution, so the count of dimensionless lines is **0** (Scenario 3).
- The budget header total exceeds `$0.00 USD` in the budget currency at its rounding increment (Scenario 4).
- Every analytic distribution totals 100.00% inside each plan it addresses, so the count of lines failing the per-plan total is **0** (Scenario 5).
- Every budget line carries a **period allocation** produced by [STORY-001-09-02](./STORY-001-09-02-allocate-budget-periods.md), so the count of lines whose period count is 0 is **0**; and each line's period amounts sum to that line's planned amount at a difference of `$0.00 USD` in the budget currency, so the count of grids whose sum differs from its line total is **0**.
- Every period allocation falls inside the fiscal year of its budget, so the count of periods dated outside 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 on a FY2025 budget is **0**.

A refusal names the budget, the ordinal position of the first line that fails, the check it failed and — where the failure is an absent or unbalanced grid — the line's period count and its period sum against its line total as two amounts. This is why the Group Controller approves the **allocated** plan: an unallocated line would put a `$0.00 USD` period budget in front of threshold evaluation for eleven months of the year and a full-year figure in the twelfth.

**Part 3 — `confirmed` is immutable, and a revision is a new version rather than an edit.** While a budget stands `confirmed`, its fiscal-year window, its line set, its line planned amounts, its analytic distributions and its period allocations are **read-only to every role, including the Group Controller who approved it**: a write is refused naming the budget, its state and the field, and the count of `confirmed` budgets whose line or period amount changed without a recorded reset is **0**. The route to a change is three recorded transitions rather than a fifth state:

1. A holder of `group_budget_approve` **resets** the budget to `draft`, and the reset is recorded with its author, its timestamp and its reason; the plan version that stood approved is retained as **superseded** rather than overwritten.
2. A holder of `group_budget_prepare` edits the plan and re-allocates the periods that remain open, under the closed-period protection of [STORY-001-09-02](./STORY-001-09-02-allocate-budget-periods.md) Edge Case 5.
3. A holder of `group_budget_approve` who is not the editor **re-approves**, which creates a new plan version carrying its own version identifier, its own approver and its own approval timestamp.

Every variance figure, consumption percentage and alert event names the **plan version** it was computed against, so a figure quoted in September and a figure quoted in November are not silently compared across two different plans; the count of superseded versions deleted is **0**; and the count of versions whose approver equals its editor is **0**. `closed` and `cancelled` are terminal for the version they hold: the only route out of `closed` is the recorded reopen to `draft` of §8.1, which is itself a `group_budget_approve` act. Two things are deliberately **outside** this immutability, because neither changes the plan: the **threshold ladder and the recipient list** of [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md), which are configuration held by `group_budget_approve` and stay modifiable while the budget stands `draft` or `confirmed` with every change recorded against its author and timestamp; and the **variance explanation notes**, which are commentary recorded by `group_budget_prepare` against a line without altering a single amount. A change to either is recorded, and neither creates a new plan version, because the figures a variance is computed from have not moved.

**Part 4 — the guard is server-side, and it is proved by denial rather than by a hidden button.** Every refusal above is raised by a model-level check, so it holds identically when the transition or the write is attempted through the form view, through a direct ORM `write` or `action_confirm` call, and through a C-023 web-service call under an integration principal. Three named denial tests prove it: a `group_budget_prepare` principal calling the confirm method directly is refused with the budget still `draft`; a `group_budget_approve` principal writing a line amount on a `confirmed` budget is refused with the amount unchanged; and an approval attempted by the plan's own author is refused with the two identities named. In each case the count of journal entries created in `US-01` is **0**, and the message discloses no stack trace, query text or file-system path (C-019, C-020).

### BHI-001 — The Budget History Load Contract

Comparative variance analysis needs a prior-year plan that predates the system, and the percentage-reuse path of [STORY-001-09-02](./STORY-001-09-02-allocate-budget-periods.md) Scenario 4 needs a prior-year allocated budget to take its shape from. That load is **owned here**, because a loaded budget is a budget record with lines and period shares — the objects this story delivers — and an unowned load is a file boundary with no acceptance criteria behind it. Discovery note D-010 in the parent feature carries the migration *approach*; the requirements below are not deferred to it.

- **The file is checked before it is parsed.** The upload is admitted only against a declared media-type allowlist recorded independently of the file name and a declared maximum file size, both refused before a byte is parsed (C-015). Where the extract is a spreadsheet workbook, it is a ZIP container whose parts are XML and is read with document-type-declaration processing off, external-entity resolution off, bounded entity expansion, and bounded member count, declared member size and compression ratio, so a hostile workbook is refused before a field is read and the count of budgets created by it is **0** (C-016).
- **The column contract is declared, not inferred.** The extract declares its required columns — company code, fiscal year, budget name, account code, analytic distribution, period start date, period end date, planned amount and currency code — each with its type, its permitted range and its maximum length. A row failing any column check is **rejected with its row number stated**, and the count of rows accepted after a silent type coercion is **0**.
- **A file is loaded once.** The load identity is a database unique constraint over the company and the digest of the file content, so a re-submitted file creates nothing new and returns the original load; the count of budgets created twice from one file is **0** (C-028).
- **A file loads whole or not at all.** All rows of one file commit together or none does, and the per-row outcome of every row — accepted, or rejected with its named reason — is recorded whichever way the file resolves.
- **The load is bounded.** A declared maximum row count per file and a declared maximum concurrent load per user are refused before the parse begins, with the ceiling and the submitted magnitude named as two values, and a load above the synchronous threshold is queued and stays cancellable (C-029).
- **The loaded plan enters as `draft`, never as approved.** A loaded budget carries state `draft`, a reference from the same group-wide sequence, and a recorded provenance naming the file, its digest, the loading user and the load timestamp. It reaches `confirmed` only through [BGT-SOD-001](#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract), so the count of budgets confirmed by the loader is **0**.
- **The extract and its load history are governed artifacts.** The uploaded file is retained under C-025 with its declared media type, a non-executing content disposition, a system-fixed filename, an unguessable identifier and company-scoped visibility, for the retention period the group's records-retention policy sets, and is removed only by a governed purge. The load history is append-only under C-026, so a correction is an appended row citing the row it corrects and the count of history rows modified in place is **0**.
- **The load reconciles before it is used.** Before a loaded prior-year plan is used as a comparative or as a percentage-shape source, the sum of its loaded period amounts is reconciled to the sum of its loaded line totals per company and per fiscal year at a difference of `$0.00` in the budget currency, and the count of loaded lines whose account code does not resolve in that company's chart is **0**. The load writes nothing to the ledger: the journal-entry count and the account balances of the loaded company are identical before and after.

### Scenario 2: Budget line held against analytic dimensions from two analytic plans

### Interface, Artifact, Evidence and Resilience Contracts

- [ ] **C-023 — Machine access is a named principal, not a shared key.** Every programmatic creation, read or approval of a budget happens over the JSON web-service surface C-023 governs, under the dedicated named principal **`budget-plan-read`** with a recorded owner, a declared expiry and rotation interval, a revocation effective on its next call, a scope confined to its company and to the models and methods it needs, execution under access rights and record rules rather than `sudo`, a per-principal rate ceiling and a per-call audit line. The principal is granted **at most one** of `group_budget_prepare` and `group_budget_approve`, so the count of budgets it approves end to end alone is **0**, and the completeness, self-approval and immutability refusals of [BGT-SOD-001](#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract) hold on its calls exactly as on a form interaction. The deprecated XML-RPC and JSON-RPC transports carry no criterion, test, demonstration or integration of this story.
- [ ] **C-024 — Portal and shared-link access does not arise here, and the boundary is stated.** No budget, budget line, allocation or approval record is exposed to an unauthenticated or portal-authenticated party; all are internal surfaces reached under an authenticated session scoped by C-014. This row becomes applicable if a budget pack is ever published to a portal or a shared link.
- [ ] **C-025 — The budget-history extract is an artifact with a lifecycle.** The uploaded extract of [BHI-001](#bhi-001--the-budget-history-load-contract) is stored with its declared media type recorded separately from the file name, served with a non-executing content disposition and a system-fixed filename sanitized of path separators, addressed by an unguessable identifier, readable only inside the owning company after an authorization check on the load record, retained for the period the group's records-retention policy sets against the applicable tax and audit requirements, and removed only by a governed purge that records what it purged and honours a legal hold.
- [ ] **C-026 — The approval trail and the load history are evidence, so they are append-only.** Each budget-state transition, each approval with its approver and plan version, each recorded reset with its reason, each threshold-free plan revision and each row outcome of a history load is appended with its acting principal, its timestamp and its outcome, and stores the values as they stood at the event — the header total, the line amounts and the version identifier — rather than re-deriving them from a plan that has since changed. A correction is an appended row citing the row it corrects, the count of rows modified in place is **0**, the retention lock does not cascade onto the budget it describes or the reverse, and the External Auditor exports the whole trail for a company and a fiscal year in one operation.
- [ ] **C-027 — The one long-running path degrades in a declared way.** Budget definition makes no outbound call. The BHI-001 load declares a maximum duration and a per-attempt timeout, retries a failed parse a bounded number of times with exponential backoff and jitter, enters a named terminal state an operator is alerted on when the budget is spent, commits whole or not at all so a crash leaves neither a half-loaded plan nor a load record without its rows, and resumes or abandons an interrupted attempt under C-028 without loading a row twice.
- [ ] **C-028 — Two episode identities are held by database constraints, not by status fields.** The budget-history load is keyed on company and file digest, so a re-submitted file creates nothing new and returns the original load; and the approval episode is keyed on the budget and its plan version, taken under a row lock across the read-decide-write window and revalidated inside the lock immediately before the transition commits, so two simultaneous approvals produce **1** confirmed version rather than two and the count of budgets carrying two live versions is **0**. Neither guard is overridable by the role it constrains, and a stale lock is reclaimed after its declared interval with the abandoned attempt recorded.
- [ ] **C-029 — The load and every listing are bounded.** The extract's row count, file size and concurrent-load count per user each declare a hard maximum refused **before** the parse begins with the ceiling and the submitted magnitude named as two values; a load above the declared synchronous threshold is queued under a per-user quota and a queue-depth ceiling and stays cancellable, with cancellation leaving no partial plan and no downloadable artifact; and the budget and budget-line listings this story exposes are paginated rather than materialized whole.

## Estimation

| Dimension | Rating | Basis |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Effort** | Medium | One budget header with its company, fiscal year, owner, reference and state; budget lines carrying an account, an analytic distribution, an amount and a period range; four confirm-time refusal paths; one duplication path with an audit link; the group-wide reference sequence; and eight acceptance tests plus the negative, isolation and hostile-input tests. The header, the line, the state transition, the distribution carrier, the duplication link and the sequence already exist in this repository; the two model changes and the lock-date integration named in § INVEST Principles Compliance do not, and they are what holds this at Medium rather than Low |
| **Complexity** | Medium | The dimension model is where the difficulty sits. A distribution has to total 100.00% within **each plan it addresses**, which means evaluating the comma-separated key form that expresses a combination across plans, and the attributed amounts have to sum back to the line's planned amount at a `$0.00 USD` difference after rounding at the currency's own increment. Beside that, the fiscal-year boundary and the company lock date each gate a line's period range through a rule of its own, so a refusal names one cause rather than two, and the group-wide sequence has to hold under concurrent creation with no duplicate reference anywhere in the group. None of it requires new infrastructure, and none of it requires a report engine |
| **Uncertainty** | Low | Every field, constraint, sequence and state value this story relies on was read in this repository during discovery, including the two fields that must change and the sequence record whose `company_id` is `False`. The residual unknowns are scoped and named rather than open-ended: whether making `account_id` optional disturbs the existing account-type constraint and the views that assume a present account, and whether DEC-001 confirms a platform version other than 19.0, which would change the analytic-distribution storage assertion of Scenario 2. What the add-on already satisfies is no longer an unknown — it is enumerated in [FEATURE-001-09 §5.2.1](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md#521-criterion-by-criterion-gap-analysis-against-account_budget_management) and confirmed rather than discovered |
| **Story Points** | **5** (Fibonacci: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) | |

Five points reflects the dimension model and the validation surface, **not** reporting work — there is no report, no scheduled action, no alert and no aggregation over posted journal items anywhere in this story. Three points would understate the analytic-distribution arithmetic, the four distinct refusal paths, the duplication path with its audit link and the multi-company isolation proof, which are five separate outcomes each needing its own demonstration. Eight points would overstate a story whose foundation already exists in `account`, `analytic` and the present budgeting add-on, whose two model changes are field-level rather than structural, and whose acceptance needs no posted data at all. The estimate assumes the account and dimension policy is signed off by the Group Controller during the sprint, and it excludes period allocation, the budget-versus-actual comparison and variance analysis with alerting, which are the three sibling stories.

## Notes

**Platform facts, recorded without judgement.** `account_budget` is **absent from `addons/`** in this repository. It is an Odoo Enterprise module, and this Odoo 19.0 Community checkout does not ship it — that is a fact of the environment, **not** a prohibition on using it. The superseded flat-layout backlog forbade the module by name; that blanket restriction is retired here. Which source supplies the budgeting capability — an Odoo Enterprise subscription, or the Odoo Community Association route with `mis_builder` alongside bespoke work — is **DEC-002** in the Epic's open decisions register, owned by the CFO / Finance Director with the Group Controller, and it is confirmed before this story enters development.

**`account_budget_management` is present in this repository, and it is not an Odoo Community Association module.** It lives at `addons/account_budget_management/` in this checkout at version 19.0.1.0.0 under AGPL-3, declaring `depends` of `account` and `analytic`, delivered by an earlier phase of this programme; the similarly purposed `account_budget_oca` in OCA/account-budgeting is a separate code base, assessed for overlap in § OCA Module Compatibility, and neither is the source of the other. It already holds budget records, budget lines, period allocation, a budget-versus-actual report and threshold alerts, and discovery note **D-003** in the Epic credits it with that capability. Its `budget.budget` model already carries the company, the currency, the date range, the `draft` / `confirmed` / `closed` / `cancelled` state selection and a `copied_from_id` link, and its `budget.budget.line` already inherits `analytic.mixin`. This story therefore begins with an assessment of what is already satisfied rather than with a new model: building what exists would split the budget record in two and give a single plan two answers.

**State names reconciled with the model, not with an invented lifecycle.** The budget model declares exactly four states — `draft`, `confirmed`, `closed` and `cancelled` — and [FEATURE-001-09 §8.1](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md#81-budget-lifecycle) is now stated in those four, with the superseded backlog's `Approved`, `Active` and `Revised` mapped onto them rather than added to them. This story delivers the `draft` to `confirmed` transition, which is the one the add-on's `action_confirm` already exposes, together with the authority that may make it and the completeness that must hold first. The Group Controller's approval is an approval of the **allocated** plan, so the grid produced by [STORY-001-09-02](./STORY-001-09-02-allocate-budget-periods.md) is a precondition of the approval rather than a later step; a `confirmed` budget is the one that contributes to threshold evaluation and variance reporting, which is what the add-on's own field help states; and a revision is a recorded reset to `draft` by a holder of `group_budget_approve`, an edit by a holder of `group_budget_prepare` and a re-approval that supersedes the prior plan version rather than a fifth state. Nothing in this story invents a state the model does not carry, and no role in it approves a plan it authored.

**Why the analytic distribution of Scenario 2 uses the combination key form.** A bare 60.00% to a Departments account and 40.00% to a Projects account would leave the Departments plan at 60.00% and the Projects plan at 40.00% — neither at the 100.00% per-plan total the feature requires. Expressing the assignment as `{"123,125": 60.0, "124,125": 40.0}`, the comma-separated key form that `analytic.mixin` supports, splits the line 60.00% / 40.00% across the two Departments accounts while attributing the whole line to the Projects account, so each plan totals 100.00% and the attributed amounts of `$720,000.00 USD` and `$480,000.00 USD` still sum back to `$1,200,000.00 USD`. The exact key values are illustrative identifiers; what the criterion fixes is the shape, the per-plan total and the arithmetic.

**Posted actuals come from journal items, not analytic lines.** The superseded backlog aggregated budget actuals from `account.analytic.line`. In this backlog the primary source is **`account.move.line`**, aggregated by account, by analytic distribution and by date and restricted to entries in the posted state; analytic lines are a secondary read. This story writes neither, and names the distinction so that the structure fixed here matches the structure the comparison in [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) groups by.

**Identifier retirement.** The flat-layout identifier that previously carried budget definition does not survive into this tree, in this file or in any dependency table. `STORY-001-09-01` is the only identifier for this work, and the sibling stories of this feature are `STORY-001-09-02`, `STORY-001-09-03` and `STORY-001-09-04`.

**Governing rules.** No project-specific implementation rules were supplied for this work — the rules input returns "No user rules provided." The authoritative constraints for this file are therefore the Epic's own output requirements and validation gates, §0.7 R-A through R-K with the prior-art constraints of §0.7.2, and this story is held to them: deterministic naming and identifiers, the 4-to-8 criterion bound with its coverage distribution, zero vague qualifiers in the criteria, currency and rounding on every monetary assertion, the explicit "no journal entry" boundary in place of a debits-equal-credits assertion that a plan cannot make, INVEST compliance, demonstrability to the Finance Controller and the Product Owner, a single named finance persona, resolvable relative links, and deterministic account codes, company names and dates. The story template's own guidance is followed except where those gates supersede it: its 3-to-6 scenario range, its `../features/…` parent-link convention, its hardcoded Odoo 18.0 target and its stale rule citation are each superseded, as recorded above.
