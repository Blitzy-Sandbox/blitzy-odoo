## User Story

**As a** FP&A Analyst

**I want** to distribute each budget line's annual amount across the periods of its own fiscal year while the plan stands `draft`, before it is approved — twelve monthly periods, four quarterly periods or one annual period, with the period boundaries resolved from the fiscal-year configuration of the company that holds the budget, and with any rounding residual placed in the final period so the period amounts always sum back to the line total

**So that** budget consumption can be measured period by period while the year is still running, instead of only at year end, giving [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) a period budget to set beside the actual amounts aggregated from posted journal entries whose total debits equal their total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD` (SM-014).

### Business Context

An annual figure cannot be exceeded until 31 December. That is the whole problem: a budget line of `$1,200,000.00 USD` for the year says nothing about whether `$400,000.00 USD` spent by the end of March is ahead of plan or behind it. Allocation is what converts a static annual figure into a time-phased control, and it is the step that makes a monthly or quarterly comparison possible at all.

This story delivers three allocation methods over the calendar published by [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md) under CAP-003 — equal distribution across the periods of the fiscal year, manual entry of a period amount, and reuse of a prior year's percentage shape on a new annual total — together with the one invariant that holds across all three: **the sum of the period amounts equals the annual budget-line total, to the cent, in the budget currency**. Where an equal division does not land on whole cents, the residual is placed in the **final** period rather than spread, so two runs of the same allocation produce the same twelve amounts and a period figure quoted in a management pack is reproducible months later.

**Allocation is planning data, not a posting.** Generating, editing and re-generating a period allocation creates, alters and reverses no journal entry; the general ledger, every account balance and the Trial Balance of the affected company are unchanged by it. That boundary is asserted as an acceptance criterion below, and it is the same boundary [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md) draws for budget definition: a budget column is supplementary management information presented beside a statutory statement, never inside one, as §7.3 of the parent feature records. The actual amounts these periods will be compared against are aggregated from `account.move.line` records belonging to journal entries in the posted state, each of which balances with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `0.00` in the company currency.

The period grid is also what makes the plan approvable. In the budget lifecycle at [FEATURE-001-09 §8.1](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md), the Group Controller approves the **allocated** plan — a plan whose annual total has been phased across the year — and [BGT-SOD-001](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract) Part 2 makes that completeness a precondition the approval is refused without: the count of lines whose period count is 0 must be **0**, and each line's period amounts must sum to its planned amount at a difference of `$0.00 USD`. The grid is therefore built while the plan stands `draft`; the approval itself, its authority and the immutability that follows it belong to [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md), and this story is the completeness gate that plan must pass.

## Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria are authored, inside the 4-to-8 bound the Epic sets in [§5.3](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#53-feature-and-story-decomposition-guidelines), and they carry the mandated coverage distribution: Scenarios 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8 are the valid-input cases (monthly allocation, the rounding residual, quarterly allocation, reuse of a prior year's percentage shape, and an accepted edit to a period that lies after the lock date), Scenario 5 is the invalid and incomplete-input case, Scenario 6 is the error-handling case, and Scenario 7 is the accounting edge case. Every scenario states a single trigger; where a criterion would otherwise carry a second trigger it is either split into its own scenario or asserted by a named test in § Test Requirements.

Every monetary figure names its currency, its amount and its rounding. Every scenario that could be mistaken for a posting states what the ledger did instead: **nothing**. The budget currency throughout is USD, held to 2 decimal places and rounded half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, except where company `NL-01` or a EUR budget is named, whose amounts are held to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01.

The baseline for all eight scenarios is company **`US-01`**, the group's United States operating entity whose functional currency is USD, holding the budget "FY2025 Operating Budget" confirmed under [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md) for fiscal year 2025. Fiscal year 2025 is defined for `US-01` as **2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31** under CAP-003 of [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md), and its four quarters resolve from that configuration as **Q1 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31**, **Q2 2025-04-01 to 2025-06-30**, **Q3 2025-07-01 to 2025-09-30** and **Q4 2025-10-01 to 2025-12-31**. The budget carries the three lines fixed by the predecessor story: Revenue 4000 at `$4,800,000.00 USD`, Expense 6100 at `$1,200,000.00 USD` and Depreciation Expense 6500 at `$240,000.00 USD`.

**The shape of the Depreciation Expense 6500 grid, stated here because the next story reports against it.** That line is allocated by **manual entry** rather than by equal distribution, because the asset it plans for was planned to enter service in April 2025: `$0.00 USD` in each of January, February and March 2025, `$26,666.67 USD` in each of the eight periods from April 2025 through November 2025 — `$213,333.36 USD` across those eight — and `$26,666.64 USD` in December 2025, being the line total of `$240,000.00 USD` less the `$213,333.36 USD` already placed, so the residual of `$0.03 USD` against the nominal `$26,666.67 USD` sits in the final period alone. The twelve amounts sum to `$240,000.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, which is what lets the Q1 2025 budget for that line read `$0.00 USD` in [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) **without** any rephasing of the annual total: a `$0.00 USD` quarter is a shape, not a missing plan.

### ALC-001 — The Allocation Grid Identity, Quantized Share and Versioning Contract

A period grid is the axis every later figure is read along, so two grids for one line, a share held as an unrounded float, or a grid edited under an approved plan each turn one question into two answers. The three parts below settle the identity of a grid, the precision of a share, and when a grid may change at all. They are read by [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) and [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md) rather than restated there.

**Part 1 — one grid per line per version, enforced by the database.** A period allocation's identity is the tuple **company, budget line, period start date, period end date and grid version**, held by a **database unique constraint** rather than by a status field or an in-application check. Two simultaneous generations of the same line produce **1** grid rather than two overlapping sets; the generating routine takes a row lock on the budget line (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) across the read-decide-write window and revalidates inside the lock, immediately before the commit, that the line's planned amount and existing period count are still what it decided on — where either moved, the generation aborts with a named error and writes **0** periods. A regeneration over an existing grid is refused as Scenario 3 states rather than silently replacing it, so the count of lines holding two grids of the same version is **0** and the count of lines holding a 12-period and a 4-period grid at once is **0**. Neither the uniqueness constraint nor the lock is overridable by the FP&A Analyst whose action it constrains, and a lock left by a crashed worker is reclaimed after a declared stale interval with the abandoned attempt recorded (C-028).

**Part 2 — a share is a quantized decimal, and an amount is derived from it once.** An allocation share is stored as a decimal **quantized to exactly 4 decimal places** — 10.0000% and 8.1818% in Scenario 4, never a binary float carried at full width — and a period amount is derived from the share and the line total in **one** rounding step to the budget currency's decimal places at its rounding increment, never by re-rounding an already-rounded intermediate. The residual is the line total less the sum of the derived amounts and is placed in the final period alone, so the grid is a pure function of the line total, the period count, the stored shares and the currency's rounding increment: two runs of the same method on the same total return the identical amounts, the count of periods whose amount differs from the rounded product of its own share and the line total is at most 1, and that period is the last. The sum invariant is a model constraint, so a grid whose period amounts do not sum to the line's planned amount within one unit of the currency's rounding increment **cannot be stored** — which is why Scenario 5 offers three resolutions and no fourth, and why "retain the entered amounts and accept the difference" is not a reachable state anywhere in this story.

**Part 3 — a grid may be edited while its plan is `draft`, and versioned while it is not.** Generation, regeneration, manual override and percentage reuse are acts on a plan standing in state **`draft`**, before the approval that [BGT-SOD-001](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract) governs. Once the Group Controller has approved the plan, the grid of that plan version is **read-only to every role**: a write to a period amount on a `confirmed` budget is refused naming the budget, its state and the period, and the count of `confirmed` budgets whose period amount changed without a recorded reset is **0**. A mid-year revision is therefore the three recorded transitions of BGT-SOD-001 Part 3 — a reset to `draft` by a holder of `group_budget_approve`, the re-allocation of the periods that remain open by a holder of `group_budget_prepare`, and a re-approval by a third party — each producing a **new grid version** whose predecessor is retained as superseded rather than overwritten. Every period figure quoted downstream names the grid version it came from, the count of superseded grid versions deleted is **0**, and a period closed by the company lock date keeps the amount its own reported variance was measured against, as Scenario 7 and Edge Case 5 assert.

### Scenario 1: Monthly allocation of an annual line into twelve periods that sum to the line total, with no journal entry created

- **Given** the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` stands **`draft`** for fiscal year 2025 defined as 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 — the state in which a grid is built, because [BGT-SOD-001](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract) Part 2 makes a complete allocation the precondition of the approval rather than a step after it — its Expense 6100 line carries a planned amount of `$1,200,000.00 USD` held to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, and that line carries no period allocation
- **When** the FP&A Analyst applies the monthly allocation method to the Expense 6100 line of that budget
- **Then** twelve period allocations are produced, one per calendar month of fiscal year 2025, whose boundaries are taken from the `US-01` fiscal-year configuration — 2025-01-01 to 2025-01-31, 2025-02-01 to 2025-02-28, 2025-03-01 to 2025-03-31, 2025-04-01 to 2025-04-30, 2025-05-01 to 2025-05-31, 2025-06-01 to 2025-06-30, 2025-07-01 to 2025-07-31, 2025-08-01 to 2025-08-31, 2025-09-01 to 2025-09-30, 2025-10-01 to 2025-10-31, 2025-11-01 to 2025-11-30 and 2025-12-01 to 2025-12-31; each period holds `$100,000.00 USD` rounded to 2 decimal places half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the sum of the twelve amounts is reported as `$1,200,000.00 USD`, equal to the line's planned amount at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; the count of period allocations dated outside 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 is 0; every period allocation is held against company `US-01` and against the Expense 6100 line alone; and **no journal entry is created, altered or reversed** — the journal-entry count of `US-01` for 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31, the balance of account 6100, and the Trial Balance of `US-01` for that range reporting total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, are each identical before and after the allocation

### Scenario 2: Rounding residual placed in the final period so the twelve amounts still sum to the line total

- **Given** the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` carries a Revenue 4000 line whose planned amount is `$1,000,000.00 USD` held to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, an amount that does not divide into twelve whole cents
- **When** the FP&A Analyst applies the monthly allocation method to the Revenue 4000 line of that budget
- **Then** the eleven periods from January 2025 through November 2025 each hold `$83,333.33 USD` and the December 2025 period holds `$83,333.37 USD`, every amount rounded to 2 decimal places half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the sum of the twelve amounts is reported as `$1,000,000.00 USD`, equal to the line's planned amount at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; the residual of `$0.04 USD` sits in the December 2025 period alone and is not spread across the twelve periods, so the count of periods differing from the nominal `$83,333.33 USD` is 1; and no journal entry is created by the allocation, the balance of account 4000 in `US-01` being identical before and after.

### Scenario 3: Quarterly allocation resolving its four period boundaries from the company fiscal-year configuration

- **Given** the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` stands **`draft`** for fiscal year 2025 defined as 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31, its Expense 6100 line carries a planned amount of `$1,200,000.00 USD` held to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, and that line **carries no period allocation** — either because none has been generated, or because an existing grid was removed in the explicit step described below
- **When** the FP&A Analyst applies the quarterly allocation method to the Expense 6100 line of that budget
- **Then** four period allocations are produced whose boundaries resolve from the `US-01` fiscal-year configuration as Q1 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31, Q2 2025-04-01 to 2025-06-30, Q3 2025-07-01 to 2025-09-30 and Q4 2025-10-01 to 2025-12-31, rather than from a calendar assumed by the allocation itself; each period holds `$300,000.00 USD` rounded to 2 decimal places half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the sum of the four amounts is reported as `$1,200,000.00 USD`, equal to the line's planned amount at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; a generation run against a line that **already** holds period allocations is refused rather than replacing them silently — the refusal names the budget line and the count of allocations it already holds, and requires those allocations to be removed before a grid is generated again — so moving from a twelve-period grid to this four-period one is a two-step the analyst performs deliberately: the twelve are removed in a step recorded with its author and its timestamp, and the four are then generated, leaving the period count at 4 and the sum at `$1,200,000.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, with no state in between holding sixteen periods; the four quarter ranges are contiguous and together cover 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 with no gap and no overlap; and no journal entry is created by the allocation

### Scenario 4: Prior-year percentage shape reused on a new annual total, with the residual re-rounded into the final period

- **Given** company `US-01` holds the budget "FY2024 Operating Budget" in state `confirmed` for fiscal year 2024, whose Expense 6100 line of `$1,100,000.00 USD` was allocated monthly as `$110,000.00 USD` in January 2024 and `$90,000.00 USD` in each of the eleven months from February 2024 through December 2024, giving stored period shares of 10.0000% for January 2024 and 8.1818% for each later month, and company `US-01` also holds the "FY2025 Operating Budget" in state `draft` whose Expense 6100 line carries a planned amount of `$1,200,000.00 USD` held to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the FP&A Analyst reuses the period shape of the FY2024 Expense 6100 line on the FY2025 Expense 6100 line of company `US-01`
- **Then** the **percentage shares are what is copied, and the amounts are recomputed** on the new annual total: the January 2025 period holds 10.0000% of `$1,200,000.00 USD`, which is `$120,000.00 USD`; each of the ten periods from February 2025 through November 2025 holds 8.1818% of `$1,200,000.00 USD`, which is `$98,181.60 USD`, giving `$981,816.00 USD` across those ten periods and `$1,101,816.00 USD` across the first eleven; the December 2025 period holds `$98,184.00 USD`, being the line total of `$1,200,000.00 USD` less the `$1,101,816.00 USD` already placed, so the residual of `$2.40 USD` sits in the final period; every amount is rounded to 2 decimal places half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 and the twelve sum to `$1,200,000.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; because a copied shape is deliberately unequal, the invariant asserted here is **not** that one period differs from the others — January and December both do — but that **December 2025 is the only period whose amount differs from the rounded product of its own share and the line total**, its `$98,184.00 USD` standing against the `$98,181.60 USD` that 8.1818% of `$1,200,000.00 USD` rounds to; the twelve period boundaries are the fiscal year 2025 months of company `US-01` and not the FY2024 dates they were copied from; the FY2024 budget is untouched, its Expense 6100 line still holding `$1,100,000.00 USD` with its January 2024 period at `$110,000.00 USD`, a difference of `$0.00 USD` against its value before the reuse; the FY2025 line records the FY2024 line it took its shape from so the path from copy to source is readable by the External Auditor without a data request; and no journal entry is created in `US-01` by the reuse

### Scenario 5: Manual override that breaks the period sum is warned, offered three named resolutions and written to the audit trail

- **Given** the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` stands **`draft`**, so its grid is editable under [ALC-001](#alc-001--the-allocation-grid-identity-quantized-share-and-versioning-contract) Part 3 and the period-sum check is the only rule in play, and its Expense 6100 line holds twelve monthly period allocations of `$100,000.00 USD` each, summing to the line's planned amount of `$1,200,000.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, every amount held to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01
- **When** the FP&A Analyst overrides the March 2025 period allocation of that line to `$150,000.00 USD`
- **Then** the `$150,000.00 USD` is carried as the **proposed** amount for the March 2025 period with its method marked as manual entry — carried in the unsaved edit rather than committed to the grid, which is what lets the analyst see the consequence before choosing between the three resolutions — and a warning names company `US-01` whose books the plan belongs to, the proposed period sum of `$1,250,000.00 USD`, the line total of `$1,200,000.00 USD` and the difference between them of `$50,000.00 USD`, each stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; three resolutions are offered and no further one is required — **raise the annual line total to `$1,250,000.00 USD`**, which restores the sum by moving the total; **pro-rate the `$50,000.00 USD` difference across the nine periods from April 2025 through December 2025**, which reduces eight of them by `$5,555.56 USD` to `$94,444.44 USD` each and reduces December 2025 by the residual `$5,555.52 USD` to `$94,444.48 USD`, restoring a period sum of `$1,200,000.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; or **discard the override and restore the March 2025 period to `$100,000.00 USD`**, which restores the sum by abandoning the change; **each of the three restores a period sum equal to the line total at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, and none of them leaves a mismatch standing** — the sum invariant is enforced by a model constraint that refuses to store a grid whose period amounts do not sum to the line's planned amount within one unit of the currency's rounding increment, so "retain the entered amounts and accept the difference" is not a reachable state and is not offered; until one of the three is applied no period amount is written, so the count of committed grid states whose period sum differs from the line total is **0**; the committed change is written to the allocation audit trail with the previous amount of `$100,000.00 USD`, the new amount, the timestamp, the acting user and the reason recorded against it; and no journal entry is created in `US-01` by the override or by any of the three resolutions

### Scenario 6: Allocation period outside the fiscal year of its budget is rejected and leaves the existing grid unchanged

- **Given** fiscal year 2025 of company `US-01` runs 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31, and the Expense 6100 line of the "FY2025 Operating Budget" holds twelve monthly period allocations of `$100,000.00 USD` each, every amount held to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, summing to `$1,200,000.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`
- **When** the FP&A Analyst enters an allocation period of 2026-01-01 to 2026-01-31 on that FY2025 Expense 6100 line

## Edge Cases

| # | Edge Case | Expected Handling |
|---|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Zero-amount line, and a line with no allocation method chosen.** The Expense 6100 line of company `US-01` carries a planned amount of `$0.00 USD`; or a line carries an amount but no allocation method has been applied to it | A `$0.00 USD` line allocates into twelve monthly periods each holding `$0.00 USD`, stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, and the twelve sum to `$0.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`. The division is by period count and never by the amount, so a `$0.00 USD` total produces no division-by-zero and no error; the residual is `$0.00 USD` and the final period holds `$0.00 USD` like every other. A period carrying no allocation reads `$0.00 USD` rather than an empty value, so a reader is not left to infer whether the period was planned at zero or never planned. Where **no** allocation method has been applied, the line stays unallocated with a period count of 0 and its annual total intact: the line is reported as unallocated rather than silently defaulted to equal distribution, because a default would put an amount into the Group Controller's approval that no one chose. An unallocated line is what the downstream comparison treats as an annual-only budget, and it is refused for approval of the allocated plan. No journal entry arises in any branch |
| 2 | **Fiscal-period lock closes a period whose allocation is being changed.** The journal-entry lock date of company `US-01` stands at 2025-03-31 and the February 2025 allocation of the Expense 6100 line of a `draft` plan is edited | The edit is blocked with a message naming company `US-01`, the lock date of 2025-03-31 and the closed range 2025-02-01 to 2025-02-28, and the February 2025 amount stays at `$100,000.00 USD` at 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, as Scenario 7 asserts. Periods from 2025-04-01 onward stay editable, which Scenario 8 asserts, so the plan for the open part of the year is still workable. The refusal is the fiscal-calendar control acting on planning data rather than a ledger rule: no journal entry is created, altered or reversed, and the balance of account 6100 for the closed range is identical before and after. Where the group budget policy requires a change to a closed period's plan — a prior-period reclassification agreed with the auditor — the exception is raised to the Group Controller, who releases the lock date under CAP-005 of [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md) with a recorded expiry, rather than the allocation bypassing the control. A closed period's amount is what a closed period's variance was measured against, which is why it is protected rather than re-derived |
| 3 | **Multi-currency rounding, and a currency whose rounding increment is not 0.01.** A budget line of `€1,000,000.00 EUR` is held in company `NL-01`, the Netherlands operating company whose functional currency is EUR, while the group reporting currency of company `US-01` is USD; and a line of `¥1,000,000 JPY` is held in a company whose functional currency is JPY | **The invariant is asserted in the budget's own currency.** The EUR line allocates into eleven monthly periods of `€83,333.33 EUR` and a December 2025 period of `€83,333.37 EUR`, each rounded to 2 decimal places half-up at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01 and summing to `€1,000,000.00 EUR` at a difference of `€0.00 EUR`; the residual of `€0.04 EUR` sits in the final period. A group presentation in USD translates each period at the rate basis the group budget policy names — the closing rate of the last day of the fiscal year for a balance-style roll-up, the period-average rate for a flow-style roll-up — and each translated figure is rounded to 2 decimal places half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, so at a period-average rate of 1.1000 USD per EUR the eleven periods present as `$91,666.66 USD` each and December 2025 as `$91,666.71 USD`, a translated total of `$1,099,999.97 USD` against the `$1,100,000.00 USD` that translating the line total directly produces. That gap of `$0.03 USD` is disclosed in the group view as a translation difference and is **never pushed back onto the EUR periods**, because the EUR amounts are the plan `NL-01` will be measured against. Each currency rounds at its own increment rather than one being re-rounded from the other, and the rate with its basis is retained beside the translated figure for the External Auditor. The JPY line, whose currency carries 0 decimal places at a rounding increment of 1, allocates into eleven periods of `¥83,333 JPY` and a final period of `¥83,337 JPY` summing to `¥1,000,000 JPY` at a difference of `¥0 JPY` — the same residual rule at a different precision, never a 2-decimal assumption applied to a zero-decimal currency |
| 4 | **The calendar decides the period boundaries — a range outside the fiscal year, and February in a leap year.** An allocation period of 2026-01-01 to 2026-01-31 is entered on a FY2025 line; and the same monthly allocation is run for fiscal year 2024, whose February holds 29 days | Both cases are answered by taking the boundaries from the company fiscal-year configuration rather than from an assumption inside the allocation. A period outside the fiscal year is rejected with a message naming the rejected range 2026-01-01 to 2026-01-31 and the boundary 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31, and no period allocation is created, as Scenario 6 asserts, so a plan cannot straddle two fiscal years and be counted in both. **Leap-year handling is a date question, not an amount question:** the February 2024 period is generated as 2024-02-01 to 2024-02-29 and the February 2025 period as 2025-02-01 to 2025-02-28, while the equal-distribution amount is unchanged at `$100,000.00 USD` in both years, because equal distribution divides the line total by the **period count** of 12 and not by the day count of 365 or 366. A fiscal year ending on 28 February generates its final period as 2025-02-01 to 2025-02-28 in a common year and 2024-02-01 to 2024-02-29 in a leap year, and the residual still lands in that final period. Should the group budget policy later adopt a day-weighted method, the day count enters the arithmetic explicitly and the leap day changes the February amount; that method is not in scope here and the difference is recorded so the two are never confused |
| 5 | **Mid-year revision that re-allocates only the periods that remain open.** In August 2025 the Group Controller approves a revision raising the Expense 6100 line of company `US-01` from `$1,200,000.00 USD` to `$1,500,000.00 USD`, while the lock date of `US-01` stands at 2025-06-30 | The six periods from January 2025 through June 2025 are closed by the lock date and keep their original amounts of `$100,000.00 USD` each, totalling `$600,000.00 USD` at 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 — a closed period's plan is what its reported variance was measured against and is not restated. The revision is therefore spread over the six periods that remain open, July 2025 through December 2025, which together must carry `$1,500,000.00 USD` less the `$600,000.00 USD` already fixed, that is `$900,000.00 USD`, giving `$150,000.00 USD` per open period and summing with the six closed periods to `$1,500,000.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`. Where the open-period division leaves a remainder it lands in December 2025, the final period, under the same rule as Scenario 2. The revision is recorded in the allocation audit trail with the previous amount, the new amount, the timestamp, the acting user and the reason. It reaches the grid through the **three recorded transitions** of [BGT-SOD-001](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract) Part 3 rather than through a `Revised` state, which the lifecycle at [FEATURE-001-09 §8.1](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md#81-budget-lifecycle) does not carry: the Group Controller resets the plan to `draft` and the reset is recorded with its author, timestamp and reason; the FP&A Analyst re-allocates the six open periods; and a re-approval by a party other than the editor produces a **new grid version** whose predecessor is retained as superseded. Every period figure quoted afterwards names the grid version it came from, so a reader can see which plan a given period was measured against and the count of superseded versions deleted is 0. No journal entry is created by the revision, and the balances of account 6100 in `US-01` are identical before and after |

- [ ] **C-004 — Odoo Community Association ecosystem compatibility.** Whichever edition path is confirmed, the period allocation structure stays consumable by Odoo Community Association add-ons, so that `mis_builder` remains an option for rendering a period-phased plan beside actuals and the present `account_budget_management` add-on remains usable against the same periods.
- [ ] **C-005, C-006 — Coding standards and static analysis.** Python follows Odoo and Odoo Community Association module guidelines including PEP 8, and static analysis passes with the repository's configured tooling, whose lint configuration is declared in `ruff.toml` at the repository root.
- [ ] **C-009 — Numeric accounting assertions.** The arithmetic of this story is asserted as amounts rather than inspected by eye: the period sum equals the budget-line total at a `0.00` difference in the budget currency for every method and every granularity, the residual is asserted to be in the final period and nowhere else, and each assertion is repeated for a EUR budget at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01 and for a zero-decimal currency at its own precision.
- [ ] **C-012 — Build on the existing models.** Period boundaries are derived from the fiscal-year configuration held on `res.company`, and decimal precision and rounding increments from `res.currency`, rather than from a calendar or a precision table maintained beside them. A second source of either would let a budget period and a report date range disagree.
- [ ] **C-013 — Analytic layer rather than a parallel one.** A period allocation inherits the analytic distribution of the budget line it belongs to and introduces no dimension of its own, so a period plan and the posted amounts attributed to it are grouped by the same analytic accounts and analytic plans.
- [ ] **C-014, D-007 — Company isolation and role separation.** A period allocation belongs to the company that holds its budget; record rules keep a persona's reach inside its allowed companies; and the role that allocates a plan is kept apart from the role that approves the allocated plan.
- [ ] **C-019, C-020, C-022 — Input validation and hostile-input tests.** Every value this story accepts — period start date, period end date, period amount, allocation share and the reason text recorded against a change — is validated before use; a rejected value returns an error naming the field and the check that failed and discloses no stack trace, query text, file-system path or credential; and at least one acceptance test submits a malformed date, an out-of-range amount, a negative amount and an over-long reason text and asserts the rejection with no journal entry created and the service still available.
- [ ] **C-018 — Rendered text.** The reason text recorded against an allocation change, and the budget and account names shown beside a period, are context-encoded before being rendered into a view, a PDF or an XLSX export, and are rendered as escaped text rather than raw markup.
- [ ] **C-017 — Export neutralization, applied by cell type.** Where a period grid is exported to CSV or XLSX, a cell written from an **untrusted text** value — a partner or account name, a reference, a label, a memo or an echoed filter value — whose first character is `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return is escaped or prefixed so the spreadsheet treats it as text, while a cell written from a **numeric, date or boolean** value keeps its type and is never coerced to text, so a negative amount stays a negative number and the exported figures still add up (C-017) — so an exported plan cannot execute in the recipient's spreadsheet and a negative allocation still sums to the annual total inside the file.
- [ ] **C-007 — Minimum 80% test coverage** for the functionality this story delivers, reported by the repository's coverage tooling.

### Interface, Artifact, Evidence and Resilience Contracts

- [ ] **C-023 — Machine access is a named principal, not a shared key.** Every programmatic generation, override, reuse or read of a period grid happens over the JSON web-service surface C-023 governs, under the dedicated named principal **`budget-allocation-write`** with a recorded owner, a declared expiry and rotation interval, a revocation effective on its next call, a scope confined to its company and to the models and methods it needs, execution under access rights and record rules rather than `sudo`, a per-principal rate ceiling and a per-call audit line. The principal holds `group_budget_prepare` at most and never `group_budget_approve`, so the count of plans it can allocate **and** approve is **0**; the sum invariant, the lock-date refusal, the out-of-year refusal and the read-only enforcement over a `confirmed` version hold identically on its calls; and the deprecated XML-RPC and JSON-RPC transports carry no criterion, test or integration of this story.
- [ ] **C-024 — Portal and shared-link access does not arise here, and the boundary is stated.** No period grid, allocation share or audit-trail row is exposed to an unauthenticated or portal-authenticated party; all are internal surfaces reached under an authenticated session scoped by C-014. This row becomes applicable if a phased plan is ever published to a portal or a shared link.
- [ ] **C-025 — An exported grid is an artifact with a lifecycle.** A PDF or XLSX export of a period grid is stored with its declared media type recorded separately from the file name, served with a non-executing content disposition and a system-fixed filename, addressed by an unguessable identifier, readable only inside the owning company after an authorization check on the budget it belongs to, retained for the declared close-evidence period and removed only by a governed purge. It is published atomically, so an interrupted export leaves no partial grid downloadable.
- [ ] **C-026 — The allocation audit trail is evidence, so it is append-only.** Every period-amount change, every resolution applied, every regeneration, every refusal and every recorded reset is appended with its acting principal, its timestamp, its reason, the grid version it belongs to and the **values as they stood at the event** — the previous amount and the new amount — rather than a reference that would re-derive them from a grid that has since changed. A row is never edited or removed in place, so the count of trail rows modified in place is **0**; a correction is an appended row citing the row it corrects; the retention lock does not cascade onto the budget it describes or the reverse; and the External Auditor exports the whole trail for a company and a fiscal year in one operation.
- [ ] **C-027 — The one bulk path degrades in a declared way.** Allocation makes no outbound call. A bulk generation across 500 budget lines declares a maximum duration and a per-attempt timeout, retries a failed unit a bounded number of times with exponential backoff and jitter, enters a named terminal state an operator is alerted on when the budget is spent, writes each line's grid whole or not at all so a crash leaves no line holding a partial grid, and resumes or abandons an interrupted attempt under C-028 without writing a period twice.
- [ ] **C-028 — The grid identity is a database constraint, not a status field.** The episode identity over company, budget line, period start date, period end date and grid version is held by a **database unique constraint**, with the budget-line row taken under a row lock across the read-decide-write window and revalidated inside the lock immediately before the commit, as [ALC-001](#alc-001--the-allocation-grid-identity-quantized-share-and-versioning-contract) Part 1 states: two simultaneous generations produce **1** grid, two simultaneous edits to one period produce one applied change and one reported conflict, a stale lock is reclaimed after its declared interval with the abandoned attempt recorded, and no part of the guard is overridable by the FP&A Analyst it constrains.
- [ ] **C-029 — Generation, listing and export are bounded.** The period count per line, the line count per bulk generation and the produced file size each declare a hard maximum refused **before** the generation or the export begins, with the ceiling and the requested magnitude named as two values; a request above the declared synchronous threshold is queued under a per-user quota and a queue-depth ceiling and stays cancellable, cancellation releasing its resources and leaving no partial grid and no downloadable file; and a grid listing is paginated rather than materialized whole.

### Accounting Standards Compliance

- [ ] **A period allocation is management information, not a statutory amount.** It is presented beside the actual column of a management report and is excluded from the statutory Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss that FEATURE-001-07 publishes, consistent with IAS 1 presentation of financial statements, under which supplementary information does not alter a statutory caption or its amount. This is why no path in this story posts to the ledger.
- [ ] **The period axis matches the reporting calendar.** The monthly and quarterly boundaries generated here are the ranges the management pack and the Profit & Loss date parameters use, so a budget column and an actual column carrying the same period label cover the identical range; this is the alignment ORD-004 requires when the figures are handed to FEATURE-001-07.
- [ ] **Deterministic phasing is what makes a period variance auditable.** Because the residual is placed in the final period by rule rather than by chance, a period budget quoted in a management pack can be reproduced from the line total months later; the retained allocation audit trail then shows every change made to it, which is the monitoring-activity evidence tested under the COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework and section 404 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.
- [ ] **Variance convention inherited, not defined here.** Absolute variance is stated as an amount in the budget currency and percentage variance to 2 decimal places, with favourable and unfavourable signing taken from the account type; the convention follows Institute of Management Accountants practice and is applied in [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md). This story fixes only the period axis that signing will be computed over.

## Estimation

| Dimension | Rating | Basis |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Effort** | Medium | One period-generation routine driven by the company fiscal-year configuration at three granularities; one residual rule; one sum invariant; three allocation methods including reuse of a prior year's percentage shape; one override warning with three resolutions; one audit-trail record per change; two refusal paths; and eight acceptance tests plus the arithmetic, boundary, idempotence, concurrency, isolation and hostile-input tests. Volume is modest because a period allocation carries no account and no dimension of its own — it inherits both from the line it belongs to |
| **Complexity** | Medium | The difficulty is **arithmetic determinism, not volume**. Three things have to hold together at once: each period rounds at its own currency's increment, the whole residual lands in the final period rather than being spread or accumulated through a second rounding step, and the period sum still equals the line total at a `0.00` difference in the budget currency — for 12 periods, for 4 periods, for a 2-decimal currency, for a zero-decimal currency, and again after a copied percentage shape has been re-applied to a different total. Beside that sits the override path: a warning that quantifies the break, three resolutions of which the pro-rate branch must itself absorb a residual over the periods that remain open, and an audit trail that survives a re-generation. Neither the period boundaries nor the rounding primitives need new infrastructure — both already exist in `account` and `base` — and there is no report engine and no aggregation over posted journal items anywhere in this story |
| **Uncertainty** | Low | The fiscal-year and lock-date fields, the currency precision and rounding fields, the rounding helpers and an existing period-allocation model with an equal-distribution generator and a sum check were all read in this repository during discovery. The residual unknowns are scoped and named rather than open-ended: how much of the story the present `account_budget_management` add-on already satisfies, which is the D-003 assessment carried as a sub-task; whether DEC-001 confirms a platform version other than 19.0, which would change the fiscal-date and rounding helpers relied on; and whether the group budget policy adds a day-weighted method, which Edge Case 4 records as out of scope here |
| **Story Points** | **5** (Fibonacci: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) | |

Five points reflects the rounding-residual determinism and the override-and-audit path, **not** the amount of work in the grid itself. Three points would understate five separate outcomes that each need their own proof: the residual rule holding across two period counts and three currency precisions, the sum invariant surviving a manual override, the three resolutions with a pro-rate branch that carries its own residual, the two calendar refusals, and the audit trail that makes a change attributable. Eight points would overstate a story whose period boundaries, rounding helpers and existing period model are all already present in this repository, and whose acceptance needs no posted data at all. The estimate assumes the residual-placement policy and the resolution order are signed off by the Group Controller during the sprint, and it excludes the budget-versus-actual comparison, variance signing and threshold alerting, which are the two later stories of this feature.

### Accounting Reconciliation Gate

This gate is the accounting contract of the story. Each item is asserted as an amount, at the named currency's decimal places and rounded half-up at its rounding increment, in the currency named.

- [ ] **The sum of the period allocations equals the annual budget-line total to the cent.** For every allocated line, in every method and at every granularity, the arithmetic sum of its period amounts equals the line's planned amount at a difference of `$0.00 USD` — twelve amounts of `$100,000.00 USD` against `$1,200,000.00 USD`; eleven amounts of `$83,333.33 USD` plus `$83,333.37 USD` against `$1,000,000.00 USD`; four amounts of `$300,000.00 USD` against `$1,200,000.00 USD`; and the reused-shape set of `$120,000.00 USD`, ten of `$98,181.60 USD` and `$98,184.00 USD` against `$1,200,000.00 USD`. The same assertion holds at a difference of `€0.00 EUR` for a EUR budget and `¥0 JPY` for a zero-decimal currency.
- [ ] **The rounding residual is in the final period and nowhere else, stated per method rather than as one rule.** Under **equal distribution** every period holds the nominal per-period amount except the last period of the fiscal year, so the count of periods whose amount differs from the nominal amount is at most 1 and that period is the last: the residual is `$0.04 USD` in Scenario 2, where eleven periods hold `$83,333.33 USD` and December 2025 holds `$83,333.37 USD`. Under **percentage reuse** and **manual entry** the periods are deliberately unequal, so "one period differs" is not the invariant and is not asserted; the invariant is that only the **last** period differs from the rounded product of its own share and the line total, and that difference is the residual: `$2.40 USD` in Scenario 4, where December 2025 holds `$98,184.00 USD` against the `$98,181.60 USD` its 8.1818% share rounds to, and `$0.03 USD` in the Depreciation Expense 6500 grid, where December 2025 holds `$26,666.64 USD` against a nominal `$26,666.67 USD`. In every method the twelve amounts sum to the line total at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, and a re-run of the same method on the same total reproduces the identical amounts.
- [ ] **Allocation writes nothing to the ledger.** Generating, re-generating, overriding, resolving, refusing, reusing and revising a period allocation each leave the journal-entry count of company `US-01` and the balances of Revenue 4000, Expense 6100 and Depreciation Expense 6500 identical before and after, and no code path in this story creates, alters or reverses an `account.move` or an `account.move.line`.
- [ ] **Trial-balance totals are unchanged.** The Trial Balance of company `US-01` for 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31, run before and after the whole walkthrough, reports the same figures on both runs, with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`.
- [ ] **Debits equal credits on the postings these periods will be measured against.** The actual amounts each period is later compared with are aggregated only from `account.move.line` records whose journal entry is in the posted state, and every such entry balances with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `0.00` in the company currency; entries in draft or cancelled state contribute `0.00`. No entry is created by this story to satisfy that condition — it is a property of the ledger the downstream comparison reads, asserted where that comparison is built in [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md).
- [ ] **Every period falls inside the fiscal year of its budget and inside one company's books.** The count of period allocations dated outside 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 on a FY2025 budget of company `US-01` is 0; the twelve monthly ranges and the four quarterly ranges are each contiguous over that fiscal year with no gap and no overlap; and every period allocation is held against company `US-01` alone.
- [ ] **Tax amounts are out of this story's scope and stay out.** No period allocation records a tax code, a base amount or a tax amount, and no tax control-account balance moves as a result of this story; where a tax expense account is budgeted, its periods are amounts against an expense account and the tax determination that populates it remains the work of FEATURE-001-05, whose tax code, base amount and tax amount are recorded separately there.
- [ ] **Report lines will tie to the sub-ledger.** The period ranges generated here are the ranges the actual column is aggregated over downstream, so a period budget and the journal items reported against it cover the identical span and a budget-versus-actual line ties to its sub-ledger detail at a difference of `$0.00 USD` when [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) is accepted.

## Notes

**Platform facts, recorded without judgement.** `account_budget` is **absent from `addons/`** in this repository. It is an Odoo Enterprise module, and this Odoo 19.0 Community checkout does not ship it — that is a fact of the environment, **not** a prohibition on using it. The superseded flat-layout backlog forbade the module by name and listed it as a specifically prohibited dependency; that blanket restriction is retired here. Which source supplies the budgeting capability — an Odoo Enterprise subscription, or the Odoo Community Association route with `mis_builder` and bespoke work alongside it — is **DEC-002** in the Epic's open decisions register, owned by the CFO / Finance Director with the Group Controller, and it is confirmed before this story enters development. The platform version is the separate decision **DEC-001**, and the mismatch between the programme request's Odoo 17, this repository's Odoo 19.0 Community and the prior backlog's 18.0 is flagged for stakeholder confirmation rather than resolved here.

**`account_budget_management` is present in this repository, and it is not an Odoo Community Association module.** It lives at `addons/account_budget_management/` in this checkout at version 19.0.1.0.0 under AGPL-3, declaring `depends` of `account` and `analytic`, delivered by an earlier phase of this programme; the similarly purposed `account_budget_oca` in OCA/account-budgeting is a separate code base, assessed for overlap in § OCA Module Compatibility, and neither is the source of the other. It already ships a `budget.budget.period` model carrying a period type of monthly, quarterly, annual or custom, an allocated amount under a non-negative database check, an allocation method of equal distribution, manual entry, copy-from-previous or percentage, an allocation share held to 4 decimal places, the audit-trail trio of previous amount, modifying user and note, and constraints that check the period dates, containment inside the parent budget window and the sum against the parent line. Discovery note **D-003** in the Epic credits it with period allocation. This story therefore begins with an assessment of what is already satisfied rather than with a new model: building what exists would give one budget line two period grids and so two answers to the same period-variance question.

**Why the residual goes to the final period, by rule.** Any other placement is defensible in isolation and indefensible in aggregate. Spreading the residual makes the twelve amounts depend on the spreading order; placing it in the first period front-loads a plan for no accounting reason; placing it where the largest period sits makes the answer depend on data that later changes. Fixing it in the final period of the fiscal year makes the whole grid a pure function of the line total, the period count and the currency's rounding increment — which is what lets a period figure quoted in a March management pack be reproduced from the line total in October, and what lets the acceptance tests assert exact amounts rather than tolerances.

**The period grid is a shared contract with the next story.** The monthly, quarterly and annual vocabulary and the boundaries generated here are consumed unchanged by [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) as the column axis of the **Budget vs. Actual** report, and by [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md) as the axis a trend and a year-to-date total are accumulated along. A period label must therefore mean one range in all three files: "Q1 2025" is 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 for company `US-01` in every one of them.

**Approval belongs to the allocated plan.** The budget lifecycle in [FEATURE-001-09 §8.1](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md#81-budget-lifecycle) is stated in the four states the model declares — `draft`, `confirmed`, `closed` and `cancelled` — and the Group Controller's approval is what moves a budget out of `draft`. It is the **allocated** plan that is approved, which is why [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md) delivers the `draft` to `confirmed` transition and this story is the gate that precedes it. Nothing in this story approves a plan; a mid-year revision is a manager-only reset to `draft`, an edit and a re-confirmation rather than a fifth state, which is also what keeps the period-sum invariant intact through a revision, and the remaining transitions are the feature's target lifecycle, and Edge Case 5 describes a revision's effect on the grid rather than delivering the state machine that records it.

**A day-weighted allocation method is deliberately out of scope.** Equal distribution divides the line total by the period count, so February carries the same amount as January and a leap day changes a period's dates and not its amount. A day-weighted method — one that pro-rates by the day count of each period — is a different arithmetic with a different answer, and it is recorded here as a candidate for the group budget policy rather than assumed. Keeping the two distinct is what stops a reviewer reading a `$100,000.00 USD` February as a rounding defect.
