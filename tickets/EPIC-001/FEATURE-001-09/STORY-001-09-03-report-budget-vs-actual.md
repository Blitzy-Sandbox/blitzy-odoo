## User Story

**As a** FP&A Analyst

**I want** to run the **Budget vs. Actual** report for a named company, a named date range and a named set of analytic dimensions, so that each budget line shows its period budget, the actual aggregated from posted journal entries, the variance as an amount and the variance as a percentage

**So that** budget consumption and deviation for the group's plan are visible within 24 hours of period close, and on demand while the period is still open, instead of being compiled by hand into a spreadsheet after the month has ended (SM-014).

### Business Context

A budget that has been defined and phased across the fiscal calendar still answers nothing on its own. The question management asks — *is Q1 spending ahead of plan, and by how much* — is answered only when the plan is placed beside the posted ledger for the identical company, the identical accounts and the identical date range. This story is that placement, and it is the point at which budgeting stops being a planning exercise and becomes a control.

Three properties make the resulting figure trustworthy, and each is asserted below rather than assumed:

- **The actual column is the ledger, not a copy of it.** Actual amounts are aggregated from `account.move.line` records whose parent journal entry stands in the **posted** state and balances with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`. Entries in draft or cancelled state contribute `$0.00 USD`. There is no cached actual and no budget-side ledger, so a variance figure and the General Ledger behind it cannot disagree (C-012).
- **Running the report changes nothing.** Generating, grouping, filtering, drilling into and exporting the comparison creates, alters and reverses no journal entry. The journal-entry count, the account balances and the Trial Balance of the reported company for the reported range are identical before and after a run — the same boundary [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md) and [STORY-001-09-02](./STORY-001-09-02-allocate-budget-periods.md) draw for definition and allocation. A budget column is supplementary management information presented beside a statutory statement, never inside one, as §7.3 of the parent feature records.
- **Every figure is reproducible.** Each amount carries its currency and its rounding basis, each percentage carries its decimal places, and each report line ties to the journal items that produced it at a difference of `$0.00 USD`. That is what lets a Q1 figure quoted in an April management pack be re-derived from the ledger in October, and what lets the External Auditor test the trail from a variance line to a journal item without a data request.

The boundary with the story that follows is deliberate and is what keeps both stories INVEST-Small. This story **computes and presents** the variance amount, the variance percentage and the percentage consumed. The **signing** of that variance as favourable or unfavourable by account type, the retained variance explanation notes, the configurable consumption thresholds and the alerts a breach delivers belong to [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md). Where this story states that an Expense 6100 overspend is unfavourable, it is applying the convention that story owns, not defining it.

## Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria are authored, inside the 4-to-8 bound the Epic sets in [§5.3](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#53-feature-and-story-decomposition-guidelines), and they carry the mandated coverage distribution: Scenarios 1, 3, 4 and 8 are the valid-input cases (the comparison itself, dimension grouping, period and year-to-date columns, and drill-down), Scenario 5 is the invalid and incomplete-input case, Scenario 6 is the error-handling case, and Scenarios 2 and 7 are the accounting edge cases — the exclusion of unposted amounts, and a reversal inside the reported range. Every scenario states a **single trigger**; where a criterion would otherwise carry a second trigger it is relocated to a named test in § Test Requirements, and nothing is lost by the relocation.

Every monetary figure names its currency, its amount and its rounding. Every variance names **both** the absolute amount in its currency **and** the percentage with its own decimal places. Amounts throughout are stated in USD, held to 2 decimal places and rounded half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, except where company `NL-01` is named, whose amounts are held to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01. Percentages are stated to 2 decimal places.

**The baseline for all eight scenarios.** Company **`US-01`**, Global Holdings Inc., the group's United States operating entity whose functional currency is USD, holds the budget **"FY2025 Operating Budget"** in state `confirmed` for fiscal year 2025, which the `US-01` fiscal-year configuration defines as **2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31** with **Q1 running 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31** (CAP-003 of [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md)). Its three lines, fixed by [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md) and allocated monthly by [STORY-001-09-02](./STORY-001-09-02-allocate-budget-periods.md), are Revenue 4000 at `$4,800,000.00 USD` annually, being twelve monthly periods of `$400,000.00 USD`; Expense 6100 at `$1,200,000.00 USD` annually, being twelve monthly periods of `$100,000.00 USD`; and Depreciation Expense 6500 at `$240,000.00 USD` annually. The report under test is named **Budget vs. Actual** throughout.

**No criterion below creates, alters or reverses a journal entry.** Where a criterion names an entry, it names an entry that already stands posted in the ledger, and it states both sides of that entry as equal amounts in USD so that total debits equal total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`.

### Scenario 1: Budget vs. Actual for a named company and date range reports budget, actual, variance amount and variance percentage per line

- **Given** the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` stands `confirmed` with its three lines allocated monthly across fiscal year 2025, and posted journal entries exist in the books of `US-01` for 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 totalling `$312,450.00 USD` against Expense 6100 and `$1,260,000.00 USD` against Revenue 4000, every contributing entry standing in the posted state and balancing with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`
- **When** the FP&A Analyst runs the **Budget vs. Actual** report for company `US-01` with the date-range parameters 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31
- **Then** the **Expense 6100** line reads a budget of `$300,000.00 USD`, being the three monthly periods of `$100,000.00 USD` that fall inside the range, an actual of `$312,450.00 USD`, a variance amount of `$12,450.00 USD` reported as unfavourable, a variance percentage of **4.15%** stated to 2 decimal places, and a percentage consumed of **104.15%** stated to 2 decimal places; the **Revenue 4000** line reads a budget of `$1,200,000.00 USD`, being the three monthly periods of `$400,000.00 USD` inside the range, an actual of `$1,260,000.00 USD`, a variance amount of `$60,000.00 USD` reported as favourable, a variance percentage of **5.00%** and a percentage consumed of **105.00%**, each stated to 2 decimal places; every monetary figure is rounded to 2 decimal places half-up at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the report header states the reporting company as `US-01`, Global Holdings Inc., and the date range as 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31, so no figure on the report is readable without knowing whose books and which span it came from; and **the ledger is unchanged by the run** — the journal-entry count of `US-01` for 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31, the balances of accounts 4000, 6100 and 6500, and the Trial Balance of `US-01` for that range reporting total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, are each identical before and after the report is generated

### Scenario 2: Actuals aggregate posted, balanced journal entries only, and unposted amounts contribute nothing

- **Given** the posted Q1 2025 population of Scenario 1 stands in the books of company `US-01`, and alongside it a **draft** vendor bill of `$25,000.00 USD` charged to Expense 6100 and dated 2025-02-14, and a **cancelled** journal entry of `$12,000.00 USD` charged to Expense 6100 and dated 2025-03-05, neither of which has ever been posted
- **When** the FP&A Analyst runs the **Budget vs. Actual** report for company `US-01` with the date-range parameters 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31
- **Then** the actual for Expense 6100 reads `$312,450.00 USD`, stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, against a budget of `$300,000.00 USD` and so a variance of `$12,450.00 USD` at 4.15% stated to 2 decimal places — the draft bill contributes `$0.00 USD` and the cancelled entry contributes `$0.00 USD`, where including both would have produced an actual of `$349,450.00 USD` and a variance of `$49,450.00 USD` at 16.48%; the report states that actual amounts aggregate `account.move.line` records whose parent journal entry stands in the **posted** state and whose parent entry balances with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; the count of contributing journal items whose parent entry is not posted is **0**; and no journal entry is created, altered or reversed by the run, the draft bill still standing in draft and the cancelled entry still cancelled after it. That posting the `$25,000.00 USD` bill later moves the actual to `$337,450.00 USD` on the next run of the same range, while leaving the budget at `$300,000.00 USD`, is a second trigger — a further action by the Accounts Payable Clerk of [STORY-001-02-03](../FEATURE-001-02/STORY-001-02-03-post-vendor-bill-entries.md) — and is asserted by `test_posting_a_previously_draft_bill_moves_the_actual_and_not_the_budget` in § Test Requirements

### Scenario 3: Grouping by analytic plan and analytic account produces subtotals that sum to the ungrouped total

- **Given** the Expense 6100 line of the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` carries the analytic distribution fixed by [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md) — 60.00% to the **Marketing** analytic account of the **Departments** plan combined with the **Website Redesign** analytic account of the **Projects** plan, and 40.00% to the **Sales** analytic account of Departments combined with the same Website Redesign account, stored as the `analytic_distribution` value `{"123,125": 60.0, "124,125": 40.0}` — and the posted Q1 2025 journal items charged to Expense 6100 carry that same distribution
- **When** the FP&A Analyst groups the **Budget vs. Actual** report for company `US-01` over 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 by analytic plan and analytic account
- **Then** the **Departments** plan presents the Marketing analytic account at a budget of `$180,000.00 USD`, an actual of `$187,470.00 USD`, a variance amount of `$7,470.00 USD` unfavourable and a variance percentage of 4.15%, and the Sales analytic account at a budget of `$120,000.00 USD`, an actual of `$124,980.00 USD`, a variance amount of `$4,980.00 USD` unfavourable and a variance percentage of 4.15%; the Departments plan subtotal reads a budget of `$300,000.00 USD`, an actual of `$312,450.00 USD` and a variance of `$12,450.00 USD` at 4.15%; the **Projects** plan subtotal names the Website Redesign analytic account and reads the same budget of `$300,000.00 USD`, actual of `$312,450.00 USD` and variance of `$12,450.00 USD` at 4.15%, because the whole line is attributed to that one project; every monetary figure is stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 and every percentage to 2 decimal places; each subtotal names the analytic account and the plan it belongs to rather than showing a bare identifier; the subtotals within each plan sum to the ungrouped report total of `$300,000.00 USD` budget and `$312,450.00 USD` actual at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, so the count of plans whose subtotals do not tie to the ungrouped total is **0**; every figure is drawn from the books of company `US-01` alone; and the ledger is unchanged by the grouping

### Scenario 4: Monthly period columns with year-to-date totals, and a period with no posted line reading zero

- **Given** the three lines of the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` hold monthly allocations across all twelve 2025 periods, the posted actuals charged to Expense 6100 inside Q1 2025 are `$98,200.00 USD` in January 2025, `$106,750.00 USD` in February 2025 and `$107,500.00 USD` in March 2025, and the Depreciation Expense 6500 line carries a January 2025 period allocation of `$0.00 USD` with no journal item posted to account 6500 in that period
- **When** the FP&A Analyst runs the **Budget vs. Actual** report for company `US-01` over 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 at monthly granularity
- **Then** three period columns appear, resolved from the `US-01` fiscal-year configuration as 2025-01-01 to 2025-01-31, 2025-02-01 to 2025-02-28 and 2025-03-01 to 2025-03-31 and each labelled with its range, and each column carries a budget, an actual, a variance amount and a variance percentage; on the Expense 6100 line January 2025 reads a budget of `$100,000.00 USD`, an actual of `$98,200.00 USD` and a variance of `$1,800.00 USD` favourable at 1.80%, February 2025 reads `$100,000.00 USD`, `$106,750.00 USD` and `$6,750.00 USD` unfavourable at 6.75%, and March 2025 reads `$100,000.00 USD`, `$107,500.00 USD` and `$7,500.00 USD` unfavourable at 7.50%; the year-to-date columns read a **YTD Budget of `$300,000.00 USD`**, a **YTD Actual of `$312,450.00 USD`** and a **YTD Variance of `$12,450.00 USD` at 4.15%**, and the three period variances of `-$1,800.00 USD`, `$6,750.00 USD` and `$7,500.00 USD` sum to that year-to-date variance at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; the January 2025 cell of the Depreciation Expense 6500 line displays a budget of `$0.00 USD` and an actual of `$0.00 USD` rather than an empty cell, so a reader is not left to infer whether the period was unplanned or merely unposted; every monetary figure is stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 and every percentage to 2 decimal places; and the ledger is unchanged by the run. That the same range at quarterly granularity collapses to one column of `$300,000.00 USD` budget and `$312,450.00 USD` actual is a second trigger and is asserted by `test_quarterly_granularity_collapses_three_monthly_columns_into_one` in § Test Requirements

### Scenario 5: A date range with no budget returns a named statement rather than a silent page of zeros

- **When** the FP&A Analyst runs the **Budget vs. Actual** report for company `US-01` with the date-range parameters 2024-01-01 to 2024-03-31
- **Then** the report returns **0** budget lines and states that no budget is defined for company `US-01` for the range 2024-01-01 to 2024-03-31, naming the company and both date parameters, rather than rendering a budget of `$0.00 USD` against the posted 2024 actual and presenting the whole actual as a variance as though a budget existed; the statement discloses no stack trace, no query text and no file-system path (C-019, C-020); no budget, budget line or period allocation is created by the request; and no journal entry is created, altered or reversed. A date range whose end precedes its start — 2025-03-31 to 2025-01-01 — is a second trigger with a different parameter set, and is refused with a message naming the start-date parameter, the end-date parameter and the ordering check that failed, asserted by `test_report_rejects_end_date_before_start_date_with_named_message` in § Test Requirements

### Scenario 6: A zero budget reports its variance as an amount and its percentages as not applicable, and stays in the report total

- **When** the FP&A Analyst runs the **Budget vs. Actual** report for company `US-01` over 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31
- **Then** the Depreciation Expense 6500 line reads a budget of `$0.00 USD`, an actual of `$18,000.00 USD` and a variance amount of `$18,000.00 USD` reported as unfavourable, each stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01; the variance percentage and the percentage consumed both display **`N/A`** rather than 0.00%, an infinite value or a division-by-zero error, and no exception is raised by the run; the line stays **included** in the report total, so the expense total for the range reads a budget of `$300,000.00 USD` and an actual of `$330,450.00 USD`, being `$312,450.00 USD` on Expense 6100 plus `$18,000.00 USD` on Depreciation Expense 6500, with a total variance of `$30,450.00 USD` unfavourable at 10.15% stated to 2 decimal places, and the count of lines dropped from the total because their budget is `$0.00 USD` is **0**; and the ledger is unchanged by the run

### Scenario 7: A posted reversal inside the reported range nets the entry it reverses and both items stay visible

- **Given** the posted Q1 2025 population of company `US-01` contains a journal entry dated 2025-02-10 that debits Expense 6100 by `$40,000.00 USD` and credits Accounts Payable 2000 by `$40,000.00 USD`, so its total debits equal its total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, together with its **posted reversal** dated 2025-02-28 that debits Accounts Payable 2000 by `$40,000.00 USD` and credits Expense 6100 by `$40,000.00 USD`, also balancing at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, and both entries stand posted with neither cancelled
- **When** the FP&A Analyst runs the **Budget vs. Actual** report for company `US-01` over 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31
- **Then** the actual for Expense 6100 reads `$312,450.00 USD`, unchanged by the pair, because the entry and its reversal both fall inside the reported range and net to `$0.00 USD` there; the February 2025 period actual reads `$106,750.00 USD` net of the pair; the variance stays `$12,450.00 USD` unfavourable at 4.15% and the percentage consumed stays 104.15%, each stated to 2 decimal places; **both journal items remain visible in the drill-down**, listed separately under their own dates of 2025-02-10 and 2025-02-28 with their own entry references rather than being suppressed as a matched pair, so the count of contributing journal items hidden from the drill-down is **0**; and the ledger is unchanged by the run — the report neither reverses, re-reverses nor re-posts either entry, and the journal-entry count of `US-01` for the range is identical before and after. A reversal dated **outside** the reported range is a different data condition and is recorded in § Edge Cases with the test that asserts it

### Scenario 8: Drill-down from a reported actual lists the journal items behind it, sums to it, and returns with its context intact

| 1 | **Zero and null amounts — a zero period budget, and a period with an allocation but no posted line.** The Depreciation Expense 6500 line of company `US-01` carries a Q1 2025 period budget of `$0.00 USD` against a posted actual of `$18,000.00 USD`; and the January 2025 period of that line has an allocation but no journal item posted to account 6500 | The zero-budget line reports a variance **amount** of `$18,000.00 USD` unfavourable, stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, and reports the variance percentage and the percentage consumed as **`N/A`** — the guard is the absence of a percentage, not a zero percentage, because 0.00% would read as "nothing consumed" on a line that consumed `$18,000.00 USD`, and an infinite value would read as a defect. In every case both entries stay posted, both keep total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, both remain listed separately in the drill-down, and the report creates, alters and reverses nothing — a cross-boundary reversal is a disclosure question for the period under review, not a licence for the report to adjust a range it was not asked for |

- [ ] **A budget column is management information, not a statutory amount.** It is presented beside a statutory statement and excluded from the Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss that FEATURE-001-07 publishes, consistent with IAS 1 presentation of financial statements, under which supplementary information does not alter a statutory caption or its amount. This is why no path in this story posts to the ledger.
- [ ] **The actual column is the sub-ledger, aggregated and not restated.** Every amount in it originates in a posted `account.move.line` whose parent entry balances, so the comparison inherits the ledger's own double-entry integrity rather than asserting a separate one, and a report line ties to the journal items behind it at a difference of `$0.00 USD`.
- [ ] **Variance presentation follows management-accounting convention.** Absolute variance is stated as an amount in the budget currency at that currency's decimal places, and percentage variance and percentage consumed to 2 decimal places, following Institute of Management Accountants practice. The favourable and unfavourable **signing rule** by account type is owned by [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md) and applied here rather than defined here.
- [ ] **Reproducibility is what makes a reported variance auditable.** Because the report is a pure function of the budget, the posted ledger and the named parameters, two runs of the same range return the same figures, and a locked period's figures cannot move while the lock stands — the monitoring-activity evidence tested under the COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework and section 404 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.
- [ ] **Currency translation follows IAS 21 where a group view crosses currencies.** A translated figure carries its rate and the basis of that rate, each translated amount rounds at the presentation currency's own increment, and a translation difference is disclosed rather than pushed back onto the functional-currency figures.

## Estimation

| Dimension | Rating | Basis |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Effort** | Medium-High | One aggregation of posted journal items over three axes — account, analytic distribution and date — for one company and one range; grouping at plan and analytic-account level with subtotals that tie; period columns at three granularities with year-to-date totals; a filter set with validation; the zero-budget guard; a drill-down listing six attributes per journal item with navigation to the source document and breadcrumbs back; two export formats with cell neutralization; and eight acceptance tests plus the arithmetic, isolation, hostile-input and 100,000-item performance tests. Volume is real but bounded: no model is created for the ledger side, because every actual is read from records that already exist |
| **Complexity** | High | The difficulty is **aggregation across three dimensions at once, held to a tie-out**. The grouped subtotals must sum to the ungrouped total at a difference of `$0.00 USD` even though the analytic attribution is a percentage applied to a line balance and each product is rounded; the year-to-date column must equal the sum of its period components rather than a separately computed figure; the actual column must equal the posted General Ledger for the same account, company and range at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; and all of that must hold while the query stays inside 30 seconds over 100,000 posted items, which is what makes the analytic-distribution query path — the JSON key index against a join against a pre-aggregate — a genuine discovery question rather than a detail. Beside that sit two exactness requirements that are easy to get subtly wrong: the zero-budget guard must produce the **absence** of a percentage rather than a zero, and a reversal must net inside a range while both of its journal items stay individually visible in the drill-down |
| **Uncertainty** | Medium | Lower than it looks on the data side and higher on the engine side. Known: the posted-state field, the balance and date fields, the analytic distribution and its GIN index, the currency precision fields, and an existing comparison model with a posted-only domain, a consumed-percentage computation, an analytic breakdown, year-to-date totals and consumption bands were all read in this repository during discovery, and two of its gaps are already identified rather than suspected. Open: the report-engine choice left to D-004, the XLSX generation path whose present implementation raises a `UserError`, whether the 30-second envelope holds over the JSON-key index at 100,000 items, and whether DEC-001 confirms a platform version other than 19.0, which would change the analytic-attribution mechanism the aggregation depends on |
| **Story Points** | **8** (Fibonacci: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) | |

**Why 8 and not 5 or 13.** The 8 reflects four things the smaller estimate would not carry: aggregation across **three** dimensions with subtotals that tie to the ungrouped total at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; period columns with **year-to-date** totals whose components must sum to them; a **drill-down and two exports** that preserve filters, carry expanded detail and neutralize every cell; and a **performance target** of under 30 seconds over 100,000 posted journal items that makes the query path a design decision rather than an implementation detail. Five points would fit a single-axis comparison with no grouping, no year-to-date and no export — which is not this story. Thirteen would overstate work whose data model is entirely read-only, whose fields and indexes already exist in this repository, and whose comparison logic is already partly present in `account_budget_management` and credited under D-003. The estimate assumes the column set and the consumption bands are signed off by the Group Controller during the sprint, and it **excludes** favourable and unfavourable signing, variance explanation notes, threshold configuration, alert delivery and the alert dashboard, all of which belong to [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md).

### Accounting Reconciliation Gate

This gate is the accounting contract of the story. Each item is asserted as an amount, at the named currency's decimal places and rounded half-up at its rounding increment, in the currency named.

- [ ] **Every report line ties back to the sub-ledger journal items that produced it.** Drilling from any reported actual lists the `account.move.line` records behind it, and those listed amounts sum to the reported figure at a difference of `$0.00 USD` — `$312,450.00 USD` for Expense 6100 over 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 in company `US-01`, and `$1,260,000.00 USD` for Revenue 4000 over the same range.
- [ ] **The sum of actuals in the report equals the sum of the underlying posted journal items for the same accounts, dimensions and date range.** For each of accounts 4000, 6100 and 6500 the report's actual equals the posted General Ledger total for company `US-01` over 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; the grouped subtotals of `$187,470.00 USD` and `$124,980.00 USD` sum to the ungrouped actual of `$312,450.00 USD` at a difference of `$0.00 USD`; and the three monthly actuals of `$98,200.00 USD`, `$106,750.00 USD` and `$107,500.00 USD` sum to the same figure.
- [ ] **The posted entries feeding those actuals have debits equal to credits.** Every entry contributing to the actual column stands in the posted state and balances with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD` in the company currency — including the `$40,000.00 USD` charge that debits Expense 6100 and credits Accounts Payable 2000, its `$40,000.00 USD` reversal that debits Accounts Payable 2000 and credits Expense 6100, and each `$9,000.00 USD` depreciation entry that debits Depreciation Expense 6500 and credits Accumulated Depreciation 1590. Entries in draft or cancelled state contribute `$0.00 USD`, and the count of contributing journal items whose parent entry is not posted is 0.
- [ ] **Report generation leaves the ledger unchanged.** Generating, grouping, filtering, changing granularity, drilling and exporting the comparison each leave the journal-entry count of company `US-01` for the reported range and the balances of accounts 4000, 6100, 6500, 2000 and 1590 identical before and after; no code path in this story creates, alters or reverses an `account.move` or an `account.move.line`; and the Trial Balance of `US-01` for 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31, run before and after the whole walkthrough, reports the same figures on both runs with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`.
- [ ] **Variance is stated as an amount and as a percentage on every line.** Expense 6100 reports `$12,450.00 USD` and 4.15% with 104.15% consumed; Revenue 4000 reports `$60,000.00 USD` and 5.00% with 105.00% consumed; each amount is stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 and each percentage to 2 decimal places; and a line whose budget is `$0.00 USD` reports its variance amount of `$18,000.00 USD` with `N/A` in both percentage columns rather than a division result.
- [ ] **Every multi-company figure names the company whose books it came from.** The report header names `US-01`, Global Holdings Inc., and the date range; a group presentation naming more than one entity attributes every figure to its contributing company; and a `NL-01` figure is stated in EUR at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01 with any USD translation carrying its rate, its basis and the disclosed translation difference of `$0.01 USD`.
- [ ] **Tax amounts are out of this story's scope and stay out.** The comparison records no tax code, no base amount and no tax amount of its own, and no tax control-account balance moves as a result of it; where a tax expense account is budgeted, its actual is the posted amount on that expense account and the tax determination that produced it — with its tax code, its base amount and its tax amount stated separately — remains the work of FEATURE-001-05.
- [ ] **A locked period's reported figures are reproducible.** With the `US-01` journal-entry lock date at 2025-03-31, two runs of the range 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 return identical figures, so a variance quoted as close evidence can be re-derived from the ledger later (SM-014, C-009).
- [ ] **The Chief Accountant and the Financial Reporting Manager have signed off** the actual column against the posted General Ledger for company `US-01` over 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31, and the alignment of the report's date range with the Profit & Loss date range for the same company and period (ORD-004), with the reconciliation worksheet retained as close evidence.

## Notes

**Platform facts, recorded without judgement.** `account_budget` and `account_reports` are **absent from `addons/`** in this repository. Both are Odoo Enterprise modules and this Odoo 19.0 Community checkout does not ship them — that is a fact of the environment, **not** a prohibition on using them. The superseded flat-layout backlog listed both as specifically prohibited dependencies; that blanket restriction is retired here. Which source supplies the budgeting and reporting capability — an Odoo Enterprise subscription, or the Odoo Community Association route with `mis_builder` and `account_financial_report` alongside bespoke work — is **DEC-002** in the Epic's open decisions register, owned by the CFO / Finance Director with the Group Controller, and it is confirmed before this story enters development. The platform version is the separate decision **DEC-001**, and the mismatch between the programme request's Odoo 17, this repository's Odoo 19.0 Community and the prior backlog's 18.0 is flagged for stakeholder confirmation rather than resolved here.

**Two Community-edition add-ons are present, and they are programme artifacts rather than Odoo Community Association modules.** `addons/account_budget_management/` stands at version 19.0.1.0.0 under AGPL-3 with `depends` of `account` and `analytic`, and `addons/account_financial_report_ce/` at version 19.0.1.1.0 under AGPL-3; both were delivered by an earlier phase of this programme. The similarly purposed `account_budget_oca` in OCA/account-budgeting and `account_financial_report` in OCA/account-financial-reporting are separate code bases, assessed for overlap in § OCA Module Compatibility, and neither is the source of the other. The present budgeting add-on already ships an abstract `budget.vs.actual.report` that restricts its actuals domain to `parent_state = 'posted'`, computes a consumed percentage, derives an analytic breakdown from the posted move lines, produces year-to-date totals and classifies consumption at thresholds of 80.0 and 100.0, together with a variance wizard carrying PDF and XLSX actions. Discovery note **D-003** in the Epic credits it accordingly. This story therefore begins with an assessment rather than a new model, and discovery has already surfaced two concrete gaps to confirm: the consumed percentage falls back to `0.0` when the planned amount is zero, where Scenario 6 requires `N/A`, and the XLSX action raises a `UserError` because the export pipeline is not bundled, where Scenario 8 requires an XLSX file carrying the expanded detail. Building what already exists would give one budget line two comparison paths and so two answers to the same variance question.

**Why the actuals source moved from analytic lines to journal items.** The superseded story aggregated actuals from `account.analytic.line`. This backlog aggregates from **`account.move.line`**, by account, by analytic distribution and by date, because the two properties that make an actual trustworthy live on the journal side: the **posted state** of the parent entry, and the fact that the entry **balances** with total debits equal to total credits. An analytic line is a projection of a journal item, so a report built on it can be right and still be unable to prove why. The analytic line is named as a permitted secondary or cross-check read, and where both are read the two are proved to agree at a difference of `$0.00 USD` rather than the report being free to prefer one.

**This story is the contract boundary of the folder.** The Q1 2025 figures are stated **once, here**, and are consumed unchanged by [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md): Expense 6100 with a budget of `$300,000.00 USD`, an actual of `$312,450.00 USD`, a variance of `$12,450.00 USD` and 4.15% with 104.15% consumed; Revenue 4000 with a budget of `$1,200,000.00 USD`, an actual of `$1,260,000.00 USD`, a variance of `$60,000.00 USD` and 5.00% with 105.00% consumed; and Depreciation Expense 6500 with a Q1 budget of `$0.00 USD` against an actual of `$18,000.00 USD`. A figure that appears in both files must be the same figure, because the two are read side by side at review and a management pack quotes one of them.

**One figure to reconcile at grooming.** §1.4 and §4.1 of the parent [FEATURE-001-09](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md) currently state the same Expense 6100 line for the same company and the same range with an actual of `$318,450.00 USD`, a variance of `$18,450.00 USD` at 6.15% and a consumption of 106.15%, a difference of `$6,000.00 USD` from the actual carried here. This story's figures are the ones its acceptance tests assert and the ones `STORY-001-09-04` inherits, so **one set must be adopted across the tree**: either the feature-level rows are aligned to the `$312,450.00 USD` worked here, or this story and its successor are re-based on `$318,450.00 USD`. The item is raised for backlog grooming rather than resolved by silently changing one file, and it changes no rule, no scenario structure and no acceptance-criteria count — only the seeded actual and the four figures derived from it.

**What this story deliberately does not do.** It does not sign a variance favourable or unfavourable by account type as a delivered rule, record a variance explanation note, configure a consumption threshold, evaluate a breach, deliver or suppress an alert, or render an alert dashboard — every one of those belongs to [STORY-001-09-04](./STORY-001-09-04-analyze-variances.md) and is referenced here rather than duplicated, which is what keeps both stories inside the Small criterion of INVEST. It also creates no journal entry of any kind, including no commitment or encumbrance entry behind a committed-spend column: the comparison reads the ledger and reports it, and the Trial Balance before and after a run is the same Trial Balance.
