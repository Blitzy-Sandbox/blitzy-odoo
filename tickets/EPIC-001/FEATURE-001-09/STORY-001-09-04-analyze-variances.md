## User Story

**As a** Group Controller

**I want** signed variance analysis across the budgeted accounts and analytic dimensions of each company, carried across periods with year-to-date totals and retained explanation notes, together with configurable consumption thresholds that notify named recipients when a threshold is breached

**So that** over-performance and under-performance against the group's plan are identified and escalated **before** period close, in the company whose books they belong to, instead of being discovered afterwards from a management pack that arrives once the period is already shut (SM-014).

### Business Context

[STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) answers *what the difference is*. It does not answer the two questions a Group Controller actually acts on: **is this difference good or bad**, and **who found out in time to do something about it**. Those are the two halves of this story, and they are one story rather than two because neither is demonstrable without the other's data and both are read from the same aggregation.

- **A signed variance, not a signless one.** `$12,450.00 USD` of difference on Expense 6100 and `$60,000.00 USD` of difference on Revenue 4000 have the same arithmetic shape and opposite meanings. The direction comes from `account.account.account_type`: on the income types `income` and `income_other` an actual above budget is **favourable**; on the expense types `expense`, `expense_other`, `expense_depreciation` and `expense_direct_cost` an actual **below** budget is favourable. Signing from the account type rather than from the sign of the number is what lets a favourable total and an unfavourable total be added up and reported as a net position that a board pack can quote.
- **Variance with a memory.** A figure explains a period; a note explains a figure. A variance explanation note carries its text, its author and its timestamp against the budget line it explains, survives a regeneration of the report over the same range, and is what turns a monthly review into a record an External Auditor can test (CF-004).
- **Variance across time, not one snapshot.** One quarter at 4.15% over plan reads differently once the next three quarters are beside it. Trend columns per period with cumulative year-to-date totals and a trend direction of improving, stable or deteriorating are what separate a seasonal pattern from a deteriorating one (CF-004).
- **Alerting is the control, and it is what makes this feature preventive rather than descriptive.** A threshold ladder configured as percentages of the budget — 75%, 90%, 100% and 110%, with severities Warning, Alert, Critical and Over-budget — converts consumption into a dated event delivered to named recipients within 1 hour of the posting that crossed it. Without it the whole feature reports history; with it the Group Controller hears about the 90% crossing while there is still budget left to manage.
- **Neither half moves the ledger.** Computing a signed variance, recording a note, evaluating a threshold and delivering an alert are all reads. The journal-entry count, the account balances and the Trial Balance of the affected company for the affected range are identical before and after every one of them, which is why nothing in this story needs a posting rule and why a commitment or encumbrance entry is not created to support a threshold.

The two halves are held together by one rule: **every variance and every alert names the company whose books it belongs to.** Budgets are held per legal entity and per fiscal year, so a Group Controller reviewing `US-01`, `NL-01`, `GB-01` and `SG-01` in one sitting has to be able to say which entity a `$12,450.00 USD` overspend and which entity a Warning notification came from. That is the multi-company clause of this story and the reason it is linked to [FEATURE-001-06](../FEATURE-001-06-multi-company-consolidation.md).

### ALT-001 — The Alert Crossing Identity, Snapshot, Dispatch, Recipient Authority and Cadence Contract

An alert is a control, and a control is only as good as the machinery that decides it, records it, delivers it and bounds who may read it. A crossing decided from a figure that moved while the decision was being taken, an event written without its dispatch, a message delivered to a recipient outside the alerting company, or a cadence stated one way in one place and another way elsewhere each turn the control into a claim. The five parts below settle each of those, once, and every other statement of a cadence in this feature reads Part 5 rather than restating it.

**Part 1 — a crossing has a stable identity, held by the database and taken under a lock.** The identity of a crossing is the tuple **company, budget line, rung, plan version and arming cycle** — the arming cycle being an integer that increments each time a rung is re-armed after its consumption has fallen back below it, so a legitimate second crossing of the same rung is a **new** identity rather than a duplicate of the first. That tuple is held by a **database unique constraint**, not by the `alert_notified` flag the present add-on carries: a flag is read and written in application code and races itself when the scheduled evaluation and a manual re-evaluation run at once. The evaluation of one budget line takes a **row lock** on that line — `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`, or a PostgreSQL advisory lock keyed on the crossing tuple — and holds it across the read-decide-write window, so two simultaneous evaluations produce **1** alert event and the second is recorded as a duplicate attempt rather than delivering a second message. A lock left by a crashed worker is reclaimed after a declared stale interval with the abandoned attempt recorded, and **no part of this guard is overridable by the Group Controller or by a C-023 principal**: a re-notification is a separate, named authorization held by another role and is recorded with its reason (C-028).

**Part 2 — the decision is taken against a recorded watermark and revalidated inside the lock.** Before it decides, the evaluation records a **watermark** of everything the decision depends on: the company, the budget line, the evaluated range, the **count and the maximum identifier of the posted journal items** in that range, the **plan version** the budget amount came from, and the **configuration version** of the rung ladder. The event it writes stores the actual amount, the budget amount, the consumption percentage and the breach amount **as they stood at that watermark** rather than a reference that would re-derive them from a ledger or a plan that has since moved (C-026). Immediately before the event is written, and inside the lock, the watermark is **re-read and compared**: where a journal item has landed, the plan version has moved or the ladder has been re-configured, the evaluation **re-reads and re-decides against the new watermark** rather than writing a decision taken on stale data — so the count of alert events whose stored actual differs from the ledger at its own stored watermark is **0**, and the count of events written against a superseded plan version is **0**. The recomputation of Scenario 8 and the budget revision of Edge Case 5 are therefore **re-armings against a new watermark**, never a rewrite of a stored one: a fired event keeps the amounts, the percentage, the plan version and the timestamp it fired with (a read-then-write race, CWE-367, being the failure this part exists to close).

**Part 3 — the event and its dispatch are coupled by a transactional outbox.** The alert event and a **dispatch intent** are written in the **same database transaction**, and delivery is a separate step that drains the outbox afterwards — so a committed event without its dispatch and a dispatch without its event are both unreachable. Delivery declares a per-attempt connect timeout and read timeout; a failure is retried a **bounded** number of times with exponential backoff and **randomized jitter**, so the retries of two or more companies' evaluations do not synchronize onto one instant; a declared count of consecutive failures against the mail transport **opens a circuit breaker** for a declared cool-down during which attempts are parked rather than issued; and work that exhausts its retries enters the named terminal state **`undeliverable`**, which never silently retries again, is visible to an operator with its failure reason and on the consumption-band dashboard beside the budget it concerns, and is resumable only by an explicit recorded action. A delivery that never leaves the outgoing-mail queue is recorded as **failed** rather than counted as delivered, so the count of crossings whose event carries no delivery outcome is **0**. After a crash or a restart the outbox is drained from its recorded position, so the count of recipients receiving two messages for one crossing identity is **0** and the count of crossings with an event but no dispatch outcome is **0** (C-027).

**Part 4 — a recipient is authorized at send time and again at open time.** The recipient set is expanded from its named `res.users` and named `res.groups` at **send time** and then **filtered**: a recipient is delivered to only where the alerting company is among that user's allowed companies **and** the user holds the budget-read right in it. A recipient failing either test is **dropped, and the drop is recorded** with the recipient, the company and the failed test, so the count of notifications delivered to a user outside the alerting company is **0** and a dropped recipient is visible rather than silent. A dropped recipient receives **nothing** — not a redacted copy, not a subject line carrying the budget name, and no figure of any kind — because a subject line naming "FY2025 Operating Budget over 100%" is itself a disclosure. The **link** the notification carries resolves through an **authenticated Odoo session** and the same two tests are applied **again at open time**, so a right revoked between the send and the open yields a generic denial under C-020 rather than a cached page, and the attempt is recorded. No access token is placed in a query string, the link is transported over HTTPS, and this story creates **no** C-024 portal share or public link at all — the count of publicly reachable alert links is **0**.

**Part 5 — one cadence, stated here and cited everywhere.** The threshold evaluation is scheduled **every 15 minutes**. That figure is fixed by arithmetic rather than left to discovery: the delivery target is 1 hour measured from the posting that crossed the rung, and an hourly pass spends the whole hour before a breach landing one minute after a completed run is even evaluated. The chain that reaches the target with headroom is **15 minutes** of waiting for the next evaluation, plus a run completing in under **10 minutes** across 1,000 active budgets carrying four rungs each, plus **20 minutes** to dispatch after the event is recorded — a **45-minute** worst case, leaving 15 minutes inside the target, and measured from a breach posted **immediately after** a completed run rather than immediately before one. A second run is refused by an overlap guard while one is in progress rather than queued twice. What remains a discovery outcome is the **batch shape**, the **lock granularity** and the **transport** — not the cadence, and not whether the chain reaches the hour. The present `ir.cron` record in `data/budget_alert_cron.xml` declares `interval_number` of 1 and `interval_type` of hours, so this is required work rather than a configuration preference, and every cadence statement elsewhere in this feature and in [FEATURE-001-09 §4.3 and §4.4](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md#43-integration-requirements) cites this part rather than restating a number of its own.

## Edge Cases

| # | Edge Case | Expected Handling |
|---|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Zero and null values — a zero budget on a line that consumed, and a budget line with no threshold configured.** The Depreciation Expense 6500 line of the "FY2025 Operating Budget" of company `US-01` carries a Q1 2025 budget of `$0.00 USD` against a posted actual of `$18,000.00 USD`, being the `$9,000.00 USD` February 2025 and `$9,000.00 USD` March 2025 charges of `FA/00002`, the twelve-van delivery fleet in service from 2025-02-01 in [Epic Appendix F.5](../../EPIC-001-enterprise-accounting-odoo.md#f5-canonical-us-01-fixed-asset-roll-forward--2025), each entry debiting Depreciation Expense 6500 and crediting Accumulated Depreciation 1590 by the entry amount in USD so total debits equal total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, and the pair being the analytic subset of that account's `$144,000.00 USD` whole-account Q1 charge rather than the whole of it; and separately, a budget line carries an empty threshold list | **The variance is an amount; the percentage is absent; and no threshold can be derived.** The line reports an absolute variance of `$18,000.00 USD` **unfavourable**, stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, because 6500 is an `expense_depreciation` account standing above a `$0.00 USD` budget. The variance percentage and the percentage consumed both read **`N/A`** rather than 0.00%, an infinite value or a division-by-zero error, and no exception is raised — the guard is the **absence** of a percentage, because 0.00% would read as "nothing consumed" on a line that consumed `$18,000.00 USD`. The line stays inside the favourable and unfavourable totals, contributing `$18,000.00 USD` to the unfavourable total. **No threshold alert fires**, because a percentage breach amount cannot be derived from a `$0.00 USD` budget: 75% of `$0.00 USD` is `$0.00 USD`, which every actual above zero would cross on its first posting, so the rung is reported as not derivable rather than treated as breached, and the count of alert events raised against a zero-budget line is **0**. A budget line whose threshold list is **empty or null** is not alerted on at all and is reported as unmonitored in the dashboard rather than as healthy, so an absent configuration cannot be mistaken for a green band. A **negative** budget amount is not a state this feature admits: the planned amount of a budget line is non-negative — the model refuses a negative value with a message naming the amount and the account, and [STORY-001-09-01](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md) plans a reduction as a **smaller positive** amount rather than as a negative one — so direction comes from the account type rather than from a negative plan. A budget line of `$0.00 USD` is the lower bound, and it is the case handled above. No journal entry arises in any branch |
| 2 | **A fiscal period locked before the variance is quoted, and a late crossing inside it.** The journal-entry lock date of company `US-01` stands at 2025-03-31, closing every period on or before that date, and the Q1 2025 variance of `$12,450.00 USD` unfavourable at 4.15% on Expense 6100 has been quoted in the March management pack | **A locked period's variance is immutable, and no retrospective alert fires for it.** The lock governs *posting*, not *reading*: the **Budget Variance Analysis** report still returns the Q1 2025 figures of `$300,000.00 USD` budget, `$312,450.00 USD` actual, `$12,450.00 USD` unfavourable and 4.15% exactly as it did before the lock, and because no entry can be posted, altered or reversed into a range on or before 2025-03-31 while the lock stands, **two runs of the same range return identical figures** — which is what makes a locked period's variance quotable as close evidence (SM-014, C-009). Threshold evaluation **does not raise a new alert for a closed period**: a rung whose crossing lies inside a locked range cannot newly occur, so the evaluation reports the closed period as settled rather than re-firing against it, and the count of alert events created against a locked period is **0**. Alert events that fired **while** the period was open are retained unchanged with their original timestamps, because the lock closes the books and does not erase the control that operated on them. The evaluation neither tests nor alters the lock date, and it creates no entry that a lock would have to refuse. Where a lock exception is released under CAP-005 of [FEATURE-001-01](../FEATURE-001-01-chart-of-accounts-fiscal-year.md) and a late entry is then posted into the closed range, the actual, the variance and the consumption move on the next evaluation of that range, a rung crossed by that late entry fires as a fresh crossing with its own timestamp, the budget amount stays at `$300,000.00 USD`, and the previously quoted figure is superseded rather than silently overwritten |
| 3 | **EUR-denominated actuals evaluated against a USD budget, where the candidate breach sits inside the translation rounding difference.** Company `US-01` holds the annual Expense 6100 budget of `$1,200,000.00 USD` with the 75% breach amount at `$900,000.00 USD`. Its posted actuals comprise `$570,012.98 USD` charged in USD plus three EUR-denominated vendor bills of `€101,378.50 EUR` each, totalling `€304,135.50 EUR`, each recorded in the company currency at `$109,995.67 USD` at the period-average rate of **1.0850 USD per EUR** | **The rung is evaluated on the company-currency amounts already recorded on the posted journal items, and a breach that exists only inside the rounding difference is disclosed rather than fired.** Each currency rounds at **its own increment** — EUR to 2 decimal places at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01 and USD to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 — and a zero-decimal currency rounds at its own increment rather than at an assumed 0.01. On the per-item basis the three bills contribute `$109,995.67 USD` each, a sum of `$329,987.01 USD`, so the actual reads `$899,999.99 USD` — **one cent below** the `$900,000.00 USD` breach amount, and **no alert fires**, the consumption reading 75.00% only after rounding while the amount comparison governs. Translating the `€304,135.50 EUR` aggregate **once** instead gives `$329,987.02 USD` and an actual of `$900,000.00 USD`, which would appear to breach. That `$0.01 USD` difference is the reconciling treatment: the **evaluation basis is named in the threshold configuration** — the company-currency amounts on the posted journal items, which are what the General Ledger holds — so two evaluations of one population cannot disagree; the difference is **disclosed as a translation difference** beside the group figure and is never pushed back onto the EUR amounts, because those amounts are what the EUR-denominated documents recorded; and **a rung is never fired on a rounding artifact**, so a candidate breach falling inside a disclosed translation difference produces no alert event and is reported as a translation difference instead. Where the budget itself is held in EUR by company `NL-01`, Global Europe SARL — an annual `€1,200,000.00 EUR` with a 75% breach amount of `€900,000.00 EUR` — the rung is evaluated in EUR against `NL-01`'s own books, its notification states EUR amounts at the EUR rounding increment of 0.01, and any USD group presentation carries the rate and the basis of that rate beside the translated figure (IAS 21) |
| 4 | **A threshold crossed and alerted on, then reversed back below the rung.** Posted actuals on Expense 6100 in company `US-01` reach `$1,210,000.00 USD` against the annual `$1,200,000.00 USD`, the Critical alert for the 100% rung fires, and a posted reversing entry of `$40,000.00 USD` — debits equal to credits, as in the original entry — then brings the actual to `$1,170,000.00 USD` | **The figure moves, the event does not.** The variance recomputes to `$30,000.00 USD` **favourable** at 2.50% and the consumption to 97.50%, each stated to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01, and the dashboard band moves from red to orange. The **fired Critical event is retained** with its original timestamp and its original actual at trigger of `$1,210,000.00 USD`: an alert that fired is a fact of the period, and deleting it would remove the evidence that the control operated, which is exactly what the External Auditor tests. The rung is **re-armed rather than re-fired** — a further Critical notification is delivered only after a fresh crossing of `$1,200,000.00 USD` — and the count of notifications delivered as a consequence of the reversal is **0**, because a reversal is not a breach. Both the original entry and its reversal stay posted, neither cancelled, each balancing with total debits equal to total credits at a difference of `$0.00 USD`, the pair **netting to `$0.00 USD`** within the reported range, and both journal items remain individually visible when the recomputed actual is traced to the items behind it. Where the reversal is dated **outside** the evaluated range, the range decides: the range that holds only the charge keeps the higher actual and its fired event, while the range that holds both nets them to `$0.00 USD` — the report is right for the range it was asked for, and it does not net across its own boundary |
| 5 | **A mid-year budget revision that moves the breach amounts under rungs that have already fired.** The Expense 6100 annual budget of company `US-01` is revised upward from `$1,200,000.00 USD` to `$1,500,000.00 USD` in October 2025 through the recorded reset, edit and re-approval of BGT-SOD-001 Part 3, which supersedes the prior plan version rather than editing it in place, while the 75% Warning has already fired at the old breach amount of `$900,000.00 USD` against a then-actual of `$905,000.00 USD` | **The rungs are re-derived, the fired events are retained against the amount they fired at, and the re-armed rungs are the new ones.** The four breach amounts are recomputed from the revised budget and stored to 2 decimal places at the USD rounding increment of 0.01 — 75% at `$1,125,000.00 USD`, 90% at `$1,350,000.00 USD`, 100% at `$1,500,000.00 USD` and 110% at `$1,650,000.00 USD` — and the consumption of an actual of `$905,000.00 USD` recomputes from 75.42% to **60.33%**, moving the dashboard band from amber to green. The **Warning event that already fired is retained** with its original timestamp, its original threshold of 75%, its original breach amount of `$900,000.00 USD`, its original consumption of 75.42% and its original actual at trigger of `$905,000.00 USD`, so the history records what was true when it fired rather than being restated against a budget that did not then exist. The 75% rung is **re-armed against the new `$1,125,000.00 USD`** and fires again on a fresh crossing of that amount, and the count of notifications delivered by the revision itself is **0** — a plan change is not a breach. The revision is recorded with its author, its timestamp, its prior amount of `$1,200,000.00 USD` and its revised amount of `$1,500,000.00 USD` for the External Auditor, and every variance and consumption figure quoted after it names which **plan version** it was computed against — the version identifier [BGT-SOD-001](./STORY-001-09-01-define-budgets.md#bgt-sod-001--the-budget-approval-authority-allocation-completeness-and-immutability-contract) Part 3 stamps on each approval — so a figure quoted in September and a figure quoted in November are not silently compared across two different plans; and because the plan version forms part of the crossing identity, the re-armed rungs of the revised plan are **new** identities rather than duplicates of the rungs that fired against the old one (ALT-001 Parts 1 and 2). The revision writes to the plan and **not** to the ledger: no journal entry is created, altered or reversed, and the posted actual of `$905,000.00 USD` is unmoved by it |

- [ ] **C-019, C-020, C-022 — Input validation and hostile-input tests.** Every value this story accepts at run time — threshold percentage, severity selection, recipient identifier, start date, end date, company, account, analytic plan, analytic account, granularity, grouping selection and note text — is validated before use; data access is expressed through the ORM or parameterized SQL with no concatenated search domain; a rejected value returns an error naming the parameter and the check that failed and discloses no stack trace, query text, file-system path or credential; and at least one acceptance test submits a non-numeric threshold, a negative threshold, an out-of-order ladder, a malformed date and an over-long note and asserts rejection with no alert event created, no journal entry created and the service still available.
- [ ] **C-017 — Export neutralization, applied by cell type.** In the PDF and XLSX exports of the variance report, the trend view and the alert history, an **untrusted text** cell whose first character is `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return — including a budget name, an analytic account name or note text that legitimately begins with one — is written as text, so an exported variance or alert history cannot execute in the recipient's spreadsheet.

- [ ] **C-021 — Credentials outside module source.** This story holds no endpoint credential, API key or signing certificate. Where alert delivery uses an outbound mail server, its host, account and password are held outside module source and outside version control, and no credential appears in a notification body, an alert event, a log line or an exported file.

### Interface, Artifact, Evidence and Resilience Contracts

- [ ] **C-023 — Machine access is a named principal, not a shared key.** Every programmatic variance run, note write, threshold read, evaluation trigger or dashboard read happens over the JSON web-service surface C-023 governs, under the dedicated named principal **`budget-alert-read`** with a recorded owner, a declared expiry and rotation interval, a revocation effective on its next call, a scope confined to its company and to the models and methods it needs, execution under access rights and record rules rather than `sudo`, a per-principal rate ceiling and a per-call audit line. The principal holds no authority its human counterpart lacks: it holds **no** write right on `account.move` or `account.move.line`, it does not hold `group_budget_approve`, so the count of threshold ladders and recipient lists it can change is **0**, and it cannot delete or rewrite a fired alert event or override the crossing identity of [ALT-001](#alt-001--the-alert-crossing-identity-snapshot-dispatch-recipient-authority-and-cadence-contract) Part 1. The **scheduled** evaluation runs under its own named service principal holding the budget-read right and the alert-write right in the companies it evaluates and nothing else. The deprecated XML-RPC and JSON-RPC transports carry no criterion, test or integration of this story.
- [ ] **C-024 — Portal and shared-link access does not arise here, and the boundary is stated.** A notification reaches an **internal** finance recipient, and the link it carries resolves through an authenticated Odoo session with the recipient's company and budget-read right re-checked at open time (ALT-001 Part 4). No alert, variance figure or dashboard line is published to a portal, a shared link or an unauthenticated URL, so the count of publicly reachable alert links is **0**. - [ ] **C-025 — Every produced file is an artifact with a lifecycle.** An exported variance report, an exported trend view and an exported alert history are each stored with the declared media type recorded separately from the file name, served with a non-executing content disposition and a system-fixed filename, addressed by an unguessable identifier, and served only after an authorization check on the **budget and company** the export concerns. Creation, download and deletion are recorded with the principal and the timestamp; the export declares the retention period the group's records-retention policy sets for alert and variance evidence together with its legal-hold treatment; purge happens only through the governed retention run; and publication is atomic, so a cancelled or interrupted export leaves no partial alert history downloadable.
- [ ] **C-026 — The alert history and the note history are evidence, so they are append-only.** A fired alert event, a recorded suppression, a recorded re-arming, a dropped recipient, a delivery outcome, a threshold change, a refused threshold change and every explanation note with its edits and its deletion are each **appended** with the acting principal, the timestamp and the outcome, and each stores the **values as at the event** — the recipient address used, the amounts and their currency, the consumption percentage, the breach amount, the plan version, the watermark and the template version — rather than a reference that would re-derive them from a plan or a ledger that has since moved. The retention lock does **not** cascade — deleting, archiving or anonymizing the budget, the journal item or the recipient a row references neither removes the row nor rewrites its snapshot, and purging the history under C-025 neither removes nor alters the budget — and the External Auditor exports the whole history, including failed deliveries and their reasons, for a company and a fiscal year in **one** operation, subject to C-017.

- [ ] **C-028 — A crossing is decided once.** The episode identity over company, budget line, rung, plan version and arming cycle is held by a **database unique constraint**, with the budget-line row taken under a row lock across the read-decide-write window and the ledger, plan-version and configuration-version watermark revalidated inside that lock immediately before the commit, so two simultaneous evaluations produce **1** event, a re-arming is a new identity rather than a duplicate, a stale lock is reclaimed after its declared interval with the abandoned attempt recorded, and no part of the guard is overridable by the Group Controller or by the C-023 principal it constrains (ALT-001 Parts 1 and 2).

### Performance and Availability

- [ ] **Threshold evaluation cadence, on the one authority that states it.** The evaluation is scheduled **every 15 minutes**, not hourly, with an overlap guard that refuses to start a second run while one is in progress, exactly as [ALT-001](#alt-001--the-alert-crossing-identity-snapshot-dispatch-recipient-authority-and-cadence-contract) Part 5 fixes it — this bullet restates that authority rather than setting a second one. The cadence is what makes the 1-hour delivery target reachable: an hourly pass leaves a breach posted one minute after a completed run waiting a full hour before it is even evaluated, so the hour is spent before a notification is queued. The present `ir.cron` record in `data/budget_alert_cron.xml` is configured with `interval_number` of 1 and `interval_type` of hours, so this is a required change rather than a preference.
- [ ] **Threshold evaluation run.** A scheduled evaluation completes in **under 10 minutes** across **1,000 active budgets** carrying four rungs each — inside its own 15-minute cadence window, so a run finishes before the next is due — which is the envelope recorded in [FEATURE-001-09 §4.4](../FEATURE-001-09-budgeting-variance-analysis.md).
- [ ] **Notification delivery latency, as a chain with headroom.** A notification is delivered to its configured recipients **within 1 hour** of the posting that crossed the rung, measured from the posting to the recorded delivery timestamp. The worst case is stated rather than assumed: **15 minutes** waiting for the next evaluation, plus **10 minutes** of evaluation, plus **20 minutes** to dispatch the notification after the alert event is recorded, is **45 minutes** — leaving 15 minutes inside the target. The target holds when **100 alerts fire concurrently**, all 100 being delivered inside the hour rather than the first few inside it and the remainder queued behind them, and the latency test posts its breach **immediately after** a completed run so it measures the worst case rather than the best.
- [ ] **Variance computation per budget line.** Under **100 ms** per budget line, reported as a per-line figure over a seeded set.
- [ ] **Variance report generation over volume.** The **Budget Variance Analysis** report renders in **under 30 seconds** over a company holding **100,000 posted journal items** for a quarter-length date range, holding the envelope of the comparison it consumes.
- [ ] **Consumption-band dashboard render.** Under **5 seconds** for the band summary across **1,000 active budgets**.
- [ ] **Variance availability after close (SM-014).** Signed variance figures are available **within 24 hours** of the period lock date being applied for the closed period, and **on demand** for the open period. This is the Epic success metric SM-014 — the same 24-hour target the superseded backlog carried as its SM-003, renumbered by the destination Epic, whose own SM-003 is the month-end close cycle time.

### Accounting Standards Compliance

- [ ] **A variance and an alert are management information, not statutory amounts.** They are presented beside a statutory statement and excluded from the Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss that FEATURE-001-07 publishes, consistent with IAS 1 presentation of financial statements, under which supplementary information does not alter a statutory caption or its amount. This is why no path in this story posts to the ledger.
- [ ] **The actual behind every variance is the sub-ledger, aggregated and not restated.** Every amount a variance is computed from originates in a posted `account.move.line` whose parent entry balances, so the analysis inherits the ledger's own double-entry integrity rather than asserting a separate one, and a variance line ties to the journal items behind its actual at a difference of `$0.00 USD`.
- [ ] **Signing follows management-accounting convention rather than the sign of the number.** Favourable and unfavourable are derived from `account.account.account_type`, following Institute of Management Accountants practice, so an income shortfall and an expense underspend are not both reported as negative outcomes. Absolute variance is stated as an amount in the budget currency at that currency's decimal places, and percentage variance and percentage consumed to 2 decimal places.
- [ ] **A fired alert is monitoring-activity evidence and is retained as such.** The alert history — a dated event per crossing with its threshold, severity, consumption, amounts, company and recipient list, undeletable and with suppressions recorded rather than silent — is the evidence of operating budget control tested under the COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework and section 404 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act. Reproducibility supports the same test: because the analysis is a pure function of the budget, the posted ledger and the named parameters, two runs of the same range return the same figures and a locked period's variance cannot move while the lock stands.
- [ ] **Currency translation follows IAS 21 where an evaluation crosses currencies.** A translated figure carries its rate and the basis of that rate, each amount rounds at its own currency's increment, a translation difference is disclosed rather than pushed back onto the functional-currency figures, and a rung is never fired on a difference that sits inside that disclosure.
- [ ] **Retention.** Alert events and variance explanation notes are retained for the period the group's records-retention policy sets against the applicable tax and audit retention requirements, and neither is deleted to reflect a later reversal or a later budget revision.

## Estimation

| Dimension | Rating | Basis |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Effort** | High | **Two superseded stories in one.** The variance half delivers the account-type signing rule over six type values, favourable, unfavourable and net totals, a trend axis at three granularities with four year-to-date columns and a trend direction, and a note record with authorship, timestamp, edit history and a presence filter. The alerting half delivers a four-rung configurable ladder with derived breach amounts on two bases, a recipient configuration per severity, a scheduled evaluation, a notification payload of six named elements, an immutable event of ten named attributes including its delivery status and delivery timestamp, a suppression rule, a re-arming rule, and a four-band dashboard with its line content, its ordering rule and its drill-down. On top of that sit eight acceptance tests plus the arithmetic, isolation, hostile-input, delivery-latency, concurrency and 1,000-budget evaluation tests. Volume is real but bounded: nothing here creates a ledger-side model, because every actual is read from records that already exist and the aggregation is consumed from [STORY-001-09-03](./STORY-001-09-03-report-budget-vs-actual.md) rather than rebuilt |
| **Complexity** | High | The difficulty is **state and time, not arithmetic**. The variance sums are simple; the alerting is a small state machine that has to be right at every edge: a rung that has fired must not fire again, a rung whose consumption falls back must **re-arm without deleting the event that fired**, a suppression must be **recorded rather than silent**, a reversal must move the figure while leaving the history alone, a budget revision must move the breach amounts while leaving the fired events at the amount they fired against, and a cross-currency evaluation must refuse to fire on a `$0.01 USD` rounding artifact. Beside that sits a **latency** requirement rather than a throughput one — 1 hour from a posting to a delivered message, including when 100 alerts fire at once — which makes the evaluation cadence, the batch shape and the mail-queue behaviour design decisions rather than details. The signing rule is the one piece of easy-looking logic that is easy to get wrong, because the direction is not the sign of the number |
| **Uncertainty** | Medium | Lower than it looks on the data side and higher on the delivery side. Known: the `account_type` selection and its income and expense sets, the posted-state field, the balance and date fields, the analytic distribution and its index, the currency precision fields, and an existing `budget.alert` model with a four-rung selection, a severity mapping, recipient fields, immutability overrides and an hourly `ir.cron` were all read in this repository during discovery, and fourteen of the existing implementation's gaps are identified rather than suspected. Settled: the hourly cadence does **not** hold the 1-hour target for a breach that lands just after a run rather than just before one, so the cadence moves to 15 minutes and the chain is stated with 15 minutes of headroom. Open: how the outgoing-mail queue behaves under 100 concurrent notifications, whether a 10-minute run holds across 1,000 active budgets at four rungs each, and whether DEC-001 confirms a platform version other than 19.0, which would restate both the scheduled-action definition and the notification call |
| **Story Points** | **8** (Fibonacci: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) | Unchanged, and now bounded: [§ Scope Boundary](#scope-boundary) publishes the thirteen exclusions that keep two work packages inside one `8`, including the two contracts moved out of this file this revision — the signed-variance arithmetic to § VAR-001 of STORY-001-09-03 and the consumption bands to CONS-001 in the parent — which is what absorbs the 15-minute evaluation cadence and the criterion-level gap matrix without the file becoming a `13` |

**Why 8 and not 5 or 13.** The 8 exists chiefly because this is **two prior-art stories compressed into one file** — the variance analysis and the budget alerting — and the estimate has to carry both halves or the merger becomes a reduction. Five points would fit the variance half alone: signing, totals, trend, year-to-date and notes over an aggregation that already exists. It would not carry the ladder, the scheduled evaluation, the notification payload, the immutable event, the suppression and re-arming rules, the dashboard bands, or the **delivery-latency requirement** of 1 hour from breach to recorded delivery including under 100 concurrent alerts — which is the single hardest thing in the story, because latency is measured end to end through a scheduler and a mail queue rather than inside a function. Thirteen would overstate work bounded by [§ Scope Boundary](#scope-boundary), whose ledger side is entirely read-only, whose fields, indexes, alert model, immutability overrides and scheduled action already exist in this repository and are credited under D-003, and whose eight criteria are each demonstrable in one walkthrough. The estimate assumes the ladder, the severities and the escalation recipients per company are signed off by the Group Controller during the sprint, and it **excludes** budget definition, period allocation and the comparison itself with its grouping, drill-down and exports, all of which belong to the three preceding stories.
